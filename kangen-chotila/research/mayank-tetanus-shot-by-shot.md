## The techniques, named
1. **Show the word.** Every noun he says is on screen at that moment as one clean object: nail, finger, hand,
   muscle, fist, soil, matchstick, boiling pot, scale, switchboard, album, vial.
2. **The picture acts out the sentence.** The rust falls off on "नहीं होता"; the arm turns to muscle on "मसल्स"; the
   hand becomes a fist on "जकड़"; the pills bounce off on "कोई दवाई नहीं".
3. **Long, calm shots that keep moving.** Each scene lasts 3-8 s with a slow camera push; the change comes from
   the headline and overlays inside the shot, not from fast cutting. That is why it feels calm yet never still.
4. **One colour world per section:** warm street for the myth, plain yellow to explain, dark red inside the body,
   blue for pain and hospital.
5. **Metaphors become physical objects**: matchstick, armour, switchboard, photo album, scale.
6. **Proof pinned on screen**: a CDC note sits next to the scale and the album.
7. **Kicker + punch headline**, two or three words, swapped on the key word every 1.5-3 s.
8. **No dead air**: 3.2 words a second, no pause longer than 0.8 s, music low under the voice, a whoosh on
   scene changes, a pop on headlines, a deep hit on reveals.
9. **Story order = Problem, Agitate, Solution.** The myth, then the enemy, then every level of danger, then the
   hopeless moment ("no medicine can stop it", "1 in 10 even with treatment"), and only then the solution
   (the injection), explained as a mechanism, not sold.
10. **Bookend callback**: the last scene is the first scene with the new meaning, then one question to the viewer.

## Why version 1 of our Hindi reel (3:04) missed
Loose stock clips cut every 2-3 s, so the picture rarely matched the exact word. It used three minutes for one
idea, and the India-elder vs Japan-elder comparison fought the local belief that village elders are the strongest.
