## The idea
Every Gujarati family has this scene: stone pain at night, an elder says "drink 3-4 lotas of water", and the stone
comes out. **Did the water dissolve it?** No. Small stones (under 5 mm) mostly pass on their own; the credit goes to
water, but the size saved him. This is Mayank's snake-bite (5.7M) structure, beat for beat, on a true belief. Then
water becomes the real hero: not dissolving stones but **stopping them from forming**. That is where Enagic
Kangen sits, honestly, all through the video.

## Facts and sources
- Kidney stones in about 12% of Indians; Gujarat in the stone belt: Foods (MDPI) 2019 review.
- Most stones are calcium oxalate: NIDDK.
- Stones under 5 mm pass on their own in about 68-87% (meta-analysis 68%; larger series 75-87%); over 9 mm about 25%.
- Calcium stones do not dissolve in water; large stones need a doctor: urology guidance (AUA).
- Renal colic comes in waves and is among the most intense pain: clinical consensus.
- About half have another stone within about 10 years: Duke/NIDDK-funded research.
- Myth "stop milk if you have stones": Apollo Hospitals "9 myths associated with kidney stones".
- Highest dietary calcium = 35% lower stone risk in women (calcium supplements = higher risk): Curhan et al.,
  Nurses' Health Study, Ann Intern Med 1997.
- Dietary calcium binds oxalate in the gut: Curhan et al. (the proposed mechanism).
- Reduce salt; enough water is the most important prevention: NIH/NIDDK "Eating, Diet & Nutrition for Kidney Stones".
- No disease or cure claims for Kangen: it is named for clean, filtered, mineral-keeping water at home.
