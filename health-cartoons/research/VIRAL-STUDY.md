# Viral study: what makes the reference channel's Shorts hit 10M-27M views (20 Nov 2026)

Data: all **406 Shorts** of the reference channel (YouTube @Dr_Healthcare1, 1.07M subscribers), view counts from yt-dlp;
the **top 10 downloaded** (12M-26M views) and transcribed word by word (faster-whisper); 5 more given by the user earlier (3M and below).
The #1 video (27M, "why eating without water can make you constipated?") could not be downloaded: YouTube's bot check blocked it.

## 1. The view distribution: a hits business
- Median 0.72M, mean 1.85M. **17 videos ≥ 10M, 34 ≥ 5M, 248 below 1M.** About 4% of uploads earn most of the views.
- The bottom of the list is rare surgeries and procedures (severed fingers, broken needle, braces behind teeth: 20K-120K).
  **Unusual procedures do not travel. Everyday bodies do.**

## 2. Topic families that hit 10M+ (ranked)
| Family | Examples (views) | Why it spreads |
|---|---|---|
| **Eating / swallowing habits that go wrong** | no water → constipation (27M), sleeping right after eating (26M), food up your nose (22.9M), pill without water (9.3M), soda (9.3M) | Everyone did it today. Mild fear ("am I hurting myself?") plus toilet humour. Easy to share ("you do this!"). |
| **"How X actually works"** (medicine) | painkillers (21.3M), antibiotic resistance (17.6M), sleeping pills (10M), copper IUD (13.2M) | A curiosity gap about something you swallow or use. "Actually" promises a hidden truth. |
| **Babies and pregnancy drama** | conjoined twins (22.1M), vanishing twin (12.3M), babies + water (13.3M), breastfeeding bite (12M) | Strong emotion: parents comment (1,200-1,900 comments, the highest). |
| **Everyday body moments** | snoring (11M), shaving (11M), toenails (13.2M), smoking lung (15.5M) | Universal and slightly gross. |

**Rule:** pick a topic that (a) almost every viewer has personally done or felt, (b) carries a famous belief or a mild fear, and
(c) can be shown as a physical object travelling inside the body.

## 3. Titles
- Questions: **Why … ? / What Actually … ? / How do … actually … ?** "Why You Shouldn't …" and "Actually" over-index.
- 5-9 words, the everyday action first ("Sleep Right After Eating", "Swallow A Pill Without Water"), no medical jargon in the title.

## 4. The hook (first 0-2 s), measured on the top 10
- **Frame 1 = the organ itself, full screen**, its eyes on the top edge (stomach, uterus, lungs, vessel, throat), often with a **label box**
  (TRACHEA, INTRAVASCULAR SPACE, Uterus).
- **An intruder enters within 0.5 s**: food falling in, a pill dropping, a glass shard cutting, sperm swimming, a nail clipper descending.
- **First words at 0.6-1.4 s**, in four flavours:
  1. a complaint to another organ: *"Yo, why are you always blocking me off?"*
  2. "what is this?": *"Huh? What's this again?"*
  3. pain: *"Ow! My head hurts!"*
  4. an action announcement by the "owner": *"Alright, time to trim those nails." / "Open your mouth, honey."*

## 5. Script mechanics
- **Speech rate 2.8-3.0 words/s**, almost no dead air; lines of 1-4 s.
- **Bro banter:** "bro", "dude", "man", "mate", "yo", sarcasm (*"You are actually hilarious"*), insults between organs, and the organ phoning
  the **Brain** for backup. Organs behave like coworkers stuck with a careless boss (the human).
- **Escalation:** a small annoyance → a fight → a consequence (burp smell, seizures, lung removed, a twin absorbed).
- **Emotional beat** in the best performers (twin: *"But I don't want it. He's my bro."*) gives likes of 2.6-3.5%.
- **Where the clinic starts: 58-83% of the runtime (about 70%).** The cartoon part is long; the doctor is short (10-17 s).
  Some top videos have no doctor at all (sleep-after-eating, food-up-nose): an organ explains the science in one line.
- **Doctor template:** *"Doc, [symptom/question]?"* → *"You're dealing with / That's / He's experiencing something called **[NAME]**"* →
  1-2 sentences of mechanism → what to do or the outcome. Sometimes the patient ends with *"Oh, that makes sense."*
- Length 24-97 s; most are 52-68 s. The 24-s one (food up the nose, 22.9M) shows that a short, loopable fight also works.

## 6. Sound
- **Music under the whole video** (light comedic bed) about 14 dB under the voice; few SFX (plop, boing, whip, ding, burp).
- **Master loudness about −9 LUFS on YouTube** (all top 10 measured between −8.7 and −10.2). Our 001 was −13.6, about 4.5 dB quieter. → Master at −9.5.

## 7. Retention measurements (top 10)
- Frozen frames 23-37% (one outlier at 75%); the longest static stretch is usually 1.0-1.7 s.
- Engagement: likes 0.9-3.5% of views; the most comments come from baby and pregnancy topics.

## 8. What we copy and what we never copy
- **Copy (format, proven patterns):** topic families, title shapes, organ-as-hero first frame, the intruder in 0.5 s, the hook flavours, bro banter,
  escalation and a twist, the clinic at about 70%, the doctor template with a named condition, music plus SFX, loudness.
- **Never copy:** their scripts (word for word), characters, mascot (white-haired doctor with a green mask), "Hi, I'm Doc!" banner, watermark or channel name.
  Exact copies risk YouTube's reused-content and impersonation policies, which a new channel cannot survive.

## 10. Cross-channel check with the yt-viral method (20 Nov 2026, second pass)

- **A second channel in exactly this format exists: "Health Manager"** (`UCq5kGeiEq4eC9sdEUyjxvRg`), 367 Shorts, median 477K, 19 Shorts at 10M+.
  The reference (`UC3wmheybwSk_GMMapnPGl0Q`, @Dr_Healthcare1): 400 Shorts, median 720K, 17 at 10M+.
- **Only one topic hit 20M+ on both channels:** "Why Does Food Sometimes Go Up Your Nose?" (22M = 30.6x on the reference, 20M = 41.9x on Health Manager).
  Snoring is the next one that repeated (11M and 3.4M).
- **Topics that did NOT repeat:** "Sleep right after eating" (26M vs 0.2M) and "Conjoined twins" (22M vs 0.2M). A single 20M+ hit is not proof that a topic works.
- Health Manager's own top: empty stomach before surgery 32M (67x), manual placenta removal 30M, grapes and bathroom urges 25M,
  ice water after hot food 21M, a diet trick 20M, food up the nose 20M, skipping breakfast and a stomach ache 12M, still hungry when full 11M.
- Topic family across both channels (share of Shorts at 10M+): sleep/breath 10%, eating/drinking habit 9.2% (12 of the 36 hits),
  medicines 12% (small sample), baby/pregnancy 9%, surgery/devices 2.7%, everything else 1.5%.
- Titles: 30 of the 36 Shorts at 10M+ are a Why/How/What question (yt-viral "The Question" formula; a label for the title, not a proof of why it worked).
- Similar but different formats (CasiCreativo "Your organs when…", Organ Drama, ORGAN INC., Human Buddy) are much smaller or long-form; not useful as proof.

## 11. Why the 002 preview failed (frame-by-frame against 3 references, 0-21 s)
1. **Hook:** 002 opened on a flat human face blowing a bubble. All three compared references open INSIDE the body with the talking characters
   already on screen, and an intruder enters by 0.6 s (a food crumb, a needle).
2. **Character size:** the gum was about 5% of the frame and the stomach's eyes sat at the top edge. Their speaking characters fill 30-60% of the width.
3. **Camera:** 002 changed framing on every line, which read as "glitchy". The references hold ONE locked wide shot for 10-20 s;
   the motion comes from acting (eyes, mouths, arms, props), not from the camera.
4. **Acting:** their organs have arms and hands and hold props (the stomach holds a fork and knife). 002's characters had no arms.
5. **Readability:** 002's stomach was dark maroon with curtain-like folds and flat yellow acid; it did not read as a stomach.
   Theirs: clean pink flesh, yellow bone, recognisable food (fried egg, burger, chicken), and small white labels on the anatomy.
6. **Script register:** their lines are plain everyday bro talk, 3-7 words each ("Yo, why are you always blocking me off?", "One bite won't hurt.",
   "See? You're choking!"). The story is "the organ wants something → the other says no (and why) → it does it anyway → the consequence → 'See?'".
   002's puns ("seven-year lease") were writer jokes, not their voice.
