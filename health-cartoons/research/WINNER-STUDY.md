## 2. The strongest proven video: "why eating without water can make you constipated?"
It leads on every measure we have: most views (27.8M), the 2nd-biggest multiple (x21.6), still 74.6K views a day averaged over a year,
and **the most likes (993K, 3.6%) and the most comments (3,600) of the 13 measured top videos**.

**What it is (from our shot-by-shot study of the user's mp4, `videos/005-constipation-no-water/script.md`):** a stomach ("Mom") stirs a
dry pile of food; her stick gets stuck; she phones the boy's brain, who says soda is basically water; a 6.3 s silent soda gag;
the stick snaps; a 4.7 s silent cut to a huge rock-hard poop that won't move; the boy strains on the toilet; then the clinic at 57%
(42.9 s), where the doctor explains for 28 s. 75.5 s in all.

**Why it worked (evidence, not assumption):**
- **A habit almost everyone has:** a big meal with soda and no water. The viewer recognises himself in the first seconds.
- **A consequence that is mildly scary and funny:** can't poop, rock-hard stool. Toilet humour travels; the poop family is the channel's
  most repeated winner (10 of 31 uploads at 3M+).
- **Family drama in the comments:** mom nagging the kid about water. It drew the channel's highest comment count (3,600).
- **The title names the habit and the result as a question:** "why eating without water can make you constipated?" (lower case, 8 words, no jargon).
- **The cover is the funniest frame:** the giant rock poop with googly eyes blocking the way (the public Shorts cover).
- **Evergreen:** no news, no season. A year later it still averages 74.6K views a day.

The underlying demand is **"what happens inside me when I skip water"**: an everyday shortcut, a hidden consequence, and a fix you can do today.

## 3. The recommendation: "Why You Should Never Take a Pill Without Water"
**Why this topic and not another:**
1. **The same demand as the #1 video, proven again:** the creator's two "without water" Shorts are their #1 (27.8M) and
   "Why You Should Never Swallow A Pill Without Water" (**9.4M, x11.0 its neighbours, 79.7K views a day**, the 2nd fastest of all their
   videos older than 90 days).
2. **Recent proof, not just 2025:** it was posted around July 2026, when the channel's median was 0.57M. The demand is alive now.
3. **The strongest family for big hits:** pills / how medicine works has the highest share of 5M+ hits (6 of 25).
4. **Universal and slightly scary:** almost everyone swallows pills, many dry-swallow them or take them right before bed. The fix is simple
   (a full glass of water, stay upright for 30 minutes) and medically real: pill esophagitis.
5. **It fits what works on our own channel:** our best Shorts are "habit -> visible consequence" (peel-off mask 39K, one lung 35K,
   leg bounce 31K). Our "how medicine works" explainers did less well (0.8K-3.1K), so the video is built as a habit story, not a lecture.
6. **New to our channel:** we already made the constipation one (005).

**Not chosen, and why:**
- Conjoined twins (22M, x42.9): the biggest outlier, but a pregnancy/birth topic with "#hot" tags (ad-suitability risk) and low likes (1.7%).
- Never fully done pooping (7.9M, x17.8) / toilet footstool (7.7M): strong, but the creator's re-dos flopped (0.44M in June 2026, 0.16M in
  November 2026), and it sits in the same poop family as our 005.
- Starter earring (2.6M in 29 days): too new to call proven.

**Honest limits:**
- The creator re-did the pill topic on 12 Nov 2026 ("Why Did These Pills Get Stuck in the Esophagus?") and it has 190K in 14 days.
  That is about their current median: their own audience has seen it. Ours has not, but nothing guarantees views.
- We could not watch the 9.4M pill video (bot check); its cover and auto-frames show the food pipe with a "Esophagus" tag, a capsule stuck on
  the wall, and a clinic with a thought bubble (a glass of water and a sore). Our story, characters and words are our own.

**Medical facts used:** a pill swallowed with little or no water, or just before lying down, can stick in the food pipe, dissolve there and
injure the lining (pill-induced esophagitis): pain when swallowing, burning behind the breastbone. Prevention: take pills upright with a full
glass of water and stay upright afterwards (sources say 10-30 minutes; we say 30). Most cases heal within days once the pill is stopped.
Sources: Banner Health, Poison Control (poison.org), Pharmacy Times, PMC3099275.
