**Changed on purpose:**
- Their medical payoff ("hot-cold shock" swelling the epiglottis) is not established. Ours is true and checked:
  - Mayo Clinic lists very hot or very cold food and drinks as common triggers of **esophageal spasms**.
  - The pain is often mistaken for heart pain.
  - Mayo advises letting very hot or cold food and drinks sit for a bit first, and getting squeezing chest pain checked right away.
- Anatomy: the esophagus runs right behind the heart. That is why the "it's not me, it's him!" twist works.
- The words are ours, except the hook line, which keeps their proven shape. A new channel cannot risk a reused-content strike.

## Facts (checked)
- Very hot or very cold food and drinks can trigger esophageal spasms.
- The pain can be intense and mistaken for heart pain (angina).
- Squeezing chest pain can also be a heart attack, so it needs medical care right away.
- Letting very hot or cold food and drinks sit for a bit first helps.
- The esophagus runs directly behind the heart.

Sources: Mayo Clinic, "Esophageal spasms" (symptoms and causes; diagnosis and treatment). The IARC 2016 statement on drinks above 65 °C is background only; it is not in the script.
