# Next video topic: research and recommendation (18 Oct 2026)

Only topic research. No script, no title package, no thumbnail, no production.

## How this was found

- **Public YouTube lists.** On 18 Oct 2026 I pulled the public video lists of **60 niche channels: 3,439 long videos**. These are public listings only, with no login and no YouTube Studio.
- **Multiples.** Every video is scored as a **multiple of its own channel's median views**. So a 10× on a small channel counts the same as a 10× on a big one, and the ranking is about the idea, not channel size.
- **Grouping.** The winners were grouped into topics. A topic only counts when several channels win with it.
- **Google Trends.** I pulled worldwide data for 5 years, for both YouTube search and web search, through `pytrends`.
- **Editorial trend reports.** Three fall/winter 2026 trend reports were read, and they count as opinion, not data.
- **Your own videos.** I read the full transcript of your previous video.

## Step 4: The one recommendation

**Topic: "The French winter wardrobe: the 8 timeless pieces elegant French women wear all winter, and how they wear them, at any age and on any budget."**

**Why this one** (strongest combined evidence, not taste):

1. **Your own audience.**
   - French style is the only topic that has clearly beaten your median: 12.8×, against 0.8-2.0× for everything else.
   - It is the closest thing you have to proof of what your viewers click.
2. **The niche.**
   - French chic has 8 winners at 5×+ from 6 channels in 12 months, and 11 at 3×+ from 7 channels in the last four months.
   - The "pieces she always wears" angle won twice this year: 13.6× and 6.0×.
3. **Timing.**
   - YouTube searches for winter outfits roughly double from October to November and stay high through January.
   - "capsule wardrobe" (+45%) and "french style" (+53%) are both up on last year.
4. **Different from your recent videos.**
   - It's a positive "what she wears and how" video, not a sixth mistakes list.
   - It shares nothing with the Old Money video (fit, tailoring, sheen, logos).
   - It shares nothing with French Never Wear (heels, gym clothes, matching, too much jewellery, fit, shiny fabric, an old style).
   - It doesn't overlap Never Wear in Winter's items either (all-black, pilled knits, novelty sweaters, fleece, sparkle, a clashing coat, scent).
   - It's a standalone video, not a part two.
5. **Channel DNA.** Named pieces and colours, how to wear each one, "you may already own it", any age, any budget, timeless rather than trendy. That is exactly what the transcripts and comments reward.

**The honest risks:**
- **Season-titled videos have been weak.** French-plus-season titles in the niche did 0.5-3.9× (Aliceinparis' fall videos, mostly under 2×). Classy Academy's season-titled videos have a median of 0.5×.
  - So keep the title about the **French woman and her pieces**; "winter" can be the setting, not the hook.
  - Title angle 3 below leaves the season word out entirely, as an evergreen option.
- **Your French win is a single video.** It may have ridden the French wave in suggestions (not checkable without Studio).
- **It is your second French video in about two weeks.** The angle must stay clearly new (pieces and styling, not rules or mistakes).

**Three title angles** (`title.py` scores; each has a number and the subject up front, all under 60 characters):

1. **8 Timeless Pieces French Women Wear All Winter**: 94/100, 46 characters. The "pieces she always wears" shape (13.6×) in this season.
2. **The French Winter Wardrobe: 8 Pieces, Endless Outfits**: 94/100, 53 characters. The capsule promise, matching the rise in capsule searches.
3. **8 Pieces French Women Build Every Winter Outfit Around**: 94/100, 54 characters. The "how she builds an outfit" angle, the most specific promise of the three. If you want it fully evergreen, drop "Winter".

All three lose the end of the title on the mobile feed, so the subject ("French women", "pieces") must stay in the first words.

**The promise in one sentence:** By the end, a woman of any age knows the few winter pieces French women rely on, how they wear each one, and which of them she may already own.
