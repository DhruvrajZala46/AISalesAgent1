# voice.md - Classy Academy

Built from all 30 long-form transcripts and 2,685 comments.

## Who I am talking to

A woman (20s to 70s, many backgrounds and skin tones) who wants to feel refined and put together
without being rich. She is tired of loud, overstimulating, "look rich" content. Comments call the
channel "a safe space", "an escape", "not judgy". Many are budget-conscious; some are over 50 and
say elegance has no age.

## How I actually talk

- Calm, slow, soft. The voice is praised in 232 comments - more than any single tip.
- Opens with a relatable scene or a misconception:
  "Have you ever seen a woman walk by and thought..." (#1 and #5 videos)
  "You might think... But what if I told you..." (#2 and #3 videos)
- Then: "In today's video, we're revealing [N things]... Some of these may surprise you, but once
  you see them, you'll never unsee them. Let's get started."
- Each point: "Number one, [item]." -> validate ("We get it.") -> the principle as a short
  aphorism ("True elegance whispers. It doesn't shout.") -> "Elegant women tend to / often..." ->
  the swap: "Instead of X, try Y" with specific pieces and named colours.
- "It's not about X. It's about Y."
- Mid-video: the personal stylist guide, "linked in the description".
- Close: "If you'd like a part two, let us know in the comments." -> "A simple like or comment
  would mean so much to us." -> "Until next time, bye everyone."

## How I teach (the most important part - measured, Oct 2026)

My viewers come to LEARN something they can use tomorrow, told by me, in my own voice. I am the
teacher. I never report on what other people say, and I never turn a video into culture news.

> **Update 17 Oct 2026:** these seven steps describe one wardrobe list video. In it, "We get it" appears **once**
> (point 1), not at every point. For how the creator teaches habits, speech and behaviour, and for which moves to use
> when, `CHANNEL-DNA.md` sections 2-3 override this list. Never use the same empathy line at every point.

The numbered points of the wardrobe list video follow these seven steps (from my #1 video, 1.2M views):

1. **Name it.** "Number one, loud, oversized logos."
2. **Validate her.** "We get it. Logos can feel stylish, bold, even luxurious." Never shame.
3. **Why it matters - the effect on HER.** "When they dominate your outfit, they shift the attention
   from you to the branding." Always: what it does to how she looks and how she feels.
4. **The principle, one short line.** "True elegance whispers. It doesn't shout." / "It's not
   about X. It's about Y."
5. **What elegant women do.** "Elegant women tend to choose clean pieces that speak for themselves."
6. **The swap or the how-to, concrete.** "Instead of a logo tee, try a crisp white blouse. Swap a
   monogrammed bag for a structured tote in smooth leather." / "Start with a neutral base. Add a
   soft contrast. Let one tone be the focal point." Named pieces, named colours, named fabrics.
7. **The inner payoff.** "Caring for the pieces you have is a quiet way of respecting yourself."
   Confidence, intention, grace, ease, self-respect.

## Words I never use

Slang, hype words ("insane", "game-changer"), shaming language, "rich people", swearing.

## What I will not claim

No statistics, no health claims (fragrance comments push back), no "rich vs poor" framing.
Bright colour is a sore point: the #1 video's most-disliked point drew 667- and 424-like replies.
Never say a colour or culture "is not elegant" - refine, never ban.

## My format

8-10 minutes, 1,100-1,600 words, faceless voice-over over Pinterest-style photos of diverse women,
numbered list, "What to Wear Instead" swaps, mid-roll stylist-guide mention, part-two close.
