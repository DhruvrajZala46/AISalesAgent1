Before you buy anything this winter, stop. Which 2026 trends look elegant on you, and which five almost never do?
This season, fashion magazines are even calling it the end of quiet luxury.
But elegance was never about following every trend. It's about knowing which ones to choose.
In today's video, we're revealing the nine winter 2026 trends that look truly elegant, and the five that almost never do, with exactly how to wear each one.
Some of these may surprise you. Let's get started.
Number one. The winter white coat.
For years, a winter coat meant black, grey or camel. This season, the runways brought back soft white, a warm, creamy shade that feels gentler than pure white.
Elegant women love it because it lights up the face, and it makes even jeans and a simple knit look polished.
Wear it with cream trousers and an ivory turtleneck, or let it soften an all-black outfit underneath.
Number two. Mulberry.
Every winter has its rich red, and this year it's mulberry, a deep shade somewhere between burgundy and plum.
It looks expensive because it's dark enough to feel calm, but warm enough to flatter so many skin tones.
Try a mulberry knit with chocolate brown trousers, or a mulberry coat over navy.
Number three. Regal purple.
If one colour owned the 2026 runways, it was purple. It appeared in almost every show.
We get it. Purple can feel bold. But elegant women don't avoid colour. They refine it.
Choose a deep plum or aubergine instead of a bright violet, and keep it to one piece: a plum knit, a purple leather bag, or a purple blazer with cream trousers.
Number four. The skirt suit.
Designers doubled down on skirt suits this season, in checked wool, soft suede and classic tweed.
Nothing looks quite as put together as a matching jacket and skirt, and you can wear the pieces separately too.
Look for a collarless jacket with a knee-length or midi skirt, and wear it with a fine knit and sleek boots.
Number five. Tuxedo dressing.
Inspired by the classic women's tuxedo first introduced in 1966, the runways brought back satin lapels, crisp dress shirts and tailored black trousers.
It's the perfect answer for the holiday season. Instead of sequins from head to toe, a black tuxedo blazer over a silk camisole looks quietly glamorous.
Add one beautiful pair of earrings, and you're ready for any dinner or party.
And here's a little secret. Most elegant women don't follow every trend. They know exactly which colours and silhouettes flatter them, so they only choose the trends that do.
If you'd like to discover yours, our personal stylist guide is linked in the description.
Number six. The high-neck knit.
This season, the turtleneck grew taller. The drama comes from the height of the neck alone, not from bold patterns or colours.
It frames the face beautifully, and it looks so elegant peeking out from under a coat.
Choose fine merino or cashmere in cream, camel or black, with wide-leg trousers or a midi skirt.
Number seven. Tartan and check.
Plaid and check were one of the biggest stories on the runways this season, from classic tartan to soft checked wool.
A timeless check feels instantly refined, especially in navy, cream or chocolate.
Keep it to one checked piece, like a tartan skirt or a checked blazer, and let everything else stay plain.
Number eight. Rich textures, suede and croc-embossed leather.
Texture is how a simple outfit starts to look expensive, and this winter it's all about soft suede and croc-embossed leather in deep brown, black and burgundy.
A suede tote, a croc-effect belt or a pair of brown suede loafers can lift the simplest knit and trousers.
Number nine. The brooch.
Brooches and flower pins appeared on coats and jackets all over the runways this season.
It's one of the oldest elegant details there is, and you may already own one, perhaps a vintage piece passed down in your family.
Pin it to a coat lapel, a blazer or the shoulder of a knit. One is all you need.
Now, the five trends elegant women tend to quietly skip, or refine.
It's not that they're wrong. It's that, worn the runway way, they rarely look elegant in real life.
Number one. Animal print from head to toe.
Leopard and zebra are back, and on the runways they were sometimes worn all at once.
A whole outfit in print can quickly feel like a costume. Choose one piece instead, a leopard scarf, a belt or a pair of flats, and keep the rest neutral.
Number two. Shrunken, skin-tight tailoring.
After years of oversized shapes, the runways swung back to very tight, cropped and skinny silhouettes.
When every piece is tight, it looks restrictive rather than refined. Elegance is fitted, but never tight. Choose tailoring that skims your shape and leaves room to move.
Number three. The open bag.
One of the biggest bag trends this season is the open tote, with everything inside on display.
Wallets, keys, receipts, all visible. It rarely looks polished. A structured bag that closes always looks more put together, and keeps your things safe in busy winter crowds.
Number four. Dark romance taken too far.
Gothic lace, corsets and dramatic black were everywhere on the runways this year.
Worn all together, it can feel like a costume. One touch of black lace, like a lace-trim camisole under a blazer, gives the same mood with far more grace.
Number five. Maximalist knits.
Knitwear went bold this season, with ribbons, cut-out holes, giant V-necks and busy stripes, sometimes all in one sweater.
When a knit is doing everything at once, it overwhelms the woman wearing it. Choose one detail instead, a soft cable, a single stripe or a beautiful neckline.
So, this winter, the nine trends that look elegant are:
a winter white coat,
mulberry,
regal purple,
the skirt suit,
tuxedo dressing,
the high-neck knit,
tartan and check,
suede and croc texture,
and the brooch.
And the five to quietly refine: animal print from head to toe, skin-tight tailoring, the open bag, dark romance taken too far, and maximalist knits.
Trends will always come and go. Elegance is simply choosing the ones that work for you.
