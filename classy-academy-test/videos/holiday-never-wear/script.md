## Hook

1. Have you ever walked into a holiday party and noticed one woman who looks incredibly elegant?

2. She's not covered in glitter. She's not wearing a costume. And yet every time you look up, she's the one you notice.

3. Here's the secret. During the holidays, elegance is rarely about what you add. It's about what you leave out.

4. In today's video, we're sharing eight things elegant women never wear on Christmas and New Year's Eve, and exactly what they wear instead.

5. And stay until the end, because I'll also give you three simple outfit formulas you can copy this season.

6. Some of these may surprise you, but once you see them, you'll never unsee them. Let's get started.

## 1. Head-to-toe sequins

7. Number one, head-to-toe sequins.

8. Sequins are festive, and there's nothing wrong with a little sparkle. But when your top, your skirt, your bag and your shoes are all shining at once, the eye has nowhere to rest, and the whole outfit starts to feel like a costume.

9. Elegant women follow one simple rule: one shine, one matte.

10. If you love a sequin skirt, pair it with a fine knit, a silk blouse or a plain black sweater. If you want a sequin top, wear it with matte wool trousers or a satin midi skirt.

11. Sparkle looks expensive when it has something quiet to sit next to.

12. And check the quality before you buy. Good sequins are dense, sewn flat and usually lined, so they don't scratch or shed.

13. If they're sparse, stiff and itchy on the inside, they'll look cheap under party lights, no matter the price tag.

## 2. Novelty holiday sweaters and themed prints

14. Number two, novelty holiday sweaters and themed prints.

15. Reindeer, snowflakes, glittery trees, anything with a pom-pom. Unless it's an actual ugly-sweater party, these pieces turn your outfit into a joke before you've even said hello.

16. Instead, carry the festive spirit through colour and texture. Think a cream cable knit, a bottle-green cashmere blend, or a deep burgundy turtleneck.

17. Pair it with a satin midi skirt or tailored trousers, and add pearl earrings. It's still cozy, it's still holiday, but now it looks like you chose it on purpose.

18. And if you want something playful, keep it to one tiny detail, like a velvet bow in your hair or a classic red lip.

## 3. Micro-minis and anything too tight or too revealing

19. Number three, micro-minis and anything too tight or too revealing.

20. Let's be clear, elegance doesn't mean covering up. It means choosing pieces you can actually enjoy wearing.

21. Here's a quick test. If you're tugging your hem down, adjusting your neckline or holding your breath at dinner, you won't look elegant, no matter how beautiful the dress is.

22. Instead, try the rule of one reveal. Choose one area to show, like your shoulders, your back or a subtle slit, and keep everything else soft and relaxed.

23. A long-sleeve velvet midi with a low back. A satin slip dress under a tailored blazer. An off-shoulder knit dress with a long skirt.

24. Confidence that feels comfortable is always more elegant than confidence that needs constant adjusting.

## 4. Thin, shiny fabrics that cling

28. Number four, thin, shiny fabrics that cling in the wrong places.

29. This is one of the biggest reasons a party outfit looks cheaper than it is. Very thin polyester satin shows every line, creases easily and has a flat, plastic shine that catches the light in an unflattering way.

30. Instead, look for fabrics with weight and a softer glow. Velvet, crepe, heavy satin, silk blends, fine wool, even a good jersey.

31. Here's a quick test you can do in the fitting room. Hold the fabric up to the light. If you can see straight through it, or the shine looks harsh and flat, put it back.

32. A fabric that drapes and moves when you walk will always look more expensive than one that clings and creases.

## 5. Accessory overload

33. Number five, accessory overload.

34. A glitter headband, three necklaces, a stack of bangles, light-up earrings and a bag covered in crystals. Each piece is cute on its own, but together they fight for attention.

35. Elegant women choose one hero accessory and let everything else step back. If your earrings are statement crystals, skip the necklace. If you're wearing a beautiful necklace, go for simple studs.

36. A pearl brooch on your coat lapel, a pair of leather gloves, a small satin clutch or a velvet hair bow are all lovely ways to feel festive without looking busy.

37. And before you leave, try the one-thing-off rule. Take off one accessory. Almost every time, the outfit looks better.

## 6. A bulky puffer or oversized teddy coat over a party dress

38. Number six, a bulky puffer or oversized teddy coat over a party dress.

39. We all want to stay warm in December, but a huge coat can swallow your outfit and your proportions before you even arrive.

40. Elegant women choose structure. A tailored wool coat in camel, chocolate brown or charcoal. A belted wrap coat. Or a long cape that drapes beautifully over a dress.

41. Chocolate brown in particular is one of the biggest coat colours this winter, and it looks rich with burgundy, cream and gold.

42. One styling tip: let your coat fall slightly longer than your dress, so the layers look intentional instead of accidental.

## 7. Towering heels you can't walk in

44. Number seven, towering heels you can't walk in.

45. If you're gripping the wall by nine o'clock, the whole look suffers. Elegance is in how you move.

46. Choose heels between one and a half and three inches, like a kitten heel, a slingback or a block heel in satin or suede. A pointed toe makes the foot look longer and sleeker.

47. And if you'll be on your feet all night, satin ballet flats with a delicate strap look gorgeous with a midi dress.

48. Try the walk test at home. Walk across your room and back. If you can do it smoothly, with your shoulders relaxed, you're ready.

## 8. Skipping the finishing touches

49. Number eight, skipping the finishing touches.

50. Holiday outfits come with their own special problems. Velvet that's creased from the closet. Lint all over black. Scuffed heels. A hem that's come down. Snagged tights. These tiny things are exactly what people notice in photos.

51. So give yourself ten minutes before you leave. Steam your velvet, or hang it in the bathroom while you shower. Never press it flat with a hot iron.

52. Run a lint roller over anything black. Wipe your shoes with a soft cloth and a little polish. And keep a spare pair of tights and a mini lint roller in your bag.

53. One more tip. Take a quick photo with flash before you go. Flash shows what the mirror doesn't, like see-through fabric, lint and bra straps, so you can fix it before you walk in.

## Bonus: three outfit formulas

54. Now, as promised, here are three outfit formulas you can copy this season.

55. For Christmas dinner: a bottle-green velvet midi dress, nude kitten heels, pearl earrings and a camel wool coat.

56. For the office party: a satin midi skirt in champagne or chocolate brown, a fine-knit sweater, a tailored blazer and gold hoops.

57. For New Year's Eve: a black satin slip dress or a silk top with tailored trousers, one sparkle piece like a crystal clutch or a sequin jacket, and a classic red lip.

58. Notice the pattern? One star, and everything else stays quiet.
