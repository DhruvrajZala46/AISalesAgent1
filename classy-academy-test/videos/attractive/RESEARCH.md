# 7 Things That Instantly Make You More Attractive: research, fact-check and quality control

**Not narrated.** The copy-ready script is `script.md`. Written 23 Oct 2026. Concept and evidence for the topic: `../../research-2026-10-23-proven-concept-attractive.md`.

## 0. Why 7 points, not 8

The concept report proposed "8 Things…". The brief says: "Rewrite weak points. Do not keep them merely because the script needs a certain number of sections."

After research, only seven points were all three of these:
- strong;
- new to this channel;
- backed by evidence.

Every candidate for an eighth was already taught here (eye contact, posture, smile, scent, nails) or weak on the evidence (wearing red, a natural flush, water for skin). See "Removed after research" below.

So the title is now **"7 Things That Instantly Make You More Attractive"**. The concept and its evidence are unchanged. If you want 8, no eighth point passed the tests, so one would have to be padding.

## 3. Research and fact-check appendix

Labels: **V** = I or the verifying agent read the primary source (PubMed abstract, PMC full text, author manuscript, or the official page). **S** = secondary only. Wiley, ScienceDirect, SAGE and APA pages were blocked (HTTP 403); where that happened the source actually used is named.

Most face studies used young, mostly white raters and faces, and several were industry-funded. Those are flagged per source. No numbers are spoken (the channel rule). The script's research mentions are three: "When researchers let people choose…", "In one study…", and "Researchers found something even more specific…".

### Point 1: resting face

- **Script:** "We form an impression of a face in a fraction of a second."
  - **Willis J, Todorov A (2006)**, *Psychological Science* 17(7):592–598. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16866745/ **V** (abstract)
  - Judgements after 100 ms correlated highly with judgements made without a time limit, for attractiveness, likeability, trustworthiness, competence and aggressiveness.
  - Correlations are not quoted: the full text was blocked.
- **Script:** "Just a hint of one… tends to be read more kindly."
  - **Said CP, Sebe N, Todorov A (2009)**, *Emotion* 9(2):260–264. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19348537/ **V**
  - "Neutral faces that are perceived to have positive valence resemble happiness… faces that are perceived to be threatening resemble anger."
- **Supporting, not spoken:** **Tracy JL, Beall AT (2011)**, *Emotion* 11(6):1379–1387. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21604870/ **V**
  - N = 1,041, ages broad: "happiness was the most attractive female emotion expression."
- **Script:** "Concentration quietly tightens it… can read as stern or distant."
  - This is the channel's own reasoning, not a research claim.
  - Hess et al. 2012 (*J Exp Soc Psychol*, PMC3491992, **V**) found that people read more anger and less warmth into neutral *older* faces. That supports "stern", but the script does not use the age angle.

### Point 2: makeup

- **Script:** "Men and women agreed. They preferred far less than the women had put on for a night out. And we tend to assume other people want more than they actually do."
  - **Jones AL, Kramer RSS, Ward R (2014)**, *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology* 67(10):2060–2068. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24670156/ **V** (accepted manuscript, https://cronfa.swan.ac.uk/Record/cronfa26122)
  - "Men and women agree on the amount of cosmetics they find attractive, but overestimate the preferences of women and… men… models' self-applied cosmetics are far in excess of individual preferences."
  - **Limits:** the 44 models were all White, wore a "night out" look, and the makeup levels were computer morphs.
- **Script:** "Heavy makeup can make a face look less like the person wearing it, so people notice the makeup more than her."
  - **Tagai K, Ohtaka H, Nittono H (2016)**, *Frontiers in Psychology* 7:226. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26973553/ **V**
  - Light makeup was rated most attractive. Heavy makeup made faces harder to recognise, "possibly because heavy makeup creates an impression of the style of makeup itself, rather than the individual."
  - **Limits:** 38 Japanese women raters; Shiseido authors.
- **Supporting, not spoken:** **Etcoff NL et al. (2011)**, *PLoS ONE* 6(10):e25656. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3185017/ **V**
  - With unlimited viewing time, a natural look *raised* trust and a glamorous look lowered it.
  - The models were Hispanic, Caucasian and African American. P&G-funded.
- **Blush:** a practical tip only (where a flush sits, cream applied with the fingertips). No research is claimed. See "natural flush" in Removed.

### Point 3: even tone

- **Script:** "What makes skin look healthy… is less about smoothness, and more about even colour."
  - **Fink B, Grammer K, Matts PJ (2006)**, *Evolution and Human Behavior* 27(6):433–442. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.evolhumbehav.2006.08.007 **V** (abstract on co-author's university page)
    - Even skin colour was rated more attractive and healthier "independent of facial form and skin surface topography".
  - **Matts PJ et al. (2007)**, *J Am Acad Dermatol* 57(6):977–984. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2007.07.040 **V**
    - Evenness correlated with attractiveness, health and youth (all r > 0.40).
  - **Fink B, Matts PJ (2008)**, *JEADV* 22(4):493–498. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-3083.2007.02512.x **V**
    - "Skin colour distribution seems to be a stronger health cue", while topography mainly signals age.
  - **Samson N, Fink B, Matts PJ (2011)**, *J Cosmet Dermatol* 10(1):78–84. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1473-2165.2010.00538.x **V**
    - Evening colour moved perceived health more; smoothing texture moved perceived age more.
  - **Limits:** mostly white women's skin, P&G involvement.
- **Script:** "And this holds for deeper skin tones too."
  - **Coetzee V et al. (2012)**, *PLoS ONE* 7(10):e48116. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23144734/ **V**
    - Skin homogeneity independently predicted the attractiveness of African women's faces rated by African observers.
    - The same study reported a preference for lighter skin. **Deliberately not carried into the script**, which says "never about being lighter".
  - **Fink B et al. (2017)**, *Evolutionary Psychology* 15(3). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28727930/ **V**
    - Maasai and Tsimane' raters, unfamiliar with light skin, also preferred even colour.
    - The skin images were of British women.
- **Script:** "Wear sunscreen every day. It helps stop new dark spots from forming. If your skin is deeper, look for a tinted sunscreen with iron oxide… blends in without a white cast… guards against the light that can darken spots."
  - **AAD, "How to fade dark spots in darker skin tones"** (updated 10 Mar 2025). https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/routine/fade-dark-spots **V**
    - "Use tinted sunscreen with iron oxide. Iron oxide helps to protect your skin from the sun's visible light (a cause of dark spots)", "avoids a white cast"; "sunscreen can prevent new dark spots and patches."
- **Shade at the jawline, in daylight; concealer pressed in:** standard makeup practice. No research is claimed.

### Point 4: eyes

- **Script:** "When they look red or yellowish, a face can look sadder, less healthy, and less attractive."
  - **Provine RR et al. (2011)**, *Ethology* 117(5):395–399. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1439-0310.2011.01888.x **V** (abstract via the publisher record)
    - Reddened whites looked "sadder, less healthy, and less attractive".
  - **Provine RR, Cabrera MO, Nave-Blodgett J (2013)**, *Human Nature* 24(2):126–136. https://doi.org/10.1007/s12110-013-9168-x **V**
    - Red and yellow whites looked less healthy, less attractive and older.
- **Script:** "And they do change, over the years…"
  - **Russell R et al. (2014)**, *Psychology and Aging* 29(3):626–635. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25244481/ **V** (author preprint)
  - Older faces have darker, redder, yellower whites.
  - **Limits:** French white women only; CHANEL R&T co-authors.
- **Script:** "Making them whiter than they naturally are doesn't add anything."
  - Provine 2013: "super-white" whites looked younger, "although not more healthy or attractive". The script's claim is about attractiveness, which is what this finding supports.
- **Script:** the 20-20-20 rule and artificial tears.
  - **AAO, "Eye Strain"** (5 May 2023). https://www.aao.org/eye-health/diseases/what-is-eye-strain **V**
  - "Every 20 minutes, shift your eyes to look at an object at least 20 feet away, for at least 20 seconds"; "Use artificial tears whenever your eyes feel dry."
- **Script:** "Try not to rub them."
  - **AAO, under-eye bags** (below): "avoid excessive eyelid rubbing." **V**
- **Script:** "Sun, wind and dust are linked to yellowish spots on the whites of the eyes."
  - **AAO, "What Is a Pinguecula and a Pterygium?"** (13 Jan 2026). https://www.aao.org/eye-health/diseases/pinguecula-pterygium **V**
  - "Yellowish, raised growth" on the conjunctiva, "believed to be caused by a combination of exposure to sunlight… wind, and dust"; recommends sunglasses.
- **Script:** "Redness-relief drops are fine once in a while, for a photo or a special day. But used often, they can make the redness come back worse."
  - **AAO, "Redness-Relieving Eye Drops"** (7 Apr 2023). https://www.aao.org/eye-health/treatments/redness-relieving-eye-drops **V**
  - "Use them only occasionally… before an important meeting, for a photograph or for a special event… don't use… for more than 72 hours"; "Sometimes the redness can be worse than before. This is called rebound redness."
- **Script:** "If your eyes stay red, or the whites turn yellow, please see a doctor."
  - This is a safety referral. AAO: see an ophthalmologist if the eye area is red or swelling "does not go away".
  - Yellow whites can signal a medical problem, so the script says "a doctor" and gives no diagnosis.

### Point 5: sleep

- **Script:** "Heavier eyelids. Swollen eyes. The corners of the mouth turning down."
  - **Sundelin T et al. (2013)**, *Sleep* 36(9):1355–1360. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3738045/ **V**
  - After a 5-hour night plus 31 hours awake, observers saw:
    - more hanging eyelids (+15 mm on a 100-mm scale);
    - droopier mouth corners (+11);
    - more swollen eyes (+10).
  - **Limits:** these are perceived cues (not measurements), from 10 white Swedish faces.
- **Script:** "People were photographed after two nights of only about four hours' sleep. Strangers saw them as a little less attractive and a little less healthy, and were less keen to spend time with them. The changes were small, but people noticed."
  - **Sundelin T et al. (2017)**, *Royal Society Open Science* 4(5):160918. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5451790/ **V**
  - Two nights of 4 hours in bed (about 4¼ hours asleep). On a 7-point scale:
    - attractiveness −0.09, health −0.11;
    - willingness to socialise −0.15;
    - trustworthiness unchanged (not claimed).
  - The authors call the effects "arguably quite small".
  - "Protect the two nights before it" is our inference from this design.
- **Supporting:** **Axelsson J et al. (2010)**, *BMJ* 341:c6614. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3001961/ **V**
  - Attractiveness fell about 4% after extreme sleep loss.
- **Script:** the cool damp cloth, head raised, less salt, and dark circles.
  - **AAO, "How to Get Rid of Bags Under the Eyes"** (29 Jul 2025). https://www.aao.org/eye-health/tips-prevention/bags-under-eyes **V**
  - Quoted advice:
    - "Use a cool compress… Wet a clean washcloth with cool water… for a few minutes";
    - "Sleep with your head raised slightly";
    - "limit salt in your diet";
    - "concealer to cover shadows";
    - "People from ethnic groups with darker skin tones are more likely to have dark circles."
  - No AAD page on under-eye bags was found. AAO is the source used.

### Point 6: colour from food

- **Script:** "People who ate more fruit and vegetables over six weeks developed a warmer, golden tone in their skin. And others saw that colour as healthier and more attractive."
  - **Whitehead RD et al. (2012)**, *PLoS ONE* 7(3):e32988. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296758/ **V**
  - Changes in fruit and vegetable intake over six weeks correlated with skin redness and yellowness (carotenoids, not melanin).
  - The estimate for a perceptibly more attractive colour is about 3.3 extra portions a day. The script says "a few extra colourful portions", with no number.
  - **Limits:**
    - observational, with self-reported diet;
    - 35 students, 34 of them white;
    - no effect at 3 weeks;
    - part-funded by Unilever.
  - The authors: "the change may be less evident in dark skin", which is why the script says so.
- **Supporting trial:** **Pezdirc K et al. (2016)**, *J Acad Nutr Diet* 116(8):1257–1265. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jand.2016.03.012 **V**
  - A randomised crossover trial: a high-carotenoid diet raised skin yellowness within 4 weeks. Attractiveness was not rated.
- **Script:** "When it was compared with the colour of a tan, people preferred the golden one."
  - **Lefevre CE, Perrett DI (2015)**, *Quarterly Journal of Experimental Psychology* 68(2):284–293, "Fruit over sunbed". https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25014019/ **V**
  - "Carotenoid colouration is consistently preferred over melanin colouration when levels of colouration are matched."
  - More melanin colour was also rated attractive, so the script never says a tan looks bad.
- **Script:** "Unlike a tan, it asks nothing of your skin."
  - **AAD, "11 ways to reduce premature skin aging"**: "Every time you get a tan, you prematurely age your skin." https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/skin-care-secrets/anti-aging/reduce-premature-aging-skin **V**
- **The foods:** carrots, sweet potatoes, red peppers, tomatoes, mangoes and spinach are common carotenoid-rich produce. This is general food advice; no supplements and no health claim.

### Point 7: kindness

- **Script:** "When people learn that someone is kind, they rate her as more physically beautiful… A single kind act on its own didn't make a clear difference. What did was kindness as part of who she is. Someone who helped regularly, not just once."
  - **Kononov N, Ein-Gar D (2025)**, "Prosocial behaviour enhances evaluation of physical beauty", *British Journal of Social Psychology* 64:e12800. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11923963/ **V** (full text, read myself)
  - 10 studies, N = 4,192. Prosocial people were rated more beautiful (internal meta-analysis d = 0.21).
  - Study 4:
    - repeated volunteering beat the control (4.84 vs 4.47);
    - a one-time act did not differ significantly (4.61).
    - The authors: the effect "only emerged when prosociality reflected a personality trait, rather than merely a situational behaviour."
  - **Limits:** mostly US online samples; descriptions rather than real acquaintances (except Study 1, on Zoom); stronger when raters were motivated toward a relationship.
- **Supporting:**
  - **He D et al. (2024)**, *Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts* 18(4):633–641. https://doi.org/10.1037/aca0000454 **V** (preprint abstract)
    - Pre-registered: faces paired with prosocial stories were rated more attractive.
  - **Niimi R, Goto M (2023)**, *PLoS ONE* 18(2):e0281758. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9925008/ **V**
    - An "honest" description raised attractiveness in a pre-registered study (N = 457).
    - **It did not replicate** in a second pre-registered study (N = 130).
- **Not used as a source:** Kniffin KM, Wilson DS (2004), *Evolution and Human Behavior* 25(2):88–101. **S** only (University of Wisconsin press release). The paper itself could not be opened.

## 4. Originality note

**What the competitors say.** The 13 written sources that could be opened (Bustle, TIME, Psychology Today ×3, AARP, SHEfinds and others) mostly repeat the same tips:
- smile, eye contact, posture, confidence, kindness, listening and humour;
- "wear red" and "makeup for symmetry" for looks.

The two looks tips are the weakest on evidence. None of the 13 sources mentions different skin tones. The named YouTube creators' videos could not be read (see Limitations).

**What this script adds:**
1. **Physical, checkable changes the lists skip:** the resting face, the amount of makeup, even tone, the whites of the eyes, sleep cues, and diet colour. Each has a mechanism and a method.
2. **A distinction in every point:**
   - a hint of a smile, not a smile;
   - less makeup reads as more you;
   - correct, don't cover;
   - clear eyes, not whiter ones;
   - some dark circles are colouring, not tiredness;
   - food colour, not a tan;
   - kindness as a habit, not one act.
3. **Honest timing:** the script says which tips are instant and which take nights or weeks, so "Instantly" isn't oversold.
4. **Every skin tone on purpose** (68- and 34-like comments): the iron-oxide sunscreen tip, dark circles in deeper skin, and saying plainly that the diet research was mostly on lighter skin.
5. **Kindness made new.** The channel's own line ("kindness is one of the most attractive things you can wear") gets the newest evidence and its key finding: a single act isn't enough.

No competitor wording, examples or structure was copied. No celebrities and no personal stories are used.
