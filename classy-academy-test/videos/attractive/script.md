# 7 Things That Instantly Make You More Attractive

## Hook

Have you ever met a woman who seemed more beautiful every time you saw her? Her features never changed. And yet her face looked brighter, softer, more alive.

Very often, that has little to do with the face she was born with. It comes from small things anyone can change. The expression your face settles into when you're not thinking about it. How much makeup you wear. How rested your eyes look.

In today's video, we're sharing seven things that can make you look more attractive. Some work the moment you try them. Others take a few nights, or a few weeks. And none of them involve changing your features. Let's get started.

## 1. Soften Your Face at Rest

Soften your face at rest.

Most of the day, your face isn't smiling or talking. It's simply resting. At your desk, waiting in line, reading on your phone. And that resting face is the one people see most. We form an impression of a face in a fraction of a second.

The trouble is that concentration quietly tightens it. The brows pull together, the jaw clenches, the lips press into a line. And a face held like that can read as stern or distant, even when we feel perfectly content.

So check in with three places. Let the space between your brows smooth out. Unclench your jaw, lips together, teeth slightly apart. And let the corners of your mouth rest a little upward. Not a smile. Just a hint of one. A calm face with that hint of warmth tends to be read more kindly.

A video call is a good place to practise, because your own face is right there on the screen. Composure was never meant to be a blank face. It's a calm, open one.

## 2. A Lighter Hand With Makeup

A lighter hand with makeup.

This one may surprise you. When researchers let people choose how much makeup looked best on a face, men and women agreed. They preferred far less than the women had put on for a night out. And we tend to assume other people want more than they actually do.

There's a reason lighter works. Heavy makeup can make a face look less like the person wearing it, so people notice the makeup more than her. A lighter hand lets them see you, just a little more polished.

To find your own amount, get ready as you normally would, then look in a mirror by a window. Take away one thing. Blend the edges of anything you can still see. If you'd like some colour, try a sheer cream blush. Press it onto the apples of your cheeks with your fingertips, and it looks like your own warmth rather than makeup.

Wear as much as you enjoy. Just know that less usually reads as more you.

## 3. An Even Tone, Not a Flawless One

An even tone, not a flawless one.

We've said it before. Beautiful skin doesn't have to be flawless. Texture, fine lines, a few marks, none of them take away from your beauty. But it helps to know what makes skin look healthy. It's less about smoothness, and more about even colour. A patch of redness around the nose. A few dark spots. A shadow at the corners of the mouth. And this holds for deeper skin tones too. It's about evenness, never about being lighter.

So instead of covering your whole face, correct only where the colour is uneven. A little concealer on the red or dark areas, pressed in with a fingertip. Then, if you like, a sheer tint everywhere else, so your skin still looks like skin. Choose your shade at the jawline, in daylight, so your face and neck match.

And wear sunscreen every day. It helps stop new dark spots from forming. If your skin is deeper, look for a tinted sunscreen with iron oxide. Dermatologists recommend it. It blends in without a white cast, and it also guards against the light that can darken spots.

## 4. Clear, Bright Eyes

Clear, bright eyes.

A detail very few people think about is the whites of your eyes. When they look red or yellowish, a face can look sadder, less healthy, and less attractive. And they do change, over the years, and with dry air and long hours at a screen.

The good news is that you don't need brilliantly white eyes. Just clear ones. Making them whiter than they naturally are doesn't add anything.

Start with your screen. Every twenty minutes, look at something about twenty feet away, across the room or out of a window, for twenty seconds. If your eyes feel dry, lubricating drops, often called artificial tears, can help. Try not to rub them. And outdoors, wear sunglasses. Sun, wind and dust are linked to yellowish spots on the whites of the eyes.

Redness-relief drops are fine once in a while, for a photo or a special day. But used often, they can make the redness come back worse. And if your eyes stay red, or the whites turn yellow, please see a doctor. That's not something to cover up.

## 5. Protect Your Sleep

Protect your sleep.

We often try to cover tiredness with concealer. But tiredness shows in places concealer can't reach. Heavier eyelids. Swollen eyes. The corners of the mouth turning down.

In one study, people were photographed after two nights of only about four hours' sleep. Strangers saw them as a little less attractive and a little less healthy, and were less keen to spend time with them. The changes were small, but people noticed.

So if something important is coming up, protect the two nights before it, not just one. And on puffy mornings, a cool, damp cloth over your eyes for a few minutes helps. So does sleeping with your head slightly raised, and having a little less salt the evening before.

One distinction. Some dark circles come from your natural colouring, and they're more common in deeper skin tones. Sleep won't change those. That's exactly what a touch of concealer is for.

## 6. Colour From Your Plate

Colour from your plate.

This one takes a little longer. People who ate more fruit and vegetables over six weeks developed a warmer, golden tone in their skin. And others saw that colour as healthier and more attractive. When it was compared with the colour of a tan, people preferred the golden one.

That colour comes from natural pigments in brightly coloured produce. Carrots, sweet potatoes, red peppers, tomatoes, mangoes, and dark leafy greens like spinach.

You don't need a special diet. Add a few extra colourful portions across your day. A handful of cherry tomatoes at lunch. Roasted carrots or peppers with dinner. A bowl of fruit where you can see it. Then allow about six weeks. It was studied mainly on lighter skin, so it may show less on deeper skin. But it's a lovely habit, whatever your skin tone.

And unlike a tan, it asks nothing of your skin.

## 7. Make Kindness a Habit

Make kindness a habit.

Remember the woman who seemed more beautiful every time you saw her? That's not your imagination. When people learn that someone is kind, they rate her as more physically beautiful. Her features don't change. The way they see her does.

Researchers found something even more specific. A single kind act on its own didn't make a clear difference. What did was kindness as part of who she is. Someone who helped regularly, not just once.

So think regular rather than grand. The neighbour you check on every week. The hour you give, again and again, to a cause you care about. The friend whose news you always remember to ask about. None of it needs an audience.

Your features are where your face begins. How people see it, you shape every day.
