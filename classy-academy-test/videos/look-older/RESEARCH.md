## 2. Research and fact-check appendix

### Peer-reviewed research (the only two research claims in the script)

**Facial contrast and perceived age (point 6, and the brows in point 7)**

**Porcheron A, Mauger E, Russell R (2013). *PLoS ONE* 8(3):e57985.** https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0057985
- **Sample:** 289 French Caucasian women aged 20–69, photographed without make-up (most with a headband).
- **What fell with age:**
  - Brow luminance contrast fell strongly (p<0.0001).
  - Lip redness contrast fell.
  - Eye colour contrast fell.
- **Manipulation:** the higher-contrast face was judged younger in 93% of forced-choice trials. Single-face estimates were about 1.1–1.3 years younger.
- Quote: "Individual faces were perceived as younger when these aspects of facial contrast were artificially increased."

**Porcheron A et al. (2017). *Frontiers in Psychology* 8:1208.** https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01208/full
- **Sample:** Chinese, Latin American and Black South African women aged 20–80.
- Contrast "significantly decrease[s] with age in all four samples".
- The high-contrast face was chosen as younger in 79.2% of trials.
- Quote: "facial contrast is a cross-cultural cue to youthfulness."
- **This supports the script line** "women from Europe, Asia, Latin America and Africa". Europe comes from the 2013 French sample.

**Script wording:** "judged a little younger" matches the small effect size. No numbers are spoken, following the channel's no-statistics rule.

**⚠ Inference:**
- "A very pale nude… takes away exactly the contrast your face is starting to lose" follows from the contrast finding but was not tested directly.
- "A heavy frame… hides the very feature that keeps your face defined" is also an inference from the brow finding.

**Glasses (point 7)**

**Brunet PM, Sharp S (2020). *i-Perception*.** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7457662/
- Digitally added glasses made young faces look about 1.3 years older, and made no difference on aged faces.
- Quote: "eyewear hardly modulates how we perceive age."
- **Limits:** one study with digitally aged faces and young raters. The script says only "very little difference".
- The often-repeated "glasses add 5 years" comes from a 2010 London Vision Clinic survey that was not peer-reviewed. It is not used.

### Professional guidance

**Frames (point 7)**
- ZEISS Vision Care, "What style of glasses is right for me?": "The upper edge of the frames should run parallel to your eyebrows without covering these up or cutting them off." It adds that very bold frames may cover the brows completely instead. https://zeiss.com/vision-care/en/eye-health-and-care/lifestyle-fashion/what-style-of-glasses-is-right-for-me.html
- AOP *Optometry Today*, "Lessons from an eyewear stylist", Selina Powell, 6 Oct 2023. Dispensing optician Dan Scott's rule of thumb: the top of the frame follows the browline. https://www.aop.org.uk/ot/features/2023/10/05/lessons-from-an-eyewear-stylist
- AARP, Lois Joy Johnson, 8 Nov 2024. https://www.aarp.org/entertainment/beauty-style/best-eyewear-for-women/
  - "Thin frames and clear frames give your brows the most visibility."
  - "Tortoiseshell does for the face what highlights do for the hair."
- Brow pencil "close to your hair colour, in short, light strokes": common make-up practice, not a sourced claim.

**Hems (point 2)**
- AARP, Lois Joy Johnson, 1 Mar 2019. https://www.aarp.org/entertainment/beauty-style/how-to-wear-midi-skirt/
  - "just above the swell of the calf (a couple of inches below the knees)… or just below the calves (so that a few inches of ankle show). The most unflattering length is exactly at the widest part of your calf."
- AARP, Lois Joy Johnson, 9 Jan 2026. https://www.aarp.org/entertainment/beauty-style/biggest-outfit-mistakes-women-50-plus/
  - "Do your midi skirt hems stop at the widest part of your calves?"
  - "Ankle pants should end at or above the anklebone."
- "Back in style": Vogue Scandinavia, 24 Jul 2025 (capris "2025's breakout trend"); Stylist.co.uk, 29 Apr 2025.
- "Hemming is one of the easiest alterations": general tailoring practice. Look Expensive #5 says "as simple as hemming a pant leg".

**Outgrown details (point 3)**
- AARP 2026 (above): "You don't need the belly-emphasizing pleats and leg-shortening cuffs of your oldies." "Power blazers? Sorry, but smaller, more natural shoulders or small shoulder pads are better."
- Removing cuffs is common alteration practice. **⚠ The sources are weak** (a tailoring-school page and forums), so the script says "can often".

**Shoes (point 1)**
- Julie Player, stylist (Hello!, Laura Sutcliffe, 29 Jul 2024). https://hellomagazine.com/fashion/709705/fashion-mistakes-that-age-you
  - "comfortable footwear doesn't have to be frumpy… Avoid clumpy sandals that fasten with Velcro!"
- MM Personal Styling transcript, 1.53M video (`gfbWOw00Y-M`, read 21 Oct): a chunky rounded trainer makes "your legs… look both shorter and wider".
- **Counterpoint:** chunky shoes and clogs are on runways (Who What Wear 2021). That is why the script balances a chunky shoe rather than banning it.

**Prints (point 4) ⚠ weak and contested**
- AARP Sisters, Alaya Linton, 24 Apr 2025: "tiny prints might not have enough impact"; "Opt for medium-scale florals that complement your proportions." https://sisters.aarp.org/culture-style/beauty-fashion/wear-floral-after-50/
- Ali Lees (Hello!, 23 Mar 2026): tunics "especially those in a blousy floral print" look dated. https://www.hellomagazine.com/fashion/889584/stylist-reveals-wardrobe-items-ageing-you/
- **Counterpoint:** Hello!, 16 Jul 2026, says ditsy prints are "immune to the trend cycle".
- **So the script** says "can look fussy, and a little dated", keeps tiny prints for small pieces, and gives a test.
- "Space around each motif" and the photo test are our own practical additions, not sourced.

**Black near the face (point 5) ⚠ stylist opinion only**
- Ali Lees (Hello!, 2026, above): "Black can emphasise under-eye circles and fine lines, dull the skin… Navy is a softer, more flattering alternative for most people."
- Julie Player (Hello!, 2024): "certain shades can make you feel drained and washed out."
- No study tests clothing colour against perceived age. The script frames this as a test the viewer runs, with "some women still look wonderful in it".

**Age rules (point 8) — opinion**
- AARP, Lois Joy Johnson, 2021, "defying age-appropriate". It endorses leggings, bright colour, sneakers and long hair after 50.
- Imogen Lamport, Inside Out Style, Jun 2024: "too many dressing frumpy and old… for fear of looking too young."
- Diane Gottsman (Who What Wear): "It's not the age — it's what you see in the mirror."
- None of these are quoted in the script.

**Left out on purpose:**
- The "Chanel" quote about trying to look young: no primary source exists.
- "Baggy clothes add 15 years": no study exists.
- Any statistic.

**Limits:**
- The search tool blocked the Telegraph, Guardian, Times, NYT, British Vogue, Harper's Bazaar, ELLE, InStyle, Good Housekeeping and Real Simple.
- YouTube blocked competitor captions after two transcripts.

## 3. Originality note

The genre's million-view videos (MM Personal Styling 1.53M, Justine Leconte 1.46M, both transcripts read) mostly repeat four things: fit, bra, accessories, and boxy or baggy clothes. This channel has taught all four already.

This script adds:
1. **The one research-backed mechanism.** Facial contrast is told plainly and turned into two everyday actions: lip colour and frames.
2. **A correction of the "glasses age you" myth.** The research shows the frame matters, not the glasses.
3. **Lines instead of bans.**
   - Where a hem lands (with a test), not "never wear capris".
   - Moving black away from the face, not "never wear black".
   - Print scale, not "no small prints".
4. **Keeping your classics.** The age signal is in the detail (pleats, cuffs, shoulders), so a tailor, not a shopping trip, fixes it.
5. **A synthesis point (8).** It explains why age rules produce the very looks in points 1–5, and answers the channel's top comment (272 likes, "elegance should not end with age").

No competitor wording, examples or structure are copied.
