# 8 Things That Make You Look Older: What to Wear Instead

*Ready-to-record voice-over. Numbered headings are the on-screen point cards. Other headings are section labels. No heading is read aloud. Everything else is spoken, in order.*

## Hook

Have you ever caught your reflection in a store window and thought, I look older than I feel? Most of us have. And often, it isn't your face at all. It's a few small details in what you're wearing. Where a hem stops. The colour sitting closest to your face. The frames you've worn for years.

And these are some of the easiest things in your wardrobe to change.

In today's video, we're sharing eight things that can make you look older than you are, and exactly what to wear instead. We'll start at your feet and work our way up. Some of these may surprise you, but once you see them, you'll never unsee them. Let's get started.

## 1. Shoes That Look Heavier Than They Need To

Number one, shoes that look heavier than they need to.

We get it. When you're on your feet all day, comfort comes first, and it should. But many comfortable shoes are built far bigger than they need to be. A thick, clumpy sole. A wide, rounded front. Velcro straps. All that bulk at the end of the leg can make your legs look shorter, and the whole outfit feel heavy.

Comfort can look light. Look for a slimmer sole, an almond or softly rounded toe that still gives your toes room, and smooth leather or suede. A low block heel, a soft loafer or a clean leather sneaker gives you the same ease with a much lighter line.

And if you love a chunkier shoe, give it balance. Let a straight or wide trouser fall over it, so the shoe looks intentional.

## 2. A Hem at the Fullest Part of Your Calf

Number two, a hem that stops at the fullest part of your calf.

Midi skirts, culottes and cropped trousers are back in style, so keep them. What matters is where the hem lands. When it falls across the widest part of your calf, it draws a line in exactly the wrong place. Your legs look shorter and wider at the same time.

The most flattering midi lengths sit just above the swell of the calf, a couple of inches below the knee. Or they fall low enough to show the slimmer part of your leg above the ankle. For cropped trousers, let the hem end at, or just above, the ankle bone.

Here's a simple test. Stand side-on to a mirror and find the fullest point of your calf. That's the one line your hems should avoid. And if a skirt you love sits right on it, hemming it is one of the easiest alterations there is. Suddenly, the same skirt looks as if it was made for you.

## 3. Details Your Classics Have Outgrown

Number three, details your classics have outgrown.

A good blazer or tailored trousers can stay with you for years. But some of the details that came with them belong to another decade. Deep front pleats add fullness just where most of us would rather have less. A turned-up cuff puts a heavy line at the ankle and shortens the leg. And big, padded shoulders give a jacket the outline of the year it was bought.

Elegant women keep their classics and let the details move on. Look for trousers with a flat front that fall straight from the hip. And for jackets, a natural shoulder, or a light pad that simply follows your own.

You may not even need to replace anything. A tailor can often take out a cuff and re-hem the leg, or remove a heavy pad. The piece you love stays. It simply looks like today.

## 4. A Tiny Print Over a Big Piece

Number four, a tiny print over a big piece.

Small prints can be lovely on a scarf, or a blouse peeking out from under a jacket. The trouble starts when a tiny, busy print covers a whole dress or a long tunic. From across a room, the pattern stops reading as a print and blurs into one busy texture. The outfit can look fussy, and a little dated.

A print usually looks fresher when its size suits the size of the piece. On a dress or a full skirt, try a medium-scale print with some space around each motif, or a simple stripe. Keep the tiny prints for smaller pieces.

To check, ask someone to take a photo of you from a few steps away. If you can still see the pattern, it's working. If it melts into texture, go one size bigger. At the right scale, a print lets people see you first.

## 5. Black Right Under Your Face

Number five, black right under your face.

Black is one of the most elegant colours there is, and you don't have to give it up. But right against the face, a flat, dark colour can dull the skin and deepen the shadows under the eyes. That shows more as our natural colouring softens with time.

Some women still look wonderful in it, so test it. Stand by a window in daylight and hold something black under your chin. Then try navy, charcoal and a soft ivory. Watch your skin, not the fabric. The right shade makes you look brighter and more rested.

If black still wins, keep it. If not, move it away from your face: black trousers, skirt or coat, with a warmer colour at the neckline. An ivory knit, a cream collar, or a silk scarf in a colour you love.

## 6. Lips That Have Lost Their Colour

Number six, lips that have lost their colour.

This one is backed by research. Scientists who study faces have found that as we get older, our features slowly lose contrast with the skin around them. Lips become a little less rosy, and brows a little lighter. When the researchers made that contrast slightly stronger in photographs, the same faces were judged a little younger. And they saw the same pattern in women from Europe, Asia, Latin America and Africa.

So a touch of colour on your lips matters. It doesn't need to be dark or dramatic. Look for a shade one or two steps deeper than your natural lip, in a rose, a berry or a warm red that suits your skin. On busy days, a tinted balm is enough.

The one thing to be careful with is a very pale nude that erases your lips altogether. It takes away exactly the contrast your face is starting to lose.

## 7. Frames That Hide Your Brows

Number seven, frames that hide your brows.

Here's something surprising. When researchers added glasses to faces in photographs, it made very little difference to how old those faces looked. So the glasses themselves aren't the problem. Very often, it's the frame.

Remember the brows? They're one of the features that lose contrast with age. Opticians often suggest that the top of the frame follows the line of your brows, without covering them or cutting across them. A heavy frame sitting across the middle of the brow hides the very feature that keeps your face defined.

So choose a top line that follows your brows, in a colour that suits your hair and skin. Tortoiseshell adds warmth, while thin or clear frames let your brows show. And if your brows have faded, a pencil close to your hair colour, in short, light strokes, brings the definition back.

Your glasses are the accessory you wear every single day. Let them be a pair you love.

## 8. Dressing by an Age Rule

Number eight, dressing by an age rule.

No knees after fifty. No long hair after sixty. No bright colours, no sneakers, nothing fitted. You've probably heard rules like these, and following them can make a woman look older than she is. One by one, they push you towards clothes that are longer, looser, darker and safer than you really are.

Elegant women ask a different question. What suits me today? Your proportions, your colouring and your life, as they are now. Keep what flatters you, update what no longer does, and let the number take care of itself.

Elegance has no age limit. And neither does wearing what makes you feel like yourself.
