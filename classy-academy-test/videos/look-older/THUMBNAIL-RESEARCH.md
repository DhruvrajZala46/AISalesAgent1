### What is constant (all 30, by eye and measured)

1. **Composition.**
   - Three vertical photos edge to edge, split by thin light dividers. Measured at x≈410–413 and ≈851–889 on most; a few have a narrower left panel (#4, #17, #18).
   - The logo sits top-left on every thumbnail.
   - The text block is centred and covers the middle band (y≈190–520).
   - The women fill their panels: waist-up or full-length, faces in the top third.
2. **Subject.**
   - Only women, polished and groomed: sleek buns and ponytails or glossy blow-outs, gold jewellery, manicured hands, often raised to the face.
   - Mostly 20s–40s. Famous women appear unnamed: Kate Middleton (#6, #12, #16, #25, #26), Grace Kelly (#25).
   - A woman of colour appears in most thumbnails (#1, #3, #5, #6, #7, #8, #10, #11, #12, #13, #20, #25, #26, #28).
   - **No grey-haired or older woman appears in any of the 30.**
3. **Colour.**
   - Black, white/ivory, cream and camel, with gold accents and warm light.
   - Outfit topics bring in navy (#9, #29). Burgundy appears once (#5).
4. **Typography.**
   - Top line: wide-spaced serif small caps.
   - Headline: one huge white geometric bold line, 1–3 words, with a soft dark shadow.
   - Small line: serif caps, often between asterisks, or white script (#7, #20, #29).
   - A dark-red rule sits under "Never" on all 4 "Never" thumbnails (#1, #5, #13, #21).
5. **Wording.**
   - The headline is the title's own keyword ("Never Wear", "Expensive", "Elegant Haircuts").
   - The top line frames it ("Elegant Ladies", "How to Look", "The Most", "Become").
   - The small line qualifies it ("\*avoid these\*", "\*Instantly\*", "All Hair Types", "Any Budget").
   - There are no numbers, no question marks, and no exclamation marks except "\*Avoid These\*".
6. **Brightness.**
   - Measured over all 30: the text zone is **brighter** than the panel edges (median ratio 1.21).
   - White text stays readable through its shadow, not by darkening the photos.
   - **The rejected set measured 0.37–0.57:** its middle was much darker than the channel's.
7. **Emotional tone.** Calm, aspirational, quietly luxurious. There are smiles, closed eyes, profiles and some direct gazes. Faces are never shocked or pointing.
8. **Photography.**
   - Real, warm, editorial or high-end creator photos.
   - Luxury cues appear (Paris street, candles, café, car, Birkin) but are never the subject.
9. **How elegance is shown.**
   - The woman is the "after": she shows what to be, not what to avoid. Even "Never Wear" (#1) and "Outfit Mistakes" (#30) show only elegant women.
   - There is no cross, tick, arrow or "before" photo anywhere.
10. **What changes with the topic.**
   - The photos show the video's subject: hair close-ups for hair (#2, #10), full-length outfits for outfit topics (#9, #14, #18, #29), shoes for shoes (#24), a bracelet close-up for jewellery (#27).
   - The text words change.
   - Everything else stays fixed.

### Strong vs ordinary: what correlates (honestly)

- **Correlations:** with one template on all 30, thumbnail measures barely correlate with views (n=30):
  - warmth +0.21;
  - bright area +0.27;
  - brightness +0.11;
  - number of faces −0.17;
  - text width +0.07.

  None is strong enough to act on alone. Upload date and topic explain far more: the ten newest videos are all 0.2–0.6×.
- **Within 2026:**
  - The strong uploads are #5 "Never Do In Public" (498k), #8 "Magnetic" (316k) and #10 "Elegant Haircuts" (242k).
  - The weak ones include #30 "Outfit Mistakes" (31k), #29 "Color Combos" (34k) and #27 "Jewelry" (49k).
  - **Visual difference:** #30 and #5 both use three women with sunglasses. #30's headline is the generic word "Mistakes" with no "Never" rule, while #5 carries "Elegant Ladies / Never".
  - This is a plausible difference, not proof.
- **Repeated in the top 6:**
  - "Elegant" or "Never" is in the text of 5;
  - bright ivory or white panels dominate #2, #4 and #6;
  - every one has a polished woman with a calm face high in the frame.

### Elements preserved in the new designs

- the 3-panel grid and dividers;
- the CA logo top-left;
- the three-line text in the same fonts and positions;
- the channel's measured brightness (no darkening, centre at or above the edges);
- black, ivory, camel and gold;
- calm, polished women shown as the "after";
- the topic shown in the photos;
- no crosses, ticks, arrows or shocked faces.

### What repeats across independent examples, and what it means for you

- **The million-view "look older" winners are creator-face channels.** The creator points or makes a face, beside loud caps text: "YOU LOOK OLD", "LOOK OLDER", "STOP!", red crosses and arrows. Examples: Marie-Anne Lecoeur, Style With Serena, Shea Whitney, MM Personal Styling.
  - They share the **words**. Among the 7 examples here with 1M+ views:
  - "look old(er)" is the main text on 4 (Marie-Anne Lecoeur, MM Personal Styling, Diana GOSS, Style With Serena);
  - Shea Whitney carries it in smaller type;
  - Dominique Sachse says "10 years younger".

  The 21 Oct research found the same pattern: about 22 of 25 winners put the age verdict in the biggest text. They do not share a calm style.
  - Their success comes with a known presenter, so it is not evidence that the loud look would work on a faceless elegance channel.
- **The elegance-styled faceless channels mostly underperformed on this topic:**
  - The Elegance Society 0.27× (a close copy of your "Elegant Ladies Never Wear" thumbnail);
  - Timeless Elegance Style 0.26×;
  - Femme Fatale "Outfit Mistakes That Make You Look Older!" 0.31× (a busy collage);
  - The Essence of Elegance 0.37–0.40×;
  - The Trendy Elegance 0.33×.

## 7. YouTube Studio test plan

1. **Upload with A** as the thumbnail and the title fixed: "8 Things That Make You Look Older: What to Wear Instead". This isolates the thumbnail.
2. **Studio → the video → Test & compare → Thumbnail test:** add A, B and C. YouTube shows them across viewers and picks a winner **by watch-time share**, not by CTR. A result usually comes within about two weeks; low traffic can end "inconclusive".
3. **Do not change the title** or the description's first lines during the test.
4. **After the test:**
   - Keep YouTube's winner and record its watch-time share against the others.
   - If the result is inconclusive, keep A, or keep whichever has the higher watch-time share in YouTube's report.
5. **A title test is a separate, later step.** If you run a combined title + thumbnail test instead, its result will not tell you which of the two made the difference.
