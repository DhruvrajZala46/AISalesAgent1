## 1. The Spencer Tiara

[ON SCREEN: white card, black script: "1. The Spencer Tiara". It appears with the first word of this line, not before it.]

**Let's start on the most famous morning of her life.**

[ON SCREEN: wedding photographs, 29 July 1981: the Spencer tiara on her veil; label "St Paul's Cathedral, 29 July 1981"]

**On the 29th of July 1981, Diana walked up the aisle of St Paul's Cathedral in a tiara that didn't come from the palace at all. It was her own family's, the Spencer tiara. Both of her older sisters had worn it at their weddings.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of Celia McCorquodale's wedding, June 2018, in the tiara; label "Her niece, 2018"]

**So where is it now? It went home. The tiara stays with the Spencer family. And in 2018, her niece, Celia McCorquodale, wore it at her own wedding, the first time in twenty-one years that anyone but Diana had been seen in it.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: a hand opening an old jewellery box, a brooch inside, the box closed again]

**Many of us have a piece like that. A grandmother's gold brooch. A mother's ring. And it sits in a box, because it feels too precious, or a little old-fashioned.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman fastening a vintage brooch or ring before a celebration dinner; then a hand writing a small card and tucking it into a jewellery box]

**Elegant women wear family pieces on their own big days. Not every day, but on the days that matter: a wedding, a birthday dinner, a first day somewhere new. Because a classic piece that has already been to other people's big moments brings a quiet strength to yours. And while you still can, ask the woman who gave it to you for its story. Write it down, and keep the note with the piece.**

**Because the most beautiful jewels are the ones that remember.**

## 2. The Lover's Knot

[ON SCREEN: white card: "2. The Lover's Knot"]

**Now, this one has a little secret.**

[ON SCREEN: photographs of Diana in the Lover's Knot tiara in the 1980s; close-up of the hanging pearls]

**After the wedding, the Queen passed Diana a tiara that had belonged to Queen Mary, the Lover's Knot, made more than a hundred years ago, with pearls hanging beneath diamond arches.**

**Diana wore it often. But it's said to have been heavy, and those hanging pearls moved with every turn of her head. Some say it gave her headaches.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of Catherine in the Lover's Knot tiara at a state banquet; label "Since 2015"]

**After her divorce, it went back to the Queen. And where is it now? Since 2015, it's the tiara Catherine, Princess of Wales, has worn more than any other.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: a woman at a dinner touching and fixing an earring, again and again]

**You know that moment when an earring pinches, or a necklace keeps catching your hair, and you spend the whole evening fixing it? Most of us have been there.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman trying earrings at home in front of a mirror, then changing the backs]

**Elegant women make sure a piece is comfortable before it's admired. Because the moment something hurts, you touch it, you adjust it, and people notice the fidgeting, not the jewel. Instead of wearing a piece for the first time on the night itself, try it at home for an hour before. If it pinches, change the backs for soft ones, or choose a lighter pair.**

[ON SCREEN: the payoff written over the last clip]

**Elegance never looks like it hurts.**

## 3. The Emerald Headband

[ON SCREEN: white card: "3. The Emerald Headband"]

**Here's something unexpected.**

[ON SCREEN: press photographs, Melbourne, 1985: the emerald and diamond choker worn as a headband; label "Melbourne, 1985"]

**On a royal tour of Australia in 1985, at a dinner dance in Melbourne, Diana took an emerald and diamond choker, a wedding gift from the Queen, and wore it across her forehead, as a headband.**

**The pictures went around the world. A necklace, worn in a way no one had seen before.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of Catherine wearing the same choker as a necklace, Boston, December 2022]

**And where is it now? In 2022, Catherine wore it in Boston, this time as a necklace.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a necklace lifted out of its box and put on the usual way]

**Most of us wear each piece one way, the way it came out of the box.**

[ON SCREEN: a row of three: a long necklace looped as a bracelet; a pendant on a short chain at the collarbone; a pearl strand doubled at the neck]

**Elegant women sometimes give one piece a second life. A long necklace, looped twice around the wrist as a bracelet. A pendant moved to a shorter chain, so it sits right at the collarbone above a cream knit. A strand of pearls doubled, so it sits close at the neck like a choker. Instead of buying something new, try one piece, two ways. One piece, two moods.**

**Sometimes elegance is simply seeing what you already own with fresh eyes.**

## 4. The Collingwood Pearls

[ON SCREEN: white card: "4. The Collingwood Pearls"]

**This one is wonderfully simple.**

[ON SCREEN: photographs: Diana in the pearl drop earrings in June 1981 and on her wedding day; then with a black evening dress, 1994]

**A month before her wedding, Diana began wearing a pair of earrings from the jeweller Collingwood: a round diamond with a pearl hanging below. She wore them on her wedding day. And thirteen years later, she wore them again, with a black evening dress.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of Catherine in the same earrings; label "Since 2017"]

**So where are they now? Catherine first wore them in 2017, at a state banquet, and she has chosen them for many important days since, including the portraits for her fortieth birthday.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a pearl box opened, then closed and put back on a high shelf]

**Many of us save pearls for formal occasions, so they spend years in a box.**

[ON SCREEN: two panels: the same pearl earrings with a white shirt and jeans in daylight; with a black dress at night]

**Elegant women let one pair of pearl earrings live in both worlds. With a crisp white shirt and blue jeans on a Saturday morning. With a navy or black dress on a Saturday night. Because a pearl is soft enough for the day and refined enough for the evening, the same pair goes everywhere with you. Real, freshwater, or a good faux pair in soft ivory, they all catch the light the same way. It's the most timeless choice there is.**

**One classic pair, worn everywhere, says more than a drawer full.**

## 5. The Sapphires Made New

[ON SCREEN: white card: "5. The Sapphires Made New"]

**Here's a quiet idea.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of Diana wearing sapphire and diamond studs in the 1980s]

**Among Diana's wedding gifts in 1981 was a set of sapphires from the Saudi royal family. Diana wore the earrings as simple studs.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of Catherine in the reworked sapphire drops, 2022; the before and after side by side on the caramel]

**And where are they now? With Catherine. And she did something clever. She had them reworked into drops, so they move a little and catch the light. She first wore them that way in 2022.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a woman holding up an old ring, then putting it back in the drawer]

**Many of us own jewellery we never wear, because it doesn't feel like us anymore. A ring that feels too heavy. Gold earrings that feel a little dated.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a jeweller's hands at a workbench reshaping a small piece; then a woman wearing a pendant made from a ring]

**Elegant women don't leave those pieces in the dark. They have them changed. A good jeweller can turn studs into drops, an old ring into a pendant, or one lonely earring into a necklace. The stones stay. The memory stays. Only the shape changes, to fit the woman you are now.**

**A jewel should fit your life, not just your jewellery box.**

## 6. The Cross She Borrowed

[ON SCREEN: white card: "6. The Cross She Borrowed"]

**And then, there's the surprise.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of Diana wearing the amethyst and diamond cross, London, October 1987; label "On loan"]

**In October 1987, at a charity gala in London, Diana wore a large cross of amethysts and diamonds, made by Garrard in the 1920s. It wasn't hers. It belonged to her friend, the businessman Naim Attallah, who lent it to her more than once.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of the cross at Sotheby's London, January 2023; label "Sotheby's, 2023"]

**So where is it now? In 2023, after his death, it was sold at Sotheby's in London, for more than twice its estimate. And the buyer was Kim Kardashian.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: two women, one fastening a necklace on the other]

**We often think we have to own something to wear it.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a mother clasping pearls on her daughter on a wedding morning; a small handwritten thank-you note]

**Elegant women share. A mother's classic pearls for a daughter's wedding. A friend's black evening bag for one special night. A sister's brooch for an important interview. Ask kindly, wear it carefully, and give it back with a little thank-you note. And lend your own pieces just as warmly.**

**Some of the most beautiful things we wear are simply trusted to us.**

## 7. The Sapphire Ring

[ON SCREEN: white card: "7. The Sapphire Ring"]

**And if there's one thing to take from this video, let it be this one.**

[ON SCREEN: photographs from the engagement, 24 February 1981: Diana showing the sapphire ring; label "February 1981"]

**In February 1981, Diana chose her engagement ring from a selection by the jeweller Garrard: a large oval sapphire, surrounded by fourteen diamonds. Some newspapers called it the commoner's sapphire, because anyone could order the same design. She chose it anyway.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of Prince William and Catherine on the day the engagement was announced, November 2010, the ring on her hand]

**And where is it now? In October 2010, in Kenya, Prince William proposed to Catherine with that same ring. He said it was his way of making sure his mother didn't miss out on the day.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a woman at a jewellery counter looking at the biggest stones]

**Many of us choose jewellery the way we think we should. The bigger stone. The more famous name. The one other people will notice.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: an older woman turning a simple ring on her finger, smiling]

**Elegant women choose the piece they love, often something simple and timeless, and then they keep it close. Because when a piece truly means something, you wear it often, and slowly it becomes part of you. The next time you choose a piece, ask yourself one quiet question: will I still love wearing this in thirty years?**

[ON SCREEN: archive photograph of Diana smiling; the payoff written over it]

**Because the jewels people remember aren't the most expensive ones. They're the ones that were loved.**
