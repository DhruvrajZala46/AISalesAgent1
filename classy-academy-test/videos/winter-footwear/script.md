## Number one: the heel check

5. Number one: the heel check.
6. You've probably worn a beautiful boot with the heel tip worn down to the metal. In winter it happens faster, because wet pavement and grit chew through those tiny caps.
7. And a worn heel does two things. It clicks and scrapes with every step, and it makes the whole shoe look tired.
8. Instead, take your boots to a cobbler and replace the heel tip as soon as it starts to thin.
9. It's usually quick, and it protects the heel itself, which is the expensive part to repair.
10. Here's a simple test. Stand in front of a mirror and look at your shoes from behind. If the heel leans, or the tip looks thin, it's time.

## Number two: the twenty-four hour rule

11. Number two: the twenty-four hour rule.
12. Most of us wear our favourite pair every single day. But feet sweat, the leather absorbs it, and one shoe-care brand notes that shoes can take around twenty-four hours to fully dry.
13. Boots that never rest tend to crease deeper, lose their shape and start to smell.
14. Instead, rotate two or three pairs, and place wooden shoe trees inside within an hour of taking them off. They hold the shape while the leather dries.
15. The test: look at the toe of your boot. If it's smooth and holds its line, you're doing it right.

## Number three: the heel-slip test

16. Number three: the heel-slip test.
17. It's easy to buy boots that look perfect in the box and feel a little loose when you walk.
18. A tiny bit of slip, about a quarter of an inch, is normal in well-made boots and often settles as the leather moulds to your foot.
19. But if your foot slides around separately from the boot, the fit is off, and you'll walk a little hurried, a little shuffling.
20. Instead, walk around properly before buying. The boot should move with your foot. If the slip is mild, small heel liners can help.
21. And try them on with the socks you'll actually wear. If you'll wear thick winter socks, go up half a size or choose a wider fit, so the boot isn't straining.

## Number four: the small parts

22. Number four: the small parts.
23. A frayed lace. A missing plastic tip. A zip that gapes or catches. These are tiny, and they're the first things people see up close.
24. Instead, replace worn laces with fresh flat laces in a matching tone, and take a sticky zip to the cobbler before it breaks.
25. The test: look at your shoes in daylight, not in your bedroom light. Anything that looks tired will show.

## Number five: the pale-boot problem

29. Number five: the pale-boot problem.
30. Cream and light boots look beautiful in photos, but they show every scuff. One 2026 boot trend article even points out that cream boots mark easily.
31. And in winter, a scuffed toe or a grey edge along the sole reads as careless, however lovely the boot is.
32. Instead, if you love pale boots, treat them as the pair you wear on dry days, and keep a matching polish or cleaner for the toe and the edge of the sole.
33. If you want more forgiving colours, deep brown, burgundy and black hide wear far better.
34. The test: tilt the boot toward a window. If you can see the scuffs, so can everyone else.

## Number six: the quiet walk

35. Number six: the quiet walk.
36. You might not realise how much sound shapes an impression. A heel that clicks sharply, a sole that squeaks on shop floors, a boot that slaps with every step.
37. Elegant women tend to move quietly.
38. Instead, choose a thin rubber heel tip over a hard one, and look for soles with a little grip, especially in winter. It also helps you walk with calm, even steps.
39. The test: walk across a hard floor at home. If you wince at the sound, so will other people.

## Number seven: one trend detail, never three

40. Number seven: one trend detail, never three.
41. This winter's boots come with a lot of personality. Glossy croc finishes, big buckles, stretched sock-style shafts, cowboy edges.
42. They're lovely, but wearing several loud details at once can make an outfit feel busy instead of refined.
43. Instead, let the shape do the talking. Pick one detail you really love, and keep everything else quiet: a plain coat, a simple trouser, a clean line.
44. The test: if you can describe the boot in more than three words, it might be doing too much.

## Number eight: the inside moment

45. Number eight: the inside moment.
46. Here's one nobody talks about. At a friend's home, at an airport security line, at a dinner where everyone leaves their shoes at the door.
47. That's when your shoes are suddenly on show, inside and out. If the insole is grey and worn, or the boots smell damp, it undoes everything.
48. Instead, remember number two. Let your boots dry, use shoe trees, and swap tired insoles before they look tired.
49. The test: slip your boot off and look inside. If you'd be happy for someone else to see it, you're ready.
