Let's start with the easiest one of all. The claw-clip bun.
What makes it so elegant is its ease. It looks relaxed, never stiff, because a clip holds your hair gently, instead of pulling it tight the way an elastic can.
You may already know the French twist, where the hair is twisted upward. This one goes the other way.
Start by brushing your hair back into a ponytail. Then twist it downward, toward the nape of your neck, and keep twisting until it curls into a bun.
Now close your claw clip over the end of the bun, so the teeth hold all of the hair securely.
If your hair is fine, choose a smaller clip, with teeth set close together, because a large one can slip. If it's long or thick, reach for a larger one, because a small clip may pop open.
And here's a detail worth knowing. Look inside the clip before you buy it. A second row of tiny teeth, or a little rubber on the teeth, gives you extra grip.
For a hold that lasts all day, tie the ponytail with an elastic first.
In tortoiseshell or black, it goes with everything, from a navy blazer to a cream knit. And it makes even a rushed morning look intentional.
Next, we have a bun that looks far more intricate than it is. The knotted bun.
What makes it so lovely is its softness. There are no hard lines, and no elastic to hide. Instead, you simply tie your hair, the way you would begin to tie a ribbon.
Start by dividing your hair at the back into two equal parts. You can leave a few soft pieces loose around your face.
Cross one over the other, and tie them into a knot, low at the nape of your neck.
Then tuck the ends around the knot, so it becomes a small, soft bun, and secure it with a few bobby pins.
Choose pins that match your hair color, so they disappear. And a light mist of hairspray will keep everything in place.
It feels soft and a little romantic, and no one will ever guess it began with one simple knot.
Now, let's talk about the bun that lasts all day. The braided bun.
A braid creates texture, so this bun looks far more detailed than the effort it takes. And it's wonderful for medium to long hair.
Start with a low ponytail. That's what gives the bun its hold, because the braid has something to anchor to.
Braid the ponytail all the way to the ends, and secure it with a small clear elastic. If your hair is very long, bring the braid over your shoulder to finish it.
Then roll the braid upward on itself, so it forms a bun, and pin along the edge until it feels secure.
And here's a tiny detail that makes all the difference. Gently pull at the edges of the bun. It instantly looks fuller.
With a little practice, it takes about a minute and a half. It's the one to choose for a long day, a flight, or a dinner that runs late, because you can forget about it, and carry on with ease.

And finally, the most classic bun of all, and the rule I promised. The ballerina bun.
There's something quietly powerful about it. Everything is smooth and lifted away, so the line of your neck and shoulders is what people see. It feels graceful, and you may find you stand a little taller.
Start with a smooth ponytail, at the centre of the back of your head.
Twist it firmly into a rope. The tighter you twist, the more secure your bun will be.
Now, here's the rule. Coil the rope around the elastic in the same direction you twisted. If you twisted clockwise, wrap clockwise.
Keep it flat against your head as you go, and tuck the ends underneath. That's what makes it look smooth and round.
And the pins matter just as much. Hold a hairpin with its points facing the centre of the bun. Catch a little of the hair just outside the bun, then turn the pin, and push it in toward the centre.
Place four of them, like the hours on a clock. Twelve, three, six and nine. If your hair is thick, add a few more.
