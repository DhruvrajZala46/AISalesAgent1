# Creator permission: shortlist and requests (27 Nov 2026)

You chose the route of asking the creators for permission. Here is how it works.

## Files
- `PERMISSION-SHORTLIST.csv`, one row per candidate clip:
  - 42 shots that cover the whole script, timed to the narration;
  - a first choice plus 1-3 alternatives per shot: 110 candidates from 99 different posts;
  - each row has the original Instagram or TikTok post, the Pinterest pinner, the pin's video size, and empty
    columns to track permission.
- `PERMISSION-SHORTLIST.jpg` (in the session work folder): a contact sheet of every candidate. The red label
  marks the first choice for each shot.

## How the shortlist was made
- **Search:** 26 Pinterest searches built from the script's shot list ("video" in every query), giving 723 video
  pins.
- **Kept:** only the 325 pins whose source link goes to a specific **Instagram reel/post or TikTok video**. A clip
  can only be licensed if its original creator can be found.
- **Removed by eye:**
  - clips that look AI-generated: too-perfect cinematic light and faces, and one carries a "MuseFemmeAI"
    watermark;
  - clips that are off-topic, loud or not elegant.
- **Viewed only:** thumbnails. **No clip has been downloaded.** Nothing goes into the video before written
  permission arrives.

## Before you message anyone
1. **Open the `original_post` link** and check the account that posted it is the person who filmed it. Look for
   "credit @…", repost accounts and brand accounts.
   - The Pinterest pinner is often *not* the creator.
   - If the post is a repost, ask the original creator instead.
2. **Brand accounts need the brand's licence, not a creator's** (The Frankie Shop, Ralph Lauren, The Lane). This
   is harder; prefer the alternatives.
3. **Ask for the original file.** Creators' originals are usually 1080x1920 or 4K, with no Instagram/TikTok
   watermark and no compression. That is what makes the footage sharp.

## Message to send (Instagram DM or email)

> Hi [name], I run a small YouTube channel, The Classy Elegance, about timeless, kind elegance for women of every
> age. I love your video [link], and I'd like to use about [3-6] seconds of it in an upcoming video, "7
> Old-Fashioned Habits That Make Any Outfit Look Expensive".
>
> - Your clip would appear with a credit on screen and in the description: "@yourhandle".
> - It would not be used in ads, sold, or changed beyond trimming, colour grading and placing it in a frame.
> - The video will be monetised on YouTube.
>
> May I have your permission? If yes, could you send me the original file (no app watermark), and reply "Yes, you
> may use my video [link] in The Classy Elegance's YouTube video, with credit"? Your written reply is my record of
> permission.
>
> Thank you so much, [your name]

Keep every "yes" reply as a screenshot, with the date and the account name visible. That screenshot is your
evidence if YouTube or anyone else asks.

## What I need back
- **The files** creators send (upload them here), named `S01_handle.mp4`, `S02_handle.mp4` and so on, after the
  shot.
- **The permission screenshots**, or the reply text, for the `permission_status` column.
- **How many are enough:**
  - **The preview:** for the 30-second preview (hook plus number card 1) I need shots **S01-S10**.
  - **The full video:** about 35 of the 42 shots. Shots with no permission get an alternative, or a layout without
    footage (a white quote card, a caramel principle line or a swatch).

## What I do with each file that arrives
- Check it frame by frame:
  - real, not AI;
  - sharp at 1080p;
  - no watermark;
  - it shows what the narration says.
- Log it in `SOURCES.csv` with the permission evidence.
- Place it with the reference's own layouts:
  - **vertical clips as panels** (2-3 side by side on caramel, growing in one per spoken item);
  - **single vertical clips centred** over a soft blur of themselves;
  - **framed clips on cream**.

  This is exactly how Classy Academy uses creator reels.
- Mark any clip that fails, and pick its alternative.
