Have you ever looked at a photograph of Grace Kelly from 1956 and noticed that it still looks elegant today, while most outfits from that year look like costumes? That kind of elegance isn't luck. It has almost nothing to do with a crown, and everything to do with small choices any woman can make.

In this video, we gently rank seven royal women, the moment that proves each one, and the lesson each one holds:
7. Princess Margaret: her Christian Dior gown for her 21st birthday in 1951, photographed by Cecil Beaton. Dress for the photograph you'll keep.
6. Queen Letizia of Spain: made-to-measure Felipe Varela beside high-street Mango, and the same grey suit four times in one year. One good piece among simple ones.
5. The Queen Mother: the white wardrobe Norman Hartnell made for the state visit to Paris in 1938. The beauty of white, near the face.
4. Catherine, Princess of Wales: the nude L.K. Bennett "Sledge" court shoe. A shoe in your own skin tone makes you look taller with any dress.
3. Queen Elizabeth II: cobalt blue, lime green, fuchsia pink, so that people could say "I saw the Queen." One clear colour, worn with confidence.
2. Princess Diana: the short haircut by Sam McKnight after a British Vogue shoot in 1990. Ask your hairdresser one question: what would you do with my hair?
1. Grace Kelly: her 1956 wedding dress by Helen Rose of MGM, with its high lace neckline and long sleeves, echoed by Catherine in 2011. The elegance of covering more.

Seven royal women, and not one of their lessons needs a crown.
