# How "The 7 Most Elegant Royal Women Ever, Ranked" is edited, and why (19 Nov 2026)

The user asked again: check the channel's videos (visuals, animation, music, sound effects), use visuals only from
Pinterest, Instagram and TikTok, no clip used twice, every visual matching its line, and better music.

## What was inspected
- The storyboards of 49 of the channel's 56 long videos (5,572 frames), read on 18 Nov
  (`../diana-things-she-loved/VISUAL-ANALYSIS.md`), and the 128 frames of the channel's jewellery video, read on 19 Nov
  (`../diana-jewels-who-wears-them/VISUAL-ANALYSIS.md`).
- The channel's named-woman videos (`../../VISUAL-EDITING-DNA.md` section 3): a named woman is shown as she is named, in
  real photographs and archive film, with the place and year written in script.
- The two measured files (motion, music level, no sound effects).
- Not inspected: the video files themselves (YouTube refuses the download; no login was used).

## Layouts used (only the channel's own)
Full clips over their own soft blurred copy; a white card per point in black script; real press photographs framed on the
caramel, with the year and place in script; a framed clip on cream with the exact words beside it; rows of two or three
clips with small labels; one sentence alone on the caramel; the guide over a full clip; the CE end card.

**New for this video:** it is a ranking, so each point's photographs show the named woman in the exact moment the line
tells: Margaret in the Dior gown (Beaton, 1951) and the gown in the museum; Letizia in the grey Mango suit and in
made-to-measure gowns; the Queen Mother in Hartnell white (the 1938 sketch "State Visit to Paris"); Catherine's nude
Sledge pumps; Elizabeth II in cobalt, lime and fuchsia; Diana with the short cut; Grace Kelly's wedding, 1956, beside
Catherine's in 2011. Royal archive film is used where a real clip exists.

## Motion and sound
Fade in from black, soft dissolves, fade to white before a card, card text written in, panels growing in softly, a slow
push-in on every photograph. **No sound effects.** **One soft solo-piano bed, about 13 dB under the voice, steady:**
"Gymnopedie No 3" then "At Rest" (Kevin MacLeod, CC BY 4.0), evened by `level.py` (0.99 and 0.89 note onsets a second;
2.1% and 5.3% of their energy below 120 Hz). A new pair, not the last video's music.

## What the build used, and the checks before the render (19 Nov 2026)

- **47 clips** (8 of them real archive or news film: Diana in red and in lilac with the short cut, Catherine walking
  at an engagement, Elizabeth II in green in 2022, Grace Kelly's wedding film of 1956, Queen Letizia twice, the Queen
  Mother in her garden) and **52 real photographs** (portraits and press photographs of all seven women, the Hartnell
  sketch "State Visit to Paris, July 1938", the Dior gown at the London Museum, the L.K. Bennett Sledge). Every clip
  and every photograph is used once. 11:20, 138 words a minute.
- **Princess Margaret and the Queen Mother are shown in photographs only:** no real 1950s film of Margaret and no
  1930s film of the Queen Mother was found; the clips offered were slideshows, AI-morphed faces or The Crown.
- **Music:** "Gymnopedie No 3" then "At Rest" (Kevin MacLeod, CC BY 4.0), solo piano, evened by `level.py`, 13 dB
  under the voice. **No sound effects.**
- **Checks:** two screenings of 191 downloads (frames at about 1 a second) plus my own reading of the archive sheets;
  every used window sheeted at three moments; the last frame of every clip read (one brand reel with a price card
  replaced, four windows trimmed before the subject walks out of frame); 10 crops measured on gridded frames; 151
  snapshots of the whole edit and 27 re-checks; a dark-frame scan (one album clip trimmed before dark frames).
- **Left as it is, said plainly:** no clip shows a woman choosing between two birthday dresses, reading a price tag,
  choosing shoes for a dress, or tugging a short hem; those lines use the nearest real moment with the words on screen,
  and the sources log marks each as PARTLY (26 of 47 clips).
