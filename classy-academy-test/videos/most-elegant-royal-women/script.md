[ON SCREEN: the sentence on the caramel: "It's not the crown. It's the choices."]

## 7. Princess Margaret

[ON SCREEN: white card, black script: "7. Princess Margaret". It appears with the first word of this line.]

**Let's start with a party dress.**

[ON SCREEN: the Cecil Beaton portrait, 1951; label "Princess Margaret, 21st birthday, 1951"]

**In 1951, for her twenty-first birthday, Princess Margaret wore a gown by Christian Dior. Cream silk, off the shoulder, with a full skirt. The photographer Cecil Beaton took her birthday portrait in it, and it became one of the best-known royal photographs of the century.**

[ON SCREEN: the gown on display (London Museum); label "Her favourite dress"]

**She later called it her favourite dress. Today it's kept by the London Museum.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on cream: a woman in front of her wardrobe holding up two party dresses, unsure]

**Most of us dress for a big birthday thinking about the evening. Will it be comfortable? Will it be enough?**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman in a simple, classic dress at a family celebration, a photograph being taken]

**Elegant women dress for the photograph they'll keep. For a birthday that matters, an anniversary, a wedding in the family, they choose one dress in a classic shape and a soft colour, the kind that won't date. Because what's in fashion looks exciting for one season, but a classic shape still looks beautiful in an album twenty years later.**

[ON SCREEN: the question on the caramel: "Will I love this photograph when I'm older?"]

**The next time a milestone comes, ask yourself one quiet question: will I love this photograph when I'm older?**

**Because some evenings deserve a dress you'll want to remember.**

## 6. Queen Letizia of Spain

[ON SCREEN: white card: "6. Queen Letizia"]

**Now, this one is wonderfully modern.**

[ON SCREEN: press photographs: Letizia in a Felipe Varela gown; Letizia in the grey Mango suit; label "Mango, 2025"]

**Queen Letizia of Spain wears beautiful made-to-measure pieces by Felipe Varela. And she also wears Mango, the Spanish high-street brand. In 2025, she wore the same grey Mango suit four times in one year.**

**And the most interesting part? She puts them together. A high-street blazer with a beautiful bag. A simple dress with an elegant coat.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a woman at a shop rail checking a price tag, putting the item back]

**You know that feeling when you think elegance has to be expensive, so you either overspend or give up?**

[ON SCREEN: a row of three: an affordable blazer with a good leather bag; a simple dress with a well-cut coat; jeans with good shoes]

**Elegant women mix. They choose affordable pieces in classic cuts and quiet colours, the kind no one can price at a glance, and they let one good piece finish the look. A well-made bag. Leather shoes. A good coat. Instead of buying everything mid-range, try one special piece and simple pieces around it.**

**Elegance isn't the price of the outfit. It's how well the pieces work together.**

## 5. The Queen Mother

[ON SCREEN: white card: "5. The Queen Mother"]

**Here's something unexpected.**

[ON SCREEN: archive photographs: Queen Elizabeth in Paris, July 1938, in white; label "Paris, 1938"]

**In 1938, Queen Elizabeth, the woman we later knew as the Queen Mother, was about to make a state visit to Paris. Her new wardrobe was ready. Then, just before the trip, her mother died, and the court went into mourning.**

[ON SCREEN: Norman Hartnell sketch or photograph of the white wardrobe (V&A / London Museum)]

**Black would have been expected. But her dressmaker, Norman Hartnell, suggested white instead, also a colour of royal mourning. So he remade her wardrobe in white.**

**She arrived in Paris dressed entirely in white, and the city was enchanted.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a woman holding a black top against herself in the mirror, frowning]

**Most of us reach for black when we want to look elegant. And black is beautiful, but on some days it can feel heavy, especially near the face.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman in a cream knit and ivory trousers, then a white shirt under a camel coat, walking]

**Elegant women know that light colours can be just as refined. Ivory, cream, soft white, worn head to toe or close to the face, look fresh and calm, and they brighten the skin. Try it this weekend with what you already own: a cream knit with ivory trousers, or a white shirt under a camel coat.**

**Sometimes the softest colour in the room is the one people remember.**

## 4. Catherine, Princess of Wales

[ON SCREEN: white card: "4. Catherine, Princess of Wales"]

**This one is wonderfully practical.**

[ON SCREEN: press photographs of Catherine 2011-2014 in nude court shoes; a product photograph of the Sledge pump]

**For years, Catherine, Princess of Wales, wore the same kind of shoe again and again: a nude court shoe by L.K. Bennett, called the Sledge. It became her signature in the early years of her marriage.**

**Royal tours mean long days on her feet, in photographs from every angle. And a shoe close to the colour of her skin made her look taller, without a single extra inch.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a woman looking at her shoe rack in front of a dress on a hanger]

**You know that moment when you find the perfect dress, and then nothing in your shoe rack seems to go with it?**

[ON SCREEN: a row of three women in heels that match their own skin tone, different skin tones]

**Elegant women keep one pair of shoes in their own skin tone. Not one beige for everyone, but the shade closest to your skin. Because when the shoe blends with your leg, the eye sees one long line, and you look taller and more polished with any colour of dress. Look for a simple shape, a soft point or an almond toe, and a heel you can actually walk in. You may already own a pair that's close.**

**Sometimes the most elegant shoe is the one nobody notices.**

## 3. Queen Elizabeth II

[ON SCREEN: white card: "3. Queen Elizabeth II"]

**Here's one you've probably seen without noticing.**

[ON SCREEN: a row of press photographs: the Queen in cobalt, lime green, fuchsia]

**Queen Elizabeth the Second wore bright colours for most of her life. Cobalt blue. Lime green. Fuchsia pink. And there was a reason.**

[ON SCREEN: the quotes on the caramel: "She needs to stand out" (Sophie, Duchess of Edinburgh); "I can never wear beige"]

**Her daughter-in-law Sophie, now the Duchess of Edinburgh, explained it in a documentary for the Queen's ninetieth birthday: she needs to stand out, for people to be able to say, I saw the Queen. Her biographer Robert Hardman remembers her joking that she could never wear beige, because nobody would know who she was.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a woman in a grey coat in a crowd]

**Many of us hide in neutral colours, especially as the years go by, because we don't want to be noticed.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman in a deep blue coat walking; then a red scarf close to the face]

**Elegant women aren't afraid to be seen. One clear colour, worn with confidence, says you came on purpose. You don't need fuchsia. Choose one colour you love, a deep blue, a soft red, an emerald green, and wear it in one bigger piece: a coat, a dress, a scarf near the face. Keep everything else simple.**

**Being seen isn't loud. It's simply being present.**

## 2. Princess Diana

[ON SCREEN: white card: "2. Princess Diana"]

**And then, there's the haircut.**

[ON SCREEN: British Vogue December 1991 / 1990 shoot photographs; then Diana with the short cut]

**In 1990, Princess Diana sat for a British Vogue shoot with the hairstylist Sam McKnight. At the end of the session, she asked him what he would do with her hair if he could do anything. He said he would cut it short. She agreed.**

**That short, soft cut became the look of her last years. Diana later said that when Sam cut her hair differently, it let out something quite different in her.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a woman touching her hair in the mirror, the same style as always]

**So many of us keep the same hairstyle for years. It suited us once, and we're afraid to change it.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman in a salon chair talking to her hairdresser; the question written: "If you could do anything, what would you do?"]

**Elegant women let their hair grow up with them. Not necessarily shorter, but chosen for the face they have now. Here's how to try it. At your next appointment, ask your hairdresser one question: if you could do anything with my hair, what would you do? Then listen. You don't have to say yes that day. But you may hear the idea that changes everything.**

**The right haircut doesn't make you look younger. It makes you look like yourself.**

## 1. Grace Kelly, Princess of Monaco

[ON SCREEN: white card: "1. Grace Kelly"]

**And if you remember only one lesson from today, let it be this one.**

[ON SCREEN: archive film of the wedding, Monaco, April 1956; label "Monaco, 1956"]

**In April 1956, Grace Kelly married Prince Rainier of Monaco. Her wedding dress was made by Helen Rose, who dressed the stars at the film studio MGM, and given to her by the studio as a gift.**

[ON SCREEN: close photographs of the lace neckline and sleeves; then Catherine's 2011 dress beside it]

**It had a high lace neckline, long lace sleeves, and a full silk skirt. It covered almost everything. And it is still called one of the most beautiful wedding dresses ever made. Fifty-five years later, people saw its echo in another royal bride, Catherine, on her own wedding day.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a woman trying on a very short evening dress, tugging the hem]

**Many of us believe that to look beautiful on a special evening, we need to show more. Lower, shorter, tighter.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman in a long-sleeved lace dress at an evening event, calm, smiling]

**Elegant women know that covering a little more often looks more beautiful, not less. A high neckline, a sleeve, a hem below the knee, let your face and your posture do the talking. For your next formal event, try one covered element: lace sleeves, a high neck, or a long, fluid skirt. And let everything else stay quiet.**

**Because true elegance doesn't ask to be looked at. It simply is.**
