# 7 Things Classy Women Never Do in Public - script v1

**The format is copied from your own video** "7 Things Elegant Ladies Never Do in Public" (`oHh4T67jJNM`: 508k views,
2.34% liked, 6:34, 1,089 words). Its storyboard was checked tile by tile (one frame every ~5 s), and the new video
keeps its shape:
1. the observer hook over 30 s of elegant women in restaurants and cafés;
2. the title over a full-screen clip at ~0:34;
3. seven white cards in black script, each naming a thing she never does;
4. inside each point: framed clips on the caramel backdrop with the key sentence written beside them, a row of 3
   women, full-screen scenes, and the payoff line written over the last clip;
5. the guide plug after point 3;
6. point 7 opening as the one to remember above all (the original's "if there's one thing to take from this video");
7. the same outro and the CA end card.

**The tips are new but close cousins of the original ones**, one for one:

| # | Original video | This video |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Over-explaining | Putting yourself down |
| 2 | Speaking loudly | Loud phone calls |
| 3 | Oversharing | Asking personal questions |
| 4 | Arguing or correcting publicly | Bickering in front of others |
| 5 | Always being late | Getting ready at the table |
| 6 | Becoming overly familiar too quickly | Rushing to nicknames |
| 7 | Diminishing your presence | Hiding behind your phone |

Standalone: no "Part 2", and no reference to the earlier video.

The spoken lines are in `beats.txt`. Each bold line below is one beat.

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a woman in an interview or film scene, speaking warmly and at ease]

**So instead of "I'm so clumsy," just smile and say, "Oops."**

**Instead of "I look awful in this one," try "Let's take one more."**

**And instead of "I'm hopeless at this," try "I'm still learning."**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, two friends laughing together]

## 2. Loud Phone Calls

[ON SCREEN: white card: "2. Loud Phone Calls"]
**We've all heard far more of a stranger's phone call than we ever wanted to.**

[ON SCREEN: row of 3, as at 1:33 in the original: a woman on a train, in a waiting room, in a pharmacy queue, each on
her phone; one of them 60+]
**On the train, in the waiting room, in the pharmacy queue. The phone on speaker. A video playing out loud, with no earphones.**

[ON SCREEN: the sentence on caramel: "In a shared space, everyone nearby becomes part of that call."]
**But in a shared space, everyone nearby becomes part of that call, whether they chose to or not. And so does your private life.**

**In public, an elegant call is a small one. Short, soft, and close.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: a woman on a train answering softly, hand near her mouth. Beside it: "I'm on the
train right now. Can I call you back in ten minutes?"]
**For anything longer, simply say, "I'm on the train right now. Can I call you back in ten minutes?"**

[ON SCREEN: close-up, hands taking earphones out of a handbag]
**Keep your earphones in your handbag. And let anything private wait for a private place.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a group of women laughing happily at an outdoor table]
**This isn't about hiding your laugh or your personality. Be warm. Be expressive. Be you.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a woman stepping outside a café to take a call, smiling; the line written over the clip]
**It's simply about keeping your call between you and the person you called. Discretion is its own quiet kind of confidence.**

## 3. Asking Personal Questions

[ON SCREEN: white card: "3. Asking Personal Questions"]
**Curiosity is lovely. It's one of the warmest ways to show we care.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, women around a dinner table, mid-conversation]
**But some questions put people on the spot, especially in front of others.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: one woman at the table leaning in. Beside it, each question written as it is
spoken.]
**"How much did that cost?" "What do you earn?" "When are you two having a baby?" "Have you put on weight?"**

**They're rarely meant to hurt. But you never know the story behind the answer. A money worry, a loss, a hope that hasn't come true yet.**

[ON SCREEN: the sentence on caramel, as at 2:17 in the original: "Elegant women let people decide how much of their
life they share, and when."]
**Elegant women let people decide how much of their life they share, and when.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: two women laughing over coffee. Beside it: "How have you been?" / "What's been

**So they ask questions that open a door without pushing it. "How have you been?" "What's been keeping you busy lately?"**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a woman smiling and lightly waving a question away]
**And if someone asks you something too personal, a warm smile and "Oh, that's a story for another day" is more than enough.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, two older women walking arm in arm; the line written over the clip]
**Because elegance gives people room. Room to share, and room not to.**

## 4. Bickering in Front of Others

[ON SCREEN: white card: "4. Bickering in Front of Others"]
**This one can happen to the kindest of us, usually with the people we love most.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen two-shot, as at 3:31 in the original: a couple at a dinner table with friends; a remark, the

**You're out to dinner, and a small remark slips out. "Well, he never listens anyway." Or a disagreement with your partner, your sister or your teenager starts right there at the table.**

[ON SCREEN: row of 3: women at a table looking awkwardly down at their plates]
**And suddenly, everyone else is very interested in their plate.**

**The person you love feels exposed, just when they most need you on their side.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: a woman touches her partner's arm and smiles. Beside it: "Let's talk about this
at home."]
**So the graceful thing is to gently move the moment. A soft voice, a light touch on the arm, and "Let's talk about this at home."**

[ON SCREEN: the sentence on caramel, as at 3:56 in the original: "It's choosing a quieter room for the real
conversation."]
**Then later, you really do talk, calmly, just the two of you. That isn't pretending everything is perfect. It's choosing a quieter room for the real conversation.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a couple walking out of a restaurant arm in arm; the line written over the clip]
**Because the people we love should always feel safe beside us, especially when others are watching.**

## 5. Getting Ready at the Table

[ON SCREEN: white card: "5. Getting Ready at the Table"]
**This one is so easy to do on a busy day.**

[ON SCREEN: row of 3, hands only where possible (never a recognisable woman shown as "the mistake"): a hairbrush at a

**It feels like no one is really looking. But people are closer than we think. And while we're fixing ourselves, we're only half there with the people we came to see.**

**Elegant women do their getting ready before they arrive. At home, in the car before walking in, or with a quiet minute in the powder room.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: a woman rising from a restaurant table with a smile. Beside it: "Excuse me for a

**And there's no need to explain where you're going. A simple "Excuse me for a moment" says everything.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, close-up of a quick touch of lip balm]
**A quick touch of lip balm is perfectly human. On a rushed morning, just keep it small and tidy.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a woman walking into a restaurant, ready, greeting a friend; the line written over the clip]
**Because when the getting ready happens first, all that's left is the lovely part: being there, calm and fully present.**

## 6. Rushing to Nicknames

[ON SCREEN: white card: "6. Rushing to Nicknames"]
**Ladies, here's a small one that says more than you might expect.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: two women meeting at an event, shaking hands. Beside it: "Elizabeth" ... then
"Liz?"]
**Someone introduces herself as Elizabeth, and within minutes she's "Liz". Or someone we met five minutes ago is already "hun" or "sweetie".**

[ON SCREEN: the sentence on caramel, as at 4:40 in the original: "A name is personal."]
**It comes from a warm place. But a name is personal, and shortening it is a closeness she hasn't offered yet.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a woman at a dinner party listening as someone introduces herself]
**A gracious woman uses a name exactly as it was given, and lets the nickname be offered, not taken.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: the same two women talking. Beside it: "Do you prefer Elizabeth, or Liz?"]
**If you're not sure, simply ask. "Do you prefer Elizabeth, or Liz?" It tells her you were listening.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a waitress and a customer exchanging warm smiles]
**My darlings, where you live, "love" or "honey" may be the warmest words there are. This is only about those first few meetings.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, the two women from the meeting, later, laughing like old friends; the line written over the
clip]
**Because warmth isn't in the nickname. It's in saying someone's name as if it matters.**

[ON SCREEN: white card: "7. Hiding Behind Your Phone"]

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a woman alone in public and entirely at ease. Candidate: the opening of "Breakfast at

[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a woman in her sixties seated alone at a café table, sitting back, hands resting, looking

**Sit back. Let your shoulders soften. Rest your hands on the table and enjoy the room. You're not waiting to be rescued. You're enjoying your own company.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: friends at a table; a phone slipped into a handbag. Beside it: "so they have all

**When someone is with you, let the phone rest in your bag, so they have all of you.**

[ON SCREEN: close-up, hands pushing a café chair back in and gathering a cup; then a woman smiling at the waiter.
