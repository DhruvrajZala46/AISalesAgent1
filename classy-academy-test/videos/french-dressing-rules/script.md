# 7 French Dressing Rules Everyone Should Learn Once & For All - script

Voice: Kokoro af_heart, speed 0.80. Every spoken line is bold on its own line. Every beat has an
`[ON SCREEN]` line. **DO** = a woman following the rule, **DON'T** = the gentle "before" (never
mocking, never a real person's face held up as a bad example). Women of every age (20s to 70s),
size and skin tone, real footage only. The French feeling comes from the pictures (Paris streets,
cafes, markets); the voice teaches the rule itself.

The rules go in the order she gets dressed: jeans, top, what sits near her face, jacket, the mix,
the colour, and the socks last.

## Hook

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in dark straight jeans, a navy sweater and flat shoes walking down a Paris street, unhurried]
**Have you ever wondered why French women look elegant in plain jeans? Which 7 rules do they learn at home that you never did?**

[ON SCREEN: row of three DO clips: a woman in her 60s in a striped top and a trench coat, a woman in her 30s in a white shirt and jeans, a woman in her 40s in a navy blazer]
**You don't need a new wardrobe, or a trip to Paris. It isn't what they own. It's what they know.**

[ON SCREEN: close clip, an older woman straightening a younger woman's collar in front of a mirror]
**Think of these as gentle guides, the kind a mother passes on while you're getting dressed.**

[ON SCREEN: white screen, "7 French dressing rules"]
**We'll go in the order you get dressed. Let's begin.**

## 1. Jeans that hold their shape

[ON SCREEN: number card: "1. Jeans that hold their shape"]
**Number one. Jeans that hold their shape.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a woman in a fitting room mirror pulling on a pair of soft jeans]
**Jeans are what most of us wear most days. And the softest, stretchiest pair always feels best in the fitting room.**

[ON SCREEN: DON'T clip, faceless, from the hip down: very stretchy pale jeans bagging at the knee]
**But by the afternoon, very stretchy denim bags at the knee and clings like a legging. That's the fabric, not you.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, a woman in her 50s walking in dark straight jeans that fall in one clean line]
**Firmer denim holds one clean line all day, and that makes your jeans look as polished as trousers.**

[ON SCREEN: text, three lines appearing one by one: "dark" / "straight" / "firm"]
**So look for three things. A dark, even blue. A leg that falls straight from the hip. And a fabric that feels firm in your hand.**

[ON SCREEN: close clip, hands pinching the denim at the thigh and pulling; the fabric barely moves]
**Here's the test in the shop. Pinch the fabric at the thigh and pull. If it stretches a long way, leave it there. If it barely moves, that's your pair.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman at home on a Sunday in soft faded jeans, barefoot, happy]
**If you love your soft, faded jeans, keep them for the weekend. Just own one dark pair for the days you want to look polished.**

[ON SCREEN: pair: DON'T (pale stretchy jeans with a plain grey sweater, faceless) | DO (the same grey sweater with dark straight jeans)]
**Dark and straight is the classic cut, so it won't date. With one good pair, a plain sweater looks intentional, and you feel put together.**

## 2. Make stripes your print

[ON SCREEN: number card: "2. Make stripes your print"]
**Number two. Make stripes your print.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a hand sliding a floral blouse along a rail of plain clothes, then putting it back]
**Prints can feel like a risk. A floral is lovely on its own, and then nothing in the closet seems to go with it.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in a navy and white striped top at a cafe table]
**A navy and white stripe is different. Because it has only two colours and one simple line, it goes with almost everything you own.**

[ON SCREEN: black and white archive clip, a woman in a striped sailor top by the sea]
**It's a true classic. The sailor stripe has stayed in French wardrobes for more than a hundred years.**

[ON SCREEN: close clip, a striped top in thick cotton, the fabric held between two fingers]
**Choose a striped top in navy and white, or black and cream, in a thick cotton that keeps its shape.**

[ON SCREEN: row of four DO clips, the same striped top: with dark jeans | under a navy blazer | under a camel coat | with white trousers]
**Wear it with your dark jeans. Under a navy blazer. Under a camel coat. With white trousers in summer.**

[ON SCREEN: pair, the same woman, same jeans, same shoes: a plain grey T-shirt | the striped top]
**Try it once. Same jeans, first with a plain T-shirt, then with the stripes. See which one looks more alive.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, a woman in a leopard print skirt with a plain black knit]
**And if you love florals or leopard, keep them. Just wear one print at a time, with plain colours taken from the print.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in her 70s in a striped top, laughing at a market stall]
**It's the top you reach for when you don't know what to wear, and you still feel like yourself.**

## 3. Keep light near your face

[ON SCREEN: number card: "3. Keep light near your face"]
**Number three. Keep light near your face.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a hand taking a black sweater from a shelf in early morning light]
**On a rushed morning, many of us reach for black. It feels safe, and it is beautiful.**

[ON SCREEN: close clip, a white collar under a woman's chin, daylight on her face]
**But the colour under your chin works like a small mirror. A light colour sends light back to your skin. A very dark one keeps it.**

[ON SCREEN: text: "it's not your face. it's the colour next to it."]
**So when you look tired in a black top, it's not your face. It's the colour next to it.**

[ON SCREEN: pair, the same woman by a window: a black sweater | a navy sweater]
**French women often choose navy where others choose black, because it's just as dark, but softer against the skin.**

[ON SCREEN: row of three DO clips: a white shirt collar showing above a black sweater | a cream T-shirt under a black blazer | a row of pearls on a black dress]
**Or keep your black, and add light. A white collar above a black sweater. A cream T-shirt at the neckline. A row of pearls. You may already own one of them.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a woman by a window holding a white cloth under her chin, then a black one]
**Try this by a window. Hold something white under your chin, then something black. Watch your face, not the fabric.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, a woman with deep skin in a black turtleneck, glowing, smiling]
**And if black lights you up, wear it with joy. This isn't a rule against black. It's a reason to look in the mirror first.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in her 40s in a navy sweater and white collar fastening an earring, calm]
**It takes ten seconds, and you start the day liking what you see.**

[ON SCREEN: text: "team navy, or team black?" with a small comment icon]
**So tell us. Are you team navy, or team black?**

## 4. Borrow from the men's side

[ON SCREEN: number card: "4. Borrow from the men's side"]
**Number four. Borrow from the men's side. This one may surprise you.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a woman holding a small fitted jacket against herself in a mirror]
**When we want to look polished, we often reach for the most fitted piece we own. And a fitted jacket can be lovely.**

[ON SCREEN: pair: a fitted jacket pulling at the button, faceless | a man's navy blazer hanging straight from a woman's shoulders]
**But a piece cut close follows every line of the body. A man's jacket falls straight from the shoulder, so the fabric skims and you can move.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in her 60s walking in a roomy navy blazer and straight trousers, hands in her pockets]
**That ease is what looks so chic.**

[ON SCREEN: row of three DO clips: a pale blue men's cotton shirt | a navy blazer from the men's rail | a fine grey wool sweater, a size up]
**Try a classic men's shirt in white or pale blue cotton. A navy blazer from the men's rail. A fine wool sweater, one size up.**

[ON SCREEN: close clip, a hand resting on a jacket shoulder seam that sits exactly on the shoulder]
**Choose it by the shoulders. If the seam sits on your shoulder, the rest can be roomy. And they're easy to find second hand.**

[ON SCREEN: row of three close clips: small gold earrings | a silk blouse under the blazer | ballet flats]
**Then add one thing that is clearly yours. Small earrings, a silk blouse, or a pair of ballet flats.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, a woman in a fitted dress with a man's leather-strap watch on her wrist]
**If you feel most like yourself in fitted, feminine pieces, keep them. Borrow just one thing, like a shirt or a watch.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in a men's shirt reading at a cafe terrace, completely relaxed]
**You're not dressing to impress anyone. You're comfortable, and there's a quiet confidence in that.**

[ON SCREEN: panel, the stylist guide]
**And if you'd like to know which colours and shapes suit you best, our personal stylist guide is linked in the description.**

## 5. One dressy, one casual

[ON SCREEN: number card: "5. One dressy, one casual"]
**Number five. One dressy piece, one casual piece.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a dress, heels and jewellery laid out together on a bed]
**When we dress up, we tend to dress up everything. The dress, the heels, the jewellery. And on an ordinary day, everything is casual.**

[ON SCREEN: pair, both faceless: all dressed up for the morning | all in loungewear]
**All dressed up can feel stiff, as if you're on your way to an event. All casual can feel unfinished.**

[ON SCREEN: text: "dress one thing up. dress one thing down."]
**The mix is what looks relaxed and polished at once. Dress one thing up. Dress one thing down.**

[ON SCREEN: row of three DO clips: a silk blouse with dark jeans at a lunch table | a knit dress with white sneakers at a grocery store | tailored trousers with a plain white T-shirt]
**A silk blouse with your dark jeans, for lunch with a friend. A simple knit dress with clean white sneakers, for the grocery store. Tailored trousers with a plain T-shirt.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a woman at the mirror by her front door touching her blouse, then her jeans]
**Before you leave, name them. Which piece is the dressy one? Which one is the easy one? If you can name both, you're ready.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, a woman in a long evening dress and heels stepping out of a car]
**For a wedding or a formal evening, dress all the way up, of course. This one is for every other day.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in a silk blouse and jeans walking through a market with a basket]
**You're never overdressed and never underdressed. You feel at ease wherever the day takes you.**

## 6. A scarf carries the colour

[ON SCREEN: number card: "6. A scarf carries the colour"]
**Number six. Let a scarf carry the colour.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a bright jacket on a hanger with the price tag still on]
**Colour is a joy. And it's easy to buy a bright jacket, wear it once, and feel it's a little too much.**

[ON SCREEN: close clip, a red silk scarf being tied at a woman's neck, her face in soft light]
**A scarf is the easy way to wear colour, because it sits right by your face, it costs far less than a coat, and you can change it every day.**

[ON SCREEN: row of three DO clips: navy and cream | grey and white | camel and black, each with jeans]
**So keep the outfit quiet. Two calm colours, plus your jeans. Navy and cream. Grey and white. Camel and black.**

[ON SCREEN: row of three scarves on a table: red, emerald, soft pink]
**Then choose one scarf in the colour you love. Red, emerald, soft pink. You may already own it.**

[ON SCREEN: three short clips: a scarf looped once with the ends hanging | tucked inside a coat collar | tied to a bag handle]
**Loop it once and let the ends hang. Tuck it inside a coat collar. Or tie it to the handle of your bag.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a woman by a window holding two scarves up to her face, one after the other]
**Hold it up to your face in daylight. The right one makes your eyes look brighter.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, a woman in a bright yellow dress and a pink coat, radiant, walking fast]
**And if you love colour from head to toe, wear it. A scarf is simply an easy place to start.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in her 50s in navy and cream with an emerald scarf, smiling in a mirror]
**One square of colour, chosen with intention, and getting dressed feels like a small pleasure again.**

## 7. Socks follow the shoes

[ON SCREEN: number card: "7. Socks follow the shoes"]
**Number seven, and it's the smallest one. Your socks follow your shoes.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a hand taking the first pair of socks from a full drawer]
**We choose the outfit with care, and then take the first pair of socks from the drawer. We all do it.**

[ON SCREEN: DON'T close clip, faceless: a white sock between dark trousers and a black loafer]
**But a white sock between dark trousers and a dark shoe draws a bright line at the ankle, and that's where the eye stops.**

[ON SCREEN: DO close clip, the same trousers and loafer with a black sock, one unbroken line]
**When the sock matches the shoe, the eye travels in one line, from your waist to your toes, and that makes your legs look longer.**

[ON SCREEN: row of three DO close clips: black loafers with black socks | white sneakers with white socks | a skirt with black tights and black shoes]
**So, dark shoes, dark socks. Light shoes, light socks. And with a skirt in winter, black tights with black shoes.**

[ON SCREEN: clip, a woman sitting in front of a long mirror, crossing her legs; her ankle shows]
**Sit down in front of a mirror and cross your legs. Whatever shows at your ankle is part of your outfit.**

[ON SCREEN: DO close clip, a red sock with navy trousers and brown loafers, nothing else red]
**And if you love a red sock, wear it on purpose. Let it be your one colour, in place of the scarf.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman walking away down a street, dark trousers, dark socks, loafers]
**Nobody will ever compliment your socks. That's the point. Everything simply looks finished, and you'll know why.**

one colour top to toe. Seven is also inside the craft rule (6-8 points for 8-10 minutes), and two videos in this
title family use 7 (Valeria Lipovetsky 420k, Teachingmensfashion 697k).

**Not repeated from our own videos** ("8 Style Rules Every Woman Should Know", "7 Things Elegant French Women Never

## How the study shaped the writing

- **Title:** the most-copied title in women's style, ten videos from eight channels, none under 420k (10.67M, 9.36M,
  8.06M; the French version 1.87M at 4.43% liked). Ours is 59 characters.
- **Hook:** the Mademoiselle Spends shape (the question everyone has -> rules learned at home -> the promise), opened
  with the channel's own "Have you ever..." and with the barrier removed ("You don't need a new wardrobe, or a trip
  to Paris"). No fear. It pays off in rule 1 (the jeans).
- **Softening:** Audrey Coyne (8.06M): "when I say rules, I don't mean you must" -> "Think of these as gentle guides".
- **Each rule:** a 2-5 word name, the common habit without blame, the reason, a line she can repeat, named pieces and
  colours, a test she can do (the pull test, the window test, the sit-down test), and one exception.
- **One direction:** the order she gets dressed, jeans to socks.
- **One question** at 43% ("team navy, or team black?"), the guide once (end of rule 4), the names in the recap, the
  number ask and the part-two ask at the close (Audrey Coyne asked; her Part 2 reached 1.29M).
- **Blame off her body:** "That's the fabric, not you", "it's not your face. It's the colour next to it."
- **One thesis, in the hook and the close:** it isn't what they own, it's what they know.
