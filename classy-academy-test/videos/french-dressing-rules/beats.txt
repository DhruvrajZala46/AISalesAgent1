Have you ever wondered why French women look elegant in plain jeans? Which 7 rules do they learn at home that you never did?
You don't need a new wardrobe, or a trip to Paris. It isn't what they own. It's what they know.
Think of these as gentle guides, the kind a mother passes on while you're getting dressed.
We'll go in the order you get dressed. Let's begin.
Number one. Jeans that hold their shape.
Jeans are what most of us wear most days. And the softest, stretchiest pair always feels best in the fitting room.
But by the afternoon, very stretchy denim bags at the knee and clings like a legging. That's the fabric, not you.
Firmer denim holds one clean line all day, and that makes your jeans look as polished as trousers.
So look for three things. A dark, even blue. A leg that falls straight from the hip. And a fabric that feels firm in your hand.
Here's the test in the shop. Pinch the fabric at the thigh and pull. If it stretches a long way, leave it there. If it barely moves, that's your pair.
If you love your soft, faded jeans, keep them for the weekend. Just own one dark pair for the days you want to look polished.
Dark and straight is the classic cut, so it won't date. With one good pair, a plain sweater looks intentional, and you feel put together.
Number two. Make stripes your print.
Prints can feel like a risk. A floral is lovely on its own, and then nothing in the closet seems to go with it.
A navy and white stripe is different. Because it has only two colours and one simple line, it goes with almost everything you own.
It's a true classic. The sailor stripe has stayed in French wardrobes for more than a hundred years.
Choose a striped top in navy and white, or black and cream, in a thick cotton that keeps its shape.
Wear it with your dark jeans. Under a navy blazer. Under a camel coat. With white trousers in summer.
Try it once. Same jeans, first with a plain T-shirt, then with the stripes. See which one looks more alive.
And if you love florals or leopard, keep them. Just wear one print at a time, with plain colours taken from the print.
It's the top you reach for when you don't know what to wear, and you still feel like yourself.
Number three. Keep light near your face.
On a rushed morning, many of us reach for black. It feels safe, and it is beautiful.
But the colour under your chin works like a small mirror. A light colour sends light back to your skin. A very dark one keeps it.
So when you look tired in a black top, it's not your face. It's the colour next to it.
French women often choose navy where others choose black, because it's just as dark, but softer against the skin.
Or keep your black, and add light. A white collar above a black sweater. A cream T-shirt at the neckline. A row of pearls. You may already own one of them.
Try this by a window. Hold something white under your chin, then something black. Watch your face, not the fabric.
And if black lights you up, wear it with joy. This isn't a rule against black. It's a reason to look in the mirror first.
It takes ten seconds, and you start the day liking what you see.
So tell us. Are you team navy, or team black?
Number four. Borrow from the men's side. This one may surprise you.
When we want to look polished, we often reach for the most fitted piece we own. And a fitted jacket can be lovely.
But a piece cut close follows every line of the body. A man's jacket falls straight from the shoulder, so the fabric skims and you can move.
That ease is what looks so chic.
Try a classic men's shirt in white or pale blue cotton. A navy blazer from the men's rail. A fine wool sweater, one size up.
Choose it by the shoulders. If the seam sits on your shoulder, the rest can be roomy. And they're easy to find second hand.
Then add one thing that is clearly yours. Small earrings, a silk blouse, or a pair of ballet flats.
If you feel most like yourself in fitted, feminine pieces, keep them. Borrow just one thing, like a shirt or a watch.
You're not dressing to impress anyone. You're comfortable, and there's a quiet confidence in that.
And if you'd like to know which colours and shapes suit you best, our personal stylist guide is linked in the description.
Number five. One dressy piece, one casual piece.
When we dress up, we tend to dress up everything. The dress, the heels, the jewellery. And on an ordinary day, everything is casual.
All dressed up can feel stiff, as if you're on your way to an event. All casual can feel unfinished.
The mix is what looks relaxed and polished at once. Dress one thing up. Dress one thing down.
A silk blouse with your dark jeans, for lunch with a friend. A simple knit dress with clean white sneakers, for the grocery store. Tailored trousers with a plain T-shirt.
Before you leave, name them. Which piece is the dressy one? Which one is the easy one? If you can name both, you're ready.
For a wedding or a formal evening, dress all the way up, of course. This one is for every other day.
You're never overdressed and never underdressed. You feel at ease wherever the day takes you.
Number six. Let a scarf carry the colour.
Colour is a joy. And it's easy to buy a bright jacket, wear it once, and feel it's a little too much.
A scarf is the easy way to wear colour, because it sits right by your face, it costs far less than a coat, and you can change it every day.
So keep the outfit quiet. Two calm colours, plus your jeans. Navy and cream. Grey and white. Camel and black.
Then choose one scarf in the colour you love. Red, emerald, soft pink. You may already own it.
Loop it once and let the ends hang. Tuck it inside a coat collar. Or tie it to the handle of your bag.
Hold it up to your face in daylight. The right one makes your eyes look brighter.
And if you love colour from head to toe, wear it. A scarf is simply an easy place to start.
One square of colour, chosen with intention, and getting dressed feels like a small pleasure again.
Number seven, and it's the smallest one. Your socks follow your shoes.
We choose the outfit with care, and then take the first pair of socks from the drawer. We all do it.
But a white sock between dark trousers and a dark shoe draws a bright line at the ankle, and that's where the eye stops.
When the sock matches the shoe, the eye travels in one line, from your waist to your toes, and that makes your legs look longer.
So, dark shoes, dark socks. Light shoes, light socks. And with a skirt in winter, black tights with black shoes.
Sit down in front of a mirror and cross your legs. Whatever shows at your ankle is part of your outfit.
And if you love a red sock, wear it on purpose. Let it be your one colour, in place of the scarf.
Nobody will ever compliment your socks. That's the point. Everything simply looks finished, and you'll know why.
So, the seven rules.
Jeans that hold their shape.
Stripes as your print.
Light near your face.
Borrow from the men's side.
One dressy piece, one casual piece.
A scarf to carry the colour.
And socks that follow your shoes.
None of them asks you to look younger, or thinner, or richer. They work at any age. It was never about what French women own. It's what they know, and now you know it too.
