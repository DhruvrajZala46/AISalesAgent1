**The one device that marks a detail.** "10 Fashion Tricks Princess Diana Used to Make Old Clothes Look New Again"

**The list: what makes one of these thumbnails win and another not.**
1. **She is the picture.** Diana herself, large, in an outfit people know. Not other royals, not a presenter.
2. **One picture beats a collage.** One clear image did three times better than two or more.
3. **No words, or very few.** The title carries the promise; the picture carries the feeling.
4. **A real press photograph.** AI-smoothed portraits and drawings sit on the losers.
5. **A known moment.** The black dress, the polka dots, the wedding gown: people click what they recognise.
6. **Nothing tabloid.** "BREAKING", tears and quote boxes have the most failures (29% under their channel's usual).

## 5. How they were made

- **A:** `tools/thumb_ca_real.py` with `thumb_spec_ca_1080.json`: your exact layout (three photos edge to edge,
  Cormorant small capitals, bold Montserrat headline, the red line under the first word, the small line with
  asterisks, the CA logo, the soft darkening and shadow behind the words). Every face sits above the headline.
- **B and C:** `thumb_research/build_bc.py`: a gentle warm grade, a soft dark vignette that pulls the eye to her, the
  CA logo on a soft dark patch; C adds two gold rings with a soft shadow so they read on light and dark.
- **Checks:** side by side with nine of the million-view Diana thumbnails at feed size (360 px) and phone size
  (168 px): all three read, and A's headline is legible at phone size. Brightness 104, 105 and 108. Sharpness: A's
  panels 1.2 to 2.0 source pixels per output pixel; C 1.09 and 0.94; **B 0.83 (the photo is enlarged by a fifth)**, a
  little soft and grainy at full size, not visible at feed size.
- Nobody is retouched. Faces, bodies and backgrounds are as in the press photographs.
