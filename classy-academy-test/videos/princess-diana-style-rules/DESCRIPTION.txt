When you get dressed, are your clothes saying what you want them to say, before you say a word? Princess Diana's clothes are still talked about almost thirty years later. Not because they were the most expensive. Because every outfit seemed to say something.

In this video, we gently walk through eight of her style rules, with the real moments behind them, and exactly how to use each one:
1. Dress for the people you're about to meet: ask one question first. "Who will I see today, and what would make them feel at ease?"
2. Never let your clothes become a barrier: if you can't hug someone or sit down with a child in it, it isn't the right outfit for that day.
3. Honour your host: at a wedding, leave white and ivory to the bride. For dinner, dress one step more carefully than you need to.
4. Plan for the awkward moment: before you leave the house, sit down in it, bend, lift your arms, walk up a stair.
5. Re-wear what works: instead of buying something new, change one thing. A camel belt, a navy jacket, your hair up.
6. Keep an off-duty uniform: dark jeans, a crisp white shirt, a navy or camel jacket, and comfortable loafers.
7. Edit as you grow: keep only what you would wear this month, and give one thing to someone who'll wear it.
8. Keep one outfit for courage: not the newest thing you own, the one that makes you stand a little taller.

You'll see the moments behind each rule: her "caring dress" for visiting children in hospital, the handshake without gloves in April 1987, the red polka dots in Japan in 1986, the clutch bags she held as she stepped out of cars, the 79 dresses she let go for charity in 1997, and the black dress of 29 June 1994.

Her clothes weren't remembered because they were expensive. They were remembered because of how she made people feel in them.
