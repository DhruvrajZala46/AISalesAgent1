Have you ever stood in front of the mirror in an outfit you love, and something just felt off?
The colours were right. The fabric was lovely. And yet the whole look seemed a little heavier, a little shorter, a little less polished than you pictured it.
More often than not, it isn't the clothes. It's the lines they draw.
Every hem, every seam and every strap draws a line across your body, and the eye follows those lines before it notices anything else. Put a line in the right place, and you look taller, lighter and more put together. Put it in the wrong place, and even an expensive outfit can look cheap.
In today's video, we're revealing six proportion mistakes that quietly cheapen an outfit, and the simple ways elegant women fix their lines instead.
None of them need a new wardrobe. Some of these may surprise you, but once you see them, you'll never unsee them. Let's get started.
Number one, a layer that peeks out from underneath.
We've all done it. A soft knit under a blazer, with a few inches of sweater hanging out below the jacket. Or a shirt tail showing under a jumper, or a dress peeking out beneath a coat.
It feels relaxed. But every extra hem adds another line across your middle. Instead of one clean shape, the eye sees a stack of little bands, and your upper body looks shorter and busier than it really is.
Elegant women let one layer finish the outfit.
Instead, tuck the knit into high-waisted trousers, or front-tuck it, so the jacket's hem draws the only line. Or choose a fine knit that stops at the waist, short enough to disappear under the jacket.
With a coat, the same rule applies. Let the coat cover the dress completely, or choose a short jacket that ends well above it, so the difference looks planned, never accidental.
Before you leave, turn to the mirror and count the hems you can see. If there's one more than you meant, tuck it, change it, or take a layer off.
Number two, a waistline that sits too low.
Dropped-waist dresses, jumpsuits that tie at the hips, and soft trousers where the seam hangs low between the legs. They're so comfortable, and many of them are very much in fashion.
But wherever a seam marks your waist, the eye believes that's where your legs begin. When that line slips down to your hips, your body looks long, your legs look short, and the outfit starts to look shapeless.
Elegant women keep their waistline where nature put it.
Instead, look for dresses and jumpsuits with the seam, the tie or the belt at your natural waist, the narrowest part of your middle. A wrap dress does this beautifully.
With trousers, stand side-on to the mirror. The seam should follow your body closely, without pulling, and without a soft fold of fabric hanging between your legs. A straight or wide-leg trouser that sits at your waist gives you back inches of leg.
You're exactly the same height you've always been. It just finally shows.
Number three, short sleeves that stop at the widest part of your arm.
So many of us would love to wear short sleeves, but feel unsure about our arms.
Here's what often happens. A tight sleeve stops right at the fullest part of the upper arm. The hem grips the arm and draws a line straight across its widest point, so the arm looks wider than it really is.
Elegant women let a sleeve skim, never squeeze.
Instead, try a sleeve that ends a little above the elbow, where the arm is slimmer, or a soft, slightly wider sleeve that floats away from the skin. A flutter sleeve, or an elbow-length tee in a fluid fabric, is wonderfully forgiving.
And in summer, a sleeveless top with a wider strap, about two fingers wide, looks far more balanced than a thin spaghetti strap.
When you try a top on, lift your arms a little. If the sleeve digs in or rides up, it's the cut, not your arm.

Number four, a gap between your skirt and your boots.
Few things feel more elegant in the colder months than a midi skirt with tall boots.
But when the skirt stops just above the boot, a little strip of bare leg shows in between. That strip cuts your leg into short pieces, and the eye stops there instead of travelling down to the floor. Suddenly the outfit looks unfinished.
Elegant women make the skirt and the boot meet.
Instead, choose a skirt long enough to overlap the top of your boots by a few inches, even when you walk. And check the slit, because that's where the gap usually appears.
Or close the gap with tights in the same colour as your boots. Black tights with black boots, deep brown with brown, and your leg becomes one long, unbroken line.
It's one of those tiny details that makes an outfit look truly considered.
Number five, a line across your ankle.
A pretty ankle strap, a bright sock peeking out, or pale shoes with dark tights. Each one feels like such a small thing.
But your ankle is the slimmest part of your leg, and the last place the eye travels. When a strap or a sudden change of colour cuts across it, your leg seems to end right there, a little earlier than it really does.
Elegant women keep the line from the hem to the shoe uninterrupted.
Instead, match your shoes to your tights, or your socks to your trousers, so the colour flows straight down. With bare legs, a shoe close to your skin tone, or a low-cut pump or loafer, makes the leg look longer without a single extra inch of heel.
And if you love an ankle strap, choose a very fine one, in the same colour as the shoe.
Your legs don't need to change at all. The line simply has to keep going.
Number six, a jacket that's buttoned the wrong way.
A blazer is one of the most elegant pieces a woman can own, which is why this one matters so much.
When every button is done up, or when a fitted jacket pulls into little creases shaped like an X around the button, those lines drag across your middle and make even a beautiful jacket look tight and cheap.
Elegant women let a jacket fall in a clean V.
Instead, on a two-button jacket, close only the top button. On a one-button jacket, close it when you stand and open it when you sit. A double-breasted jacket looks best kept closed, so choose one that fastens flat.
And if the button pulls into that X, simply wear the jacket open. Two long, straight edges falling down the front will lengthen you far more than a strained button ever could.
That clean V at the front is the quiet signature of a woman who knows how to wear tailoring.
Before you buy anything new, try these six fixes on what's already in your wardrobe. Very often, the outfit that never felt quite right only needed one line moved.
Tuck a layer, lift a sleeve, close the gap, match the colour, change a button, and notice how differently you stand in front of the mirror. Because elegance was never about adding more. It's about letting every line work with you.
