## The script (spoken, line by line; numbers = beats.txt lines)


### Hook

1. Have you ever said something you were completely sure of, and heard it come out sounding like a question?
2. Or tried to speak softly, only to have someone talk right over you?
3. In our first video on speaking elegantly, we talked about slowing down, softening your voice, and replying with grace.
4. Today, we're going one step further. Six more speaking habits that make you sound certain, clear and warm, from the first word of a conversation to the moment someone tries to interrupt you.
5. Many of them came from your comments, so thank you. Let's begin.

### 1. Let your sentences land

6. First, let your sentences land.
7. When we feel a little nervous, our voice often rises at the end of a sentence, even when we're not asking anything. "I'm Claire? I work in design?"
8. It sounds friendly, but it quietly asks for permission, and it makes you sound unsure of something you know perfectly well.
9. Now listen to Audrey Hepburn in Charade. She's teasing, she's relaxed, and every sentence still comes down at the end.
   *[Audrey Hepburn, "Charade" (1963), her own voice: "Because I already know an awful lot of people, and until one of them dies, I couldn't possibly meet anyone else."]*
10. Light, playful, and completely certain. That's the feeling we want.
11. Here's how to practice. The next time you introduce yourself, let your voice come down on the last word. "I'm Claire. I work in design."
12. When you order, "I'd like the table by the window." A full stop, not a question mark.
13. And when you share an opinion at work, finish it, and then stop talking. The pause does the rest.
14. A sentence that lands sounds like a decision. A sentence that rises sounds like a request.

### 2. Speak clearly, not loudly

15. Speak clearly, not loudly.
16. After our first video, some of you told us something important. When you lowered your voice, people talked over you, or asked you to speak up.
17. That's because soft doesn't mean swallowed. A graceful voice is quiet, but it's never hard to follow.
18. The secret is clarity. Finish the ends of your words. "Thank you," not "thank-yuh." "I'm going to," not "I'm gonna."
19. Turn your face toward the person you're speaking to, and keep your chin level, so your voice travels to them, not down to the table.
20. In a busy café, don't raise your voice. Lean in a little, slow down a little, and say each word fully.
21. And here's a simple exercise. Each morning, read two or three sentences of a book out loud, slowly, finishing every word. It takes one minute, and little by little, it becomes the way you speak.
22. Soft and clear will always be heard. Loud and rushed only gets noticed.

### 3. Opening a conversation

23. Opening a conversation.
24. So many of you asked us how to start a conversation, especially with someone you've just met.
25. Most of us reach for the weather, or the dreaded "So, what do you do?" and then hope for the best.
26. A lovely opener is simple. A small, warm question about the moment you're both in.
27. At a dinner, "How do you know the hosts?"
28. At a wedding, "What's been your favorite moment so far?"
29. At a class or an event, "What brought you here today?"
30. Each one is easy to answer, and it gives the other person something pleasant to talk about.
31. Then do the most elegant thing of all. Listen to the answer, and ask about one little detail they mentioned.
32. By the way, if you'd like to discover the colors, outfits and hairstyles that make you look and feel your best, your personal stylist guide is waiting in the description.

### 4. Holding your place, gracefully

33. Holding your place, gracefully.
34. It happens to all of us. You're halfway through a thought, and someone starts talking over you.
35. Most of us do one of two things. We go quiet and let the thought disappear, or we raise our voice and talk over them. Neither feels good.
36. There's a calmer way. Pause for a breath, look at them kindly, and say, "Let me just finish this thought."
37. Or, even softer, "One moment, I'd love to hear that, right after I finish."
38. Then finish your sentence, slowly, in the same calm voice.
39. And when you're done, give them the floor back. "Sorry, you were saying?"
40. You didn't fight for the room, and you didn't shrink in it. You simply kept your place.

### 5. Talking about yourself

41. Talking about yourself.
42. When someone asks what you do, notice the little word that sneaks in. "Just." "Oh, I'm just a stay-at-home mom." "It's just a little job."
43. That one word makes your life sound smaller, before anyone has even had the chance to be curious about it.
44. Instead, give one calm, true sentence. "I'm raising three children, and I love it."
45. "I work at a dental clinic. I look after the patients."
46. "I'm between jobs at the moment, and I'm enjoying the pause."
47. No apology, no long explanation. Just your life, said with quiet pride.
48. You don't have to sound impressive. You only have to sound like you respect your own story.

### 6. Introducing people

49. Introducing people.
50. One of the loveliest things you can do in a conversation is to bring someone else into it.
51. But a quick "This is Sophie" often leaves two people smiling at each other in silence.
52. A beautiful introduction has two parts. The name, and a little bridge.
53. "Clara, this is my friend Sophie. Sophie just came back from Lisbon, and Clara, you've always wanted to go."
54. Now they have their first sentence, and you've made both of them feel welcome.
55. Say each name clearly, look at the person you're speaking to, and smile as you say it. It takes ten seconds, and it's the kind of grace people feel without knowing why.

### And one more tiny thing

56. And one more tiny thing.
57. In our first video, we suggested "Say that again" instead of a sharp "What?" And one of you wrote that it can still feel a little direct.
58. You're right. If you didn't catch something, a soft "Pardon?" or "Sorry, I didn't quite catch that" is even gentler.
59. Small words, said slowly, carry the most grace.

### Close

60. These are small shifts, but together they change how people hear you, and how you hear yourself.
61. Which one will you try first? Tell us the number in the comments. We read every single one.
62. And if you'd like your personal stylist guide, sent instantly to your email, you'll find it in the description.
63. Thank you so much for spending your time with us today. A simple like or comment would truly mean the world to us.
64. Until next time, bye, my loves.
