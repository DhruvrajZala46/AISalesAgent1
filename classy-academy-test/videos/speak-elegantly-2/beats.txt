Speak clearly, not loudly.
After our first video, some of you told us something important. When you lowered your voice, people talked over you, or asked you to speak up.
That's because soft doesn't mean swallowed. A graceful voice is quiet, but it's never hard to follow.
The secret is clarity. Finish the ends of your words. "Thank you," not "thank-yuh." "I'm going to," not "I'm gonna."
Turn your face toward the person you're speaking to, and keep your chin level, so your voice travels to them, not down to the table.

Most of us reach for the weather, or the dreaded "So, what do you do?" and then hope for the best.
A lovely opener is simple. A small, warm question about the moment you're both in.
At a dinner, "How do you know the hosts?"
At a wedding, "What's been your favorite moment so far?"
At a class or an event, "What brought you here today?"
Each one is easy to answer, and it gives the other person something pleasant to talk about.

Holding your place, gracefully.
It happens to all of us. You're halfway through a thought, and someone starts talking over you.
Most of us do one of two things. We go quiet and let the thought disappear, or we raise our voice and talk over them. Neither feels good.
There's a calmer way. Pause for a breath, look at them kindly, and say, "Let me just finish this thought."
Or, even softer, "One moment, I'd love to hear that, right after I finish."
Then finish your sentence, slowly, in the same calm voice.
And when you're done, give them the floor back. "Sorry, you were saying?"
You didn't fight for the room, and you didn't shrink in it. You simply kept your place.
Talking about yourself.
When someone asks what you do, notice the little word that sneaks in. "Just." "Oh, I'm just a stay-at-home mom." "It's just a little job."
That one word makes your life sound smaller, before anyone has even had the chance to be curious about it.
Instead, give one calm, true sentence. "I'm raising three children, and I love it."
"I work at a dental clinic. I look after the patients."
"I'm between jobs at the moment, and I'm enjoying the pause."
No apology, no long explanation. Just your life, said with quiet pride.
You don't have to sound impressive. You only have to sound like you respect your own story.
Introducing people.
One of the loveliest things you can do in a conversation is to bring someone else into it.
But a quick "This is Sophie" often leaves two people smiling at each other in silence.
A beautiful introduction has two parts. The name, and a little bridge.
"Clara, this is my friend Sophie. Sophie just came back from Lisbon, and Clara, you've always wanted to go."
Now they have their first sentence, and you've made both of them feel welcome.
Say each name clearly, look at the person you're speaking to, and smile as you say it. It takes ten seconds, and it's the kind of grace people feel without knowing why.
