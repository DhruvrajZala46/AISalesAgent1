# 8 Ways to Dress for Your Body Shape — script

Voice: Kokoro af_heart, speed 0.80. Every spoken line is bold on its own line. Every beat has an
`[ON SCREEN]` line. **DO** = a woman following the tip, **DON'T** = the gentle "before" (never
mocking, never a real person's face held up as a bad example). **The same woman, or a woman of the
same size and age, in the DON'T and the DO** (competitor viewers: "Please use plus size women for
both the Don't and the Do", 124 likes). Real women of every shape, size, age (30s to 70s+) and skin
tone, including women over 50 with a tummy, truly petite women and plus-size women over 60 (our
viewers: "models too young / too thin", 39 comments, 1,119 likes). Real footage only, never AI.

## Hook

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in her 50s in a fitting room, turning to the mirror in a dress, unsure]
**Before you blame your body for one more outfit, stop.**

[ON SCREEN: row of three DO clips: a woman in her 60s in a navy wrap dress, a curvy woman in her 30s in high-rise wide trousers and a fitted knit, a petite woman in her 40s in one colour from top to toe]
**Which 8 ways to dress for your body shape make clothes look made for you, at any age?**

[ON SCREEN: white screen, "it's not your body. it's the cut."]
**There's no right or wrong body. Only clothes that are cut for it, or not.**

[ON SCREEN: close clip, an older woman helping a younger woman straighten a jacket in a mirror]
**If no one ever showed you how, we will today. Let's begin.**

## 1. Find your shape

[ON SCREEN: number card: "1. Find your shape"]
**Number one. Find your shape.**

[ON SCREEN: DON'T clip, hands flipping the size label inside a dress on a rail]
**It's easy to shop by size and hope for the best.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, two women of the same size side by side in the same simple dress, each fitting differently]
**But two women can wear the same size and have completely different shapes, and that's why the same dress looks so different on each of them.**

[ON SCREEN: text: "dress for your shape, not your size"]
**Elegant women don't dress for a size. They dress for their shape.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman in fitted clothes in front of a full-length mirror in daylight, hands at her shoulders, then her waist, then her hips]
**Try this now. In front of a full-length mirror, in daylight and fitted clothes, look at three widths only: your shoulders, your waist and your hips.**

[ON SCREEN: row of five real women, word-timed, each with a small label: "hips wider" / "shoulders wider" / "even, clear waist" / "even, straighter waist" / "fuller middle"]
**Hips wider than shoulders is often called a pear. Shoulders wider than hips, an inverted triangle. Even, with a clear waist, an hourglass. Even, with a straighter waist, a rectangle. And fuller through the middle, an apple.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman trying on a jacket and feeling the shoulders]
**Not sure? Notice where clothes feel tight first. And if you're a little of two, that's perfectly normal.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman smiling at her reflection, relaxed]
**These are only descriptions, nothing good or bad, and they can change over the years. Once you know yours, shopping stops being a guess, and you choose with calm confidence.**

## 2. Thirds, not halves

[ON SCREEN: number card: "2. Thirds, not halves"]
**Number two. Thirds, not halves.**

[ON SCREEN: DON'T clip (neck down), a long top ending mid-thigh over straight trousers]
**It's tempting to reach for a long top because it feels safe, and then wonder why the outfit looks heavy.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, the same woman, the same trousers, with a shorter fitted jacket ending at the hip bone; a thin line marks one third / two thirds]
**A top that ends mid-thigh divides you into two equal halves, so the eye stops right in the middle. The eye loves thirds: about one third on top, two thirds below.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman stacking both hands flat under her bust, looking down at them]
**Where that line falls depends on your torso. Here's a quick test. Place both hands flat, one above the other, right under your bust. If they cover your belly button, your torso is short. Right at the edge, it's balanced. And if your belly button sits below them, it's long.**

[ON SCREEN: pair: long torso, high-rise trousers with a tucked blouse | short torso, mid-rise trousers with a top ending just below the waist]
**A longer torso looks lovely with high-rise trousers and a tucked top or a shorter jacket. A shorter torso feels best in a mid rise, with tops that end at or just below the waist.**

[ON SCREEN: pair: DON'T low-rise jeans (neck down) | DO the same woman in mid-rise jeans]
**Either way, skip low rise. It cuts you in half at the widest part of the hips.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman with a full bust in an untucked top and a fitted jacket ending just above the hip bone]
**And if a tuck doesn't suit your bust or your tummy, a fitted jacket that ends just above the hip bone creates the same thirds.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman walking in high-rise trousers and a short jacket]
**Get the thirds right, and the whole outfit looks intentional, without changing a thing about you.**

## 3. Show your narrowest point

[ON SCREEN: number card: "3. Show your narrowest point"]
**Number three. Show your narrowest point.**

[ON SCREEN: DON'T clip, a woman in her 50s in a loose, shapeless tunic dress]
**When we don't love a part of our body, it's tempting to cover everything in loose clothes.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, the same woman, the same dress, now softly tied at the waist]
**But fabric that hangs straight down from your fullest point makes you look wider all over, because the eye can't find your shape.**

[ON SCREEN: text: "don't hide. highlight."]
**Elegant women don't hide. They let one narrow point show.**

[ON SCREEN: row, word-timed: "clear waist" a wrap dress | "fuller middle" an empire line just under the bust | "straighter waist" a peplum top and a structured jacket]
**Mark a clear waist with a classic wrap dress or a belted coat. If you're fuller through the middle, mark the narrow place just under your bust, with an empire line. And if your waist is straighter, let a peplum or a structured jacket create the shape, rather than a belt.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman in a lingerie shop with a fitter holding a tape measure (clothed, tasteful)]
**It starts underneath. A well-fitted bra, wired or soft, lifts and gives you back the space between your bust and your waist.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, hands threading a ribbon through two safety pins on the side seams of a dress turned inside out]
**And for a dress you already own, turn it inside out, pin a safety pin on each side seam at the waist, thread a soft ribbon through both, and tie it as close as you like. It costs nothing.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, the woman in the tied dress turning to the mirror, pleased]
**One clear point of shape, and you look at ease in your clothes instead of lost in them.**

## 4. Lead the eye to what you love

[ON SCREEN: number card: "4. Lead the eye to what you love"]
**Number four. Lead the eye to what you love.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, a woman with wider hips in a light printed blouse, earrings and dark straight trousers]
**Many of us were taught to hide the part we like least. But colour, pattern and detail draw the eye first, so wherever you place them, that's what people see.**

[ON SCREEN: text: "detail where you love. calm everywhere else."]
**So put the detail where you love, and keep everything else calm.**

[ON SCREEN: row, word-timed: "hips wider" print and light colour on top, darker trousers | "shoulders wider" a simple dark top, light wide trousers | "fuller middle" an open neckline, earrings, straight trousers]
**Wider hips? Wear the print, the lighter colour and the earrings on top, with darker trousers. Wider shoulders? Try the opposite: a simple top, with the colour and a wide leg below. Fuller through the middle? Bring the detail up to your face, with earrings or a scarf, because it draws the eye up.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman with broad shoulders in a halter top, confident]
**And if you love your shoulders or your hips, show them off, with confidence. These are choices, never rules.**

[ON SCREEN: text on caramel: "which shape are you closest to?"]
**Tell us in the comments, which shape are you closest to, or are you a blend?**

[ON SCREEN: panel, the stylist guide]
**And if you'd like to know which shapes, colours and fabrics flatter you most, our personal stylist guide is linked in the description.**

## 5. One colour, top to toe

[ON SCREEN: number card: "5. One colour, top to toe"]
**Number five. One colour, top to toe.**

[ON SCREEN: DON'T clip (neck down), a stark white blouse with black trousers]
**A crisp white blouse with black trousers is classic, and it can be beautiful.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, the same woman in a navy blouse, navy trousers and navy shoes]
**But a strong colour break at the waist or the hips draws a line straight across you, and that's exactly where the eye stops.**

[ON SCREEN: text: "one colour. one long line."]
**One colour, or close tones of one colour, creates one long line from top to toe.**

[ON SCREEN: row, word-timed: navy blouse + navy trousers | soft beige knit + beige trousers + nude shoes | cream with ivory]
**Try navy with navy, a soft beige knit with beige trousers, or cream with ivory, and mix textures, so it looks rich, never flat.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman in one colour putting on a long camel coat and walking with it open]
**Then add a long cardigan or coat, worn open, to frame the column. And the layer underneath still works when the coat comes off.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman in a white top and black trousers with a black-and-white scarf]
**And if you love contrast, keep it. Just add a jacket or a scarf that ties the two colours together.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in cream from top to toe walking calmly]
**It's timeless, it feels effortless and polished, and it's the easiest outfit you'll put together all week.**

## 6. Open the neckline

[ON SCREEN: number card: "6. Open the neckline"]
**Number six. Open the neckline.**

[ON SCREEN: DON'T clip, a woman with a full bust in a high crew-neck tee]
**It can feel safer to cover up with a high neck, especially with a full bust.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, the same woman in a soft V-neck knit]
**But a high, closed neck makes the upper body look shorter and fuller, and the eye goes to the chest instead of your face.**

[ON SCREEN: text: "a little open space brings the eye to your face"]
**A little open space at the neck brings the eye up to your face.**

[ON SCREEN: row, word-timed: V-neck | scoop | square neck | wrap]
**Choose a soft V, a scoop, a square neck or a wrap. A light V-neck knit or a classic wrap top suits almost every shape.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman in a crew-neck knit putting on a long pendant necklace]
**And if you prefer more coverage, keep your crew neck and add a long pendant necklace. It creates the same long V.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in a V-neck laughing with a friend at a café table]
**When people see your face first, you feel seen, and you carry yourself with quiet confidence.**

## 7. Fine knits, not chunky

[ON SCREEN: number card: "7. Fine knits, not chunky"]
**Number seven. Fine knits, not chunky ones.**

[ON SCREEN: DON'T clip, a woman in a thick cable-knit sweater]
**We get it. A chunky knit feels so cosy when the weather turns cold.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, the same woman in a fine V-neck knit]
**But thick cable knits, padding and fur add their own width on top of yours, often right where you're fullest.**

[ON SCREEN: text: "let the fabric skim your shape"]
**Let the fabric skim your shape, never add to it.**

[ON SCREEN: row, word-timed: a light cashmere knit | soft crepe | light wool trousers | fine linen]
**Choose fine knits, like a light cashmere or a thin wool, and fabrics that drape, like crepe and fine linen. Before you buy, hold it, move it, and see how it falls.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman in a fine knit under a long wool coat on a cold street]
**On a really cold day, warmth comes first. A fine knit under a warm coat keeps you cosy without the bulk.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman with wider hips in a chunky cream knit with slim dark trousers]
**And if your hips are wider than your shoulders, a chunky knit on top can be lovely, because it adds volume where you're narrower.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, a woman in a fine knit moving easily]
**Soft fabric moves with you, so you feel comfortable and graceful from morning to night.**

## 8. Shoes that continue the line

[ON SCREEN: number card: "8. Shoes that continue the line"]
**Number eight. Shoes that continue the line.**

[ON SCREEN: DON'T clip, a knee-length skirt with heavy black ankle-strap sandals (legs only)]
**It's easy to choose shoes last.**

[ON SCREEN: DO clip, the same skirt with flats close to her skin tone]
**But a shoe in strong contrast, or a strap across the ankle, ends the leg in one hard stop, and the eye drops to your feet.**

[ON SCREEN: text: "continue the line. don't stop it."]
**The shoe should continue the line, not stop it.**

[ON SCREEN: pair: navy trousers + navy loafers | a dress + flats close to her skin tone]
**With trousers, choose the same colour family, navy with navy, black with black. With skirts and dresses, a shade close to your skin tone feels like part of the leg.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, a woman in her 70s walking in pointed-toe flats]
**And you don't need heels. A pointed-toe flat, a low block heel or a sleek loafer continues the line just as well.**

[ON SCREEN: single clip, comfortable wide-strap sandals in a shade close to her skin tone]
**If your feet need a wider strap or a rounder toe, comfort comes first. Choose it in your skin tone or your trouser colour, and let the colour do the work.**

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, feet in skin-tone flats walking, then the whole woman walking away]
**When nothing stops the eye, you look finished from head to toe, and you walk with ease.**

## How the competitor study shaped the writing

From the 14 winners and their 463 most-liked comments (verified):
- **The lens is absolution, never correction.** The most-liked comment of the whole study praises
  careful, neutral words about bodies ("Bodies just are", 9,900); Libby's 1.6M video opens on "blaming
  your body"; Desi: "We're not here to change your body." So: "it's not your body, it's the cut",
  "only descriptions, nothing good or bad", "choices, never rules". The words "slimmer", "thinner",
  "taller", "flaws", "hide" and "problem areas" never appear as goals ("Why is everyone obsessed with
  trying to look taller", 190; "I don't like the thought of 'looking slimmer'", 8).
- **Tests she can do while watching** drew "I just did it" comments (hand test 924, safety pin 140), so
  ways 1, 2, 3 and 7 each carry one (mirror, hands under the bust, safety pins, hold and move).
- **Every way has its exception**, taken from the top pushback (short torso, full bust, no waist, no
  heels, swollen feet, more coverage, the cold, loving a feature).
- **Models that don't match the viewer** are named in the comments of 8 of the 14 winners (317, 155,
  124, 105, 92, 65, 59, 58): same size in DO and DON'T, women over 50 with a tummy, truly petite, plus size over 60.
- **Mid-video question** "which shape are you closest to, or are you a blend?" (Libby asks it at 03:47;
  viewers naming their shape or blend drew 1,900, 1,200, 1,093 over 9 comments, and 670).
- **The close asks her to pass on what works on her shape** (Libby asks for it; viewers' own tips
  drew 615, 631, 123, 89 likes).
