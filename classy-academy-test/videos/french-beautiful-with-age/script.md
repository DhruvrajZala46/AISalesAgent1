## Chapter 1: Younger, or more beautiful?

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman in her sixties at her mirror, fingertips on the lines beside her eyes; cut to a silver-haired woman laughing at a café table]

**Let me ask you an honest question. What has trying to look younger cost you, and has it ever once made you feel more beautiful?**

**Most of us are taught to fight our age. Almost no one is taught to grow into it. And that's why so many women spend years chasing the face they had at thirty, and never enjoy the one they have today.**

[ON SCREEN: a woman of about seventy, silver hair, navy coat, walking into a restaurant; a friend stands up to greet her]

**Meanwhile, there's always that one woman. She's sixty, maybe seventy. Silver in her hair. Lines around her eyes. And somehow, she's the most beautiful woman in the room, because she walks in with a calm that no cream can give. You know exactly who I mean.**

[ON SCREEN: the sentence on the caramel backdrop: "How do I look more like myself?"]

**So the real question is not, "How do I look younger?" The real question is, "How do I look more like myself?" Because that's what ageing beautifully actually is. It isn't a cream. It isn't a procedure. It's a handful of quiet choices, made every day. And almost nobody teaches this.**

[ON SCREEN: the title in black script on a white card: "French Women Get More Beautiful With Age: 7 Rules"]

**So today, I'm going to break down why French women so often seem to get more beautiful with age, in a way that's practical, a little surprising, and possible on any budget. By the end, you'll understand exactly why some women glow at sixty and seventy, and how to become one of them, without pretending to be thirty. Most of it, you already own.**

**Stay with me until the end, because I'll share seven rules. And the last one is the one that changes how you walk into any room. And if you'd like to go deeper after this, our personal stylist guide is waiting for you in the description. Let's get into it.**

## Chapter 2: What ageing beautifully really means

[ON SCREEN: a woman pulling her cheeks back in a mirror, then letting go and smiling; then a woman in her sixties reading in a chair by a window, completely at ease]

**Let's clear this up immediately. Ageing beautifully isn't looking ten years younger. That's a disguise. Real beauty, at any age, is timeless. It's when your face, your clothes and your manner all say the same thing, with quiet confidence. "I know who I am."**

**Think about it. You don't remember the woman who looked young for her age. You remember the woman who looked completely at ease in hers, because ease makes everyone around her feel calm too.**

[ON SCREEN: three photographs side by side on the caramel: Inès de la Fressange, Mireille Guiliano, Caroline de Maigret, each with her name]

**Three French women have spoken about this more honestly than almost anyone. And when you put their words side by side, the same seven rules appear.**

## Chapter 3: Rule 1. Accept your age first

[ON SCREEN: white card, black script: "1. Accept your age first"]

**The first rule. Accept your age first. Most of us begin the morning with a small battle in the mirror. A new line. A grey hair. And the day starts with disappointment, because we're looking for what's gone.**

[ON SCREEN: photographs and interview film of Inès de la Fressange today, silver-streaked hair, laughing]

**Inès de la Fressange is a French model and designer. She's sixty-nine now. And when a journalist asked for her secret to ageing gracefully, she didn't name a cream. She said, "I think it's to accept that you are ageing."**

[ON SCREEN: a woman at her mirror in the morning, smiling at her own reflection; close on her eyes, then her hands]

**Here's the truth. You can't look relaxed in a face you're at war with, because tension shows. And so does grace. So tomorrow morning, try this. Before you look for what has changed, find one thing you still love. Your eyes. Your smile. The shape of your hands. Start there.**

## Chapter 4: Rule 2. Care for your skin before you cover it

[ON SCREEN: white card: "2. Care for your skin before you cover it"]

**Now, the second rule. Care for your skin before you cover it. Many of us were taught to hide. One more layer of foundation. One more layer of powder.**

[ON SCREEN: a woman washing her face at a basin in the evening; then smoothing on a day cream; then a wide-brimmed hat in the sun]

**Women who age beautifully do the opposite. Inès de la Fressange keeps her essentials very simple. Cleaning her face, eating well, protecting her skin from the sun, and putting on day cream. And the author Mireille Guiliano put it this way: "I'd rather have one facial a year than six manicures."**

[ON SCREEN: the three habits on cream, one by one: "Clean, every night" "Day cream, every morning" "A hat in strong sun"]

**Here's the truth. Makeup sits on your skin, so it can only look as lovely as what's underneath. So instead of buying another foundation, try three small habits. Clean your face every single night, however tired you are. A simple day cream every morning. And a hat when the sun is strong. None of this needs to be expensive. It only needs to be every day.**

## Chapter 5: Rule 3. Soften everything

[ON SCREEN: white card: "3. Soften everything"]

**Rule number three. Soften everything. The makeup that suited you at thirty-five may not be the makeup that suits you now. And that isn't a loss. It's simply a new face to dress.**

[ON SCREEN: photographs of Caroline de Maigret, soft smudged eye, dark hair]

**Caroline de Maigret, the French model and writer, said it with real honesty. "Everything should be a bit blurry, because blurry make-up goes with a blurry face." What she means is kind. Soft edges flatter a softer face.**

[ON SCREEN: close-ups: a fingertip smudging soft brown along the lash line; cream blush dabbed high on the cheek; a rose lip colour pressed on with a finger; a woman smoothing her hair into a low twist]

**So instead of a hard black line around the eye, try a soft brown, smudged with your fingertip. Instead of heavy powder, a little cream blush, high on the cheek. Instead of a dark, sharp lip line, a rose or a soft berry, pressed on with a finger. And keep your hair a little tidier than you did at twenty. Soft face, soft makeup, polished hair. That harmony is what looks beautiful.**

## Chapter 6: Rule 4. Never freeze your style

[ON SCREEN: white card: "4. Never freeze your style"]

**The fourth rule surprises people. Never freeze your style. Most of us find a look in our thirties, and keep it. The same cut. The same shapes. It feels safe.**

[ON SCREEN: photographs of Inès de la Fressange across the years: the 1980s, then today in a navy jacket]

**Inès de la Fressange believes the opposite. "The worst thing a woman can do when getting older is try to keep her style the same."**

[ON SCREEN: close-ups of a sleeve, a row of buttons, a collar; then the words on the caramel: "Not a nun. Not a teenager."]

**But she doesn't mean chasing every trend. Timeless style still moves, just a little. In her words, "The size of a sleeve or type of buttons can be all it takes to make something feel chic for now." And at fifty or sixty, "you don't want to look like a nun, but you don't want to look like a teenager either."**

[ON SCREEN: a woman at her open wardrobe, swapping one pair of trousers for a slightly wider pair; trying on new glasses and smiling]

**So once a year, stand in front of your wardrobe, and change one small thing. A slightly wider trouser. A softer shoulder. A new pair of glasses. Not a new you. Just this year's you.**

[ON SCREEN: a silver-haired woman walking along a river in a camel coat, unhurried]

**If you're still here, you already think about beauty more deeply than most people. Most people click away the moment a video asks them to think. So let's go a little deeper.**

## Chapter 7: Rule 5. Let them notice you, not the dress

[ON SCREEN: white card: "5. Let them notice you, not the dress"]

**Rule number five. Let them notice you, not the dress.**

[ON SCREEN: photographs of Inès de la Fressange in white jeans and a navy sweater]

**Inès wears almost the same classic pieces every day. "My daily uniform is white jeans and a navy sweater. I don't wear too many colours together."**

[ON SCREEN: the sentence on the caramel: "You look great." not "That's a nice dress."]

**And then she gives the reason. "The clothes are there to make you look better, so that someone says, 'You look great', not 'That's a nice dress'."**

[ON SCREEN: three outfits in a row with script labels: "navy + cream" "camel + white" "grey + soft blue"; then one pair of gold earrings on a plain neckline]

**Here's the truth. As the years go by, our faces carry more story, and too much around them takes the attention away. So try this. Two colours, three at most. Navy and cream. Camel and white. Grey and soft blue. You probably already own them. One piece of jewellery you love, instead of five. And then let your face do the talking.**

[ON SCREEN: the question on the caramel: "When did someone last say, 'You look great'?"]

**So I'm curious. Be honest. When did someone last say to you, "You look great", and what were you wearing? Tell me in the comments. I really want to know.**

## Chapter 9: Rule 6. Choose ease

[ON SCREEN: white card: "6. Choose ease"]

**The sixth rule. Choose ease. Inès de la Fressange puts it in one sentence. "You cannot be chic if you are not comfortable."**

[ON SCREEN: photographs of Inès de la Fressange in flat shoes; close on black patent ballerina flats]

**She doesn't wear high heels out of duty. Heels, in her words, "shouldn't be worn systematically." And for shorter women who feel they must wear them? "They should rather buy a ladder!" Her own rescue, many times, has been a pair of shiny black ballerina flats.**

[ON SCREEN: a woman in a fitting room choosing the looser of two jackets, turning, pleased]

**Caroline de Maigret adds one more trick. When she still wants a certain look, "the best thing to do is to take two sizes up."**

[ON SCREEN: a woman walking easily in low block heels and soft wide trousers; then sitting down gracefully]

**Here's the truth. Discomfort shows on your face long before anyone looks at your shoes, because a woman who is pulling at her waistband, or aching in her heels, can't feel calm. So instead of the tight version, try the one that lets you breathe. Instead of the highest heel, a low block heel or a classic flat. Ease isn't giving up. Ease is what grace looks like when it's lived in.**

## Chapter 10: Rule 7. Stop chasing perfect

[ON SCREEN: white card: "7. Stop chasing perfect"]

**And finally, the seventh rule. The one that changes how you walk into any room. Stop chasing perfect.**

[ON SCREEN: photographs of Caroline de Maigret in profile, her strong nose, laughing]

**Caroline de Maigret puts it beautifully. "We kind of gave up a long time ago on the utopia of being perfect." When the idea of changing her nose came up, she refused. And today? "I'm really proud of that nose that stands tall and high in the middle of my face."**

[ON SCREEN: interview film or photographs of Mireille Guiliano smiling; then a woman in her sixties with a silver streak, laughing with friends]

**And Mireille Guiliano names what you gain in return. At this age, "you know yourself and you have a confidence that you don't have in your twenties."**

[ON SCREEN: close-ups of real women: laugh lines, a silver streak, a strong profile; then a woman walking into a dinner, head high]

**Here's the truth. The feature you've spent years hiding is very often the one people remember you by, because it's the one that makes you look like no one else. So choose one. The nose. The silver streak. The laugh lines. And for one week, stop covering it. Wear what feels closest to who you are. Because, as Caroline de Maigret puts it, "then when you enter a room, half the work is already done."**

## Chapter 11: Your own way of ageing

[ON SCREEN: the seven rules in black script on cream, one by one]

**So let's bring it all together. Ageing beautifully isn't a secret that belongs to one country. It's a decision, made with intention. To accept your age. To care for your skin. To soften. To keep your style moving. To let them notice you. To choose ease. And to stop chasing perfect.**

[ON SCREEN: recap, three lines on cream: "One thing you love." "Take one thing away." "Let it be seen."]

**So here's what I want you to do after this video. Tomorrow morning, find one thing in the mirror that you love, before anything else. This week, take one thing away. One layer of makeup, one colour, or one accessory. And choose the one feature you've been hiding, and let it be seen.**

**If this changed the way you think about getting older, tell me in the comments, which of the seven rules will you try first? I read every comment. And the personal stylist guide is waiting for you in the description.**

[ON SCREEN: a silver-haired woman turning to the camera and smiling; the end card with the channel's logo]

**And remember, there's a line Inès de la Fressange likes to quote. Ageing is becoming who you always should have been. So don't ask the mirror for the woman you were. Let it show you the woman you've become. Thank you so much for spending your time with us today. Until next time. Bye, my loves.**
