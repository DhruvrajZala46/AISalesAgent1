# 8 Things Elegant Women Never Say — script

Voice: Kokoro af_heart, speed 0.80. Every spoken line is bold on its own line. Every beat has an `[ON SCREEN]` line.
The edit: real women in real conversations (cafés, dinner tables, friends walking, a phone call), and for every point a
**swap card** beside her: ✗ the everyday phrase → ✓ what to say instead, building into a list she can screenshot
(the loved winners' viewers wrote these lists out themselves). Women of every age, 30s to 70s+, many backgrounds.
Never a real person's face held up as a bad example: the ✗ is always on the words, never on a woman.

## Hook

[ON SCREEN: full-screen clip, two women at a café table, one talking, the other nodding and about to cut in]
**Before you reply "whatever" or "me too" again, stop.**

[ON SCREEN: close on a woman in her 60s listening, a small smile; words appear softly: "whatever" · "me too" · "I know"]
**Which of these eight phrases do you say without noticing?**

[ON SCREEN: three quick real moments: a dinner table laughing, two friends walking arm in arm, a woman on the phone by a window]
**We all say them. They feel harmless, even friendly. But each one quietly changes how people feel around you, and how you feel too.**

[ON SCREEN: a woman in her 40s pouring tea for a friend, unhurried]
**In today's video, we're sharing eight everyday phrases elegant women gently replace, and exactly what to say instead.**

**Some of these may surprise you, but once you hear them, you'll never unhear them. Let's get started.**

## 1. "Can I get…"

[ON SCREEN: number card "1. Polite words"]
**Number one. "Can I get…"**

[ON SCREEN: a woman at a café counter ordering, the barista smiling]
**We get it. "Can I get a coffee?" is what almost everyone says, and it's perfectly friendly.**

[ON SCREEN: swap card builds: ✗ "Can I get…" → ✓ "May I have…, please"]
**But a few small, polite words change the whole feeling of a moment, because they make the other person feel respected. They sound softer, calmer, and a little more gracious.**

[ON SCREEN: a woman in her 50s at a restaurant, looking up at the waiter with a warm smile]
**Instead of "Can I get the salmon?", try "I'd like the salmon, please." Instead of "Can I get a table by the window?", try "May I have a table by the window, please?"**

[ON SCREEN: swap card adds: ✗ "You're welcome" → ✓ "My pleasure"]
**And when someone thanks you, "you're welcome" is lovely. But "it's my pleasure" makes it feel like you were truly happy to help.**

[ON SCREEN: a woman in her 70s handing a friend a wrapped gift, both smiling]
**Words change from country to country, so use the ones that feel natural where you live. It's not about sounding formal. It's about sounding kind.**

**Gracious words cost nothing. They're timeless, like a classic navy blazer or a cream silk blouse, and they quietly show respect, for others and for yourself.**

## 2. "Me too!"

[ON SCREEN: number card "2. Me too"]
**Number two. "Me too!"**

[ON SCREEN: two friends on a sofa with coffee, one looking tired, rubbing her eyes]
**We get it. A friend tells you, "I'm so tired this week." And before she's finished, we reply, "Me too! I barely slept."**

[ON SCREEN: close on the tired friend, her smile fading slightly as she looks down at her cup]
**We say it to connect, because it feels warm. But without meaning to, it makes the moment about us, just as she was starting to open up.**

[ON SCREEN: swap card builds: ✗ "Me too!" → ✓ "That sounds exhausting. What's been going on?"]
**Elegant women keep the focus on her for one more moment. Instead of "me too", try "That sounds exhausting. What's been going on?"**

[ON SCREEN: the same friend now talking, hands moving, the other leaning in and listening]
**One more question. That's all it takes for someone to feel truly heard, and it makes you the calm, generous presence people love to talk to.**

[ON SCREEN: both laughing together]
**And your own story? Tell it. That's how friendships grow. Just let hers finish first.**

**Listening isn't the quiet part of a conversation. It's the most graceful part.**

## 3. "I know."

[ON SCREEN: number card "3. I know"]
**Number three. "I know."**

[ON SCREEN: a woman excitedly showing her friend something on her phone at a kitchen table]
**We've all been there. Someone tells you about something they're excited about, a story, a tip, a little discovery. And we reply, "I know," or "Oh, I heard that already."**

[ON SCREEN: the friend's face, the excitement softening]
**It's true, and it's quick. But it makes her feel she has nothing new to offer, and it closes the door on a moment she wanted to enjoy with you.**

[ON SCREEN: swap card builds: ✗ "I know." → ✓ "That's interesting. What did you think of it?"]
**Instead, try "That's interesting. What did you think of it?" Or "When I first heard that, it surprised me too."**

[ON SCREEN: the two women laughing over the phone screen together]
**You don't have to pretend you've never heard it. You simply let her enjoy telling you, because her joy matters more than being first.**

**It's not about who knows more. It's about how she feels after talking to you: at ease, respected, and listened to.**

## 4. "Oh yes, of course."

[ON SCREEN: number card "4. Of course"]
**Number four. "Oh yes, of course," when you're not sure what they mean.**

[ON SCREEN: a dinner party, a guest talking about a book; a woman in her 40s nodding along politely]
**We've all done it. Someone mentions a film, a place or a new restaurant, and we nod along so we don't feel left out.**

[ON SCREEN: the nodding woman glancing away, a little uneasy]
**But then the next question comes, and we're quietly hoping no one asks what we thought. It feels uncomfortable, and it looks a little unsure.**

[ON SCREEN: swap card builds: ✗ "Oh yes, of course." → ✓ "I haven't. Tell me about it."]
**Elegant women are comfortable not knowing, and that ease is exactly what makes them look so confident. Instead of pretending, try "I haven't seen it yet. Tell me about it."**

[ON SCREEN: the guest lighting up as she explains, the woman listening, chin on her hand]
**It's honest, it's warm, and it gives the other person the joy of sharing something they love.**

**Curiosity is always more graceful than pretending.**

[ON SCREEN: soft caramel card, "Your Personal Stylist Guide", a woman in a cream coat walking]
**And if you'd like to know which colours, shapes and pieces flatter you most, our personal stylist guide is linked in the description.**

[ON SCREEN: words appear: "me too" · "I know" · "of course" — "which one is yours?"]
**Before we continue, which of these phrases do you catch yourself saying most? Tell us in the comments. We all have one.**

## 5. "Whatever."

[ON SCREEN: number card "5. Whatever"]
**Number five. "Whatever," or "I don't mind, whatever you want."**

[ON SCREEN: two women looking at a menu or a map, one shrugging gently]
**We get it. We often say it to be easy-going. Someone asks where we'd like to have dinner, and we reply, "I don't mind."**

[ON SCREEN: close on her, smiling, but you can tell she has a quiet wish]
**But when we always hide our own wishes, it can make us feel invisible, because people slowly stop asking for them.**

[ON SCREEN: swap card builds: ✗ "Whatever." → ✓ "I'd love the little bistro on the corner, if that suits you."]
**Elegant women tend to offer their preference gently. Try "I'd love the little bistro on the corner, if that suits you."**

[ON SCREEN: swap card adds: ✗ "I don't care." → ✓ "That's not really my preference."]
**And when something isn't for you, instead of "I don't care" or "whatever", try "That's not really my preference, but thank you."**

[ON SCREEN: the two women walking into a warm restaurant, laughing]
**If you truly don't mind, say it warmly and mean it. Both sound lovely.**

**Knowing what you like, and saying it kindly, is a quiet form of self-respect and confidence.**

## 6. "Calm down."

[ON SCREEN: number card "6. Calm down"]
**Number six. "Calm down."**

[ON SCREEN: a tense moment, two women at a kitchen counter, one upset, hands on her forehead]
**It's tempting. When someone is upset, "calm down" feels like the obvious thing to say. But have you ever been told it yourself?**

[ON SCREEN: the upset woman turning away]
**It almost never calms anyone, because it makes them feel their feelings are too much.**

[ON SCREEN: swap card builds: ✗ "Calm down." → ✓ "Let's take a moment."]
**Instead, try "Let's take a moment," or "Let's pause, and come back to this in a bit."**

[ON SCREEN: swap card adds: ✗ "You made me feel…" → ✓ "When that happened, I felt…"]
**And when you're the one who's hurt, instead of "You made me feel ignored", try "When that happened, I felt a little ignored." It shares your feeling without turning it into blame.**

[ON SCREEN: the two women sitting down together, one hand resting on the other's arm]
**Feeling frustrated is completely normal. Elegant women simply wait until the first wave passes before they speak, and they never need to raise their voice.**

**A calm voice in a tense moment is one of the most elegant things you can offer. It creates a little space where everyone can breathe.**

## 7. Speaking unkindly about another woman

[ON SCREEN: number card "7. About another woman"]
**Number seven. Speaking unkindly about another woman.**

[ON SCREEN: three friends over a glass of wine, two leaning in, whispering]
**It can feel like bonding. Everyone's leaning in, someone mentions another woman's outfit, her marriage, her choices.**

[ON SCREEN: the third friend sitting back slightly, uneasy]
**But those little comments always leave a mark, because they make everyone wonder what we'd whisper about them when they leave the room.**

[ON SCREEN: swap card builds: ✗ "Did you see what she wore?" → ✓ "I wouldn't know. How was your trip, by the way?"]
**Elegant women gently step out of it. Try "Oh, I wouldn't know about that," and then turn the conversation somewhere lovely. "How was your trip, by the way?"**

[ON SCREEN: a woman in her 60s hugging a friend who looks sad]
**And when a friend is hurting after a breakup, skip "I never liked him anyway." Try "I'm here. How are you holding up?"**

[ON SCREEN: an older woman and a younger woman, mother and daughter, walking together]
**Many of us were taught by our mothers and grandmothers: if you can't say something kind, it's better to say nothing at all. It's timeless advice, and it's still the most elegant.**

**The way you speak about others is the reason people trust you. That quiet grace is felt long after you've left.**

## 8. Speaking unkindly about yourself

[ON SCREEN: number card "8. About yourself"]
**And number eight. Speaking unkindly about yourself.**

[ON SCREEN: a woman in her 50s receiving a compliment from a friend and waving it away, laughing]
**"Oh, I look awful today." "I'm hopeless with names." "I'm too old for that."**

[ON SCREEN: close on her, the laugh fading into something a little smaller]
**We get it. We say these things as a joke, or to seem humble. But people listen, and so do we, because the words we repeat become the way we feel.**

[ON SCREEN: swap card builds: ✗ "I look awful today." → ✓ "Thank you."]
**When someone compliments you, let it land. A simple "Thank you" is enough, and it looks so much more confident than brushing it away.**

[ON SCREEN: swap card adds: ✗ "I'm hopeless at this." → ✓ "I'm still learning."]
**And instead of "I'm hopeless at this", try "I'm still learning." Instead of "I'm too old for that", try "I'd love to try that."**

[ON SCREEN: a woman in her 70s laughing on a dance floor, a woman in her 60s trying on a coat in a mirror, smiling]
**This isn't about pretending you feel wonderful every day. It's about speaking to yourself the way you'd speak to a friend you love.**

**The kindest words you'll ever speak are the ones about yourself. That's where real confidence begins.**

## Recap

[ON SCREEN: the full swap card list, each line lighting up as it's said]
**So, the eight phrases, and the classic, gentle words to say instead.**

**"Can I get" becomes "May I have, please."**

**"Me too" becomes one more question.**

**"I know" becomes "What did you think of it?"**

**"Of course" becomes "Tell me about it."**

**"Whatever" becomes your gentle preference.**

**"Calm down" becomes "Let's take a moment."**

**Unkind words about others become kind silence.**

**And unkind words about yourself become "Thank you."**
