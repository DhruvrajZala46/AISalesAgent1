**Now, this one surprises people.**

[ON SCREEN: press photographs, polo, June 1981: Diana in the red jumper with rows of white sheep and one black sheep,

**In June 1981, weeks before her wedding, Diana watched a polo match in a bright red jumper, knitted with rows of white sheep, and one black sheep.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: hands knitting red wool; then a market stall of jumpers]
**It came from Warm and Wonderful, a tiny knitwear label started by Sally Muir and Joanna Osborne, who sold their jumpers from a market stall in Covent Garden.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of the jumper on display at Sotheby's New York, September 2023; the label "Sotheby's, New York,

**In 2023, the original sold at Sotheby's in New York for more than a million dollars. And the same design is still knitted today.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip: a woman holding up a cheerful patterned jumper, then folding it back into a drawer]
**You know that feeling when a piece makes you smile, but it stays in the drawer, because it doesn't feel serious enough?**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman in a red patterned jumper with a white collar, navy trousers and flat shoes, walking

**Elegant women make room for one playful piece. Just one, in a classic shape, with everything around it calm. Because when the rest of your outfit is quiet, the playful piece looks intentional, not busy. Try it this weekend: a red jumper with navy trousers, a white collar at the neck, simple flat shoes.**

**Elegance doesn't ask you to stop having fun. It simply chooses where the fun goes.**

## 3. Soft Brown, Not Blue

[ON SCREEN: white card: "3. Soft Brown, Not Blue"]
**Here's something subtle.**

[ON SCREEN: archive photographs of Diana in the 1980s with bright blue liner inside the lower lash line]
**For years, Diana's signature was bright blue eyeliner. Then, in 1991, she sat for a British Vogue shoot with the make-up artist Mary Greenwell. And Mary gently took the blue away.**

[ON SCREEN: the sentence on the caramel: "Blue eyes should never wear blue pencil or shadow." with "Mary Greenwell, make-up
artist" beneath]
**Her reason was simple. Blue eyes should never wear blue pencil or shadow. Beiges and browns, she said, are just so much prettier. And Diana loved the change.**

[ON SCREEN: photograph of Diana in the 1990s with soft brown eyes and full lashes]
**What Diana kept was the mascara. Mary has said she liked loads of it.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: a woman at her mirror drawing a bright coloured line under her eye]
**Most of us match our make-up to our eyes, or keep doing it the way we did at twenty. I know I have.**

[ON SCREEN: close-ups: a fingertip smudging a soft brown pencil along the upper lashes; one coat of black mascara]
**Here's how to try it tomorrow morning. Instead of a bright liner, take a soft brown or taupe pencil. Smudge it close to your upper lashes with your fingertip. Then let one good coat of mascara do the work. It costs very little, and you may already own both. Because a liner the same colour as your eyes competes with them, while a soft brown makes them look clearer and brighter. It looks polished, never overdone.**

**Sometimes the most elegant change is simply taking one thing away.**

## 4. One Shoe, Three Colours

[ON SCREEN: white card: "4. One Shoe, Three Colours"]

[ON SCREEN: photographs of Diana in the 1990s in flat suede driving loafers; then product photographs of the Tod's

**In the nineties, Diana loved soft Italian driving loafers, the Tod's Gommino, with little rubber pebbles on the sole. And she didn't own just one pair. She had them in beige suede, in light blue suede, and in tan leather. They're still made today.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: a woman surrounded by shoe boxes, trying on and taking off]
**You know that endless search for the perfect pair of shoes? Buying, returning, buying again. Most of us know it well.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman lining up the same flat loafer in two colours by her door]
**Elegant women tend to stop searching once they've found what works. When a shoe is comfortable, flattering and easy to wear, they simply get it in a second colour. That isn't boring. That's knowing yourself. And every decision you don't have to make in the morning leaves more room for ease.**

[ON SCREEN: a row of three women in flat loafers: with jeans, with a midi skirt, with tailored trousers]
**You don't need the original. Choose a soft flat loafer in suede or leather, in a neutral like beige, navy or tan, so it goes with what you already own: jeans, a skirt, tailored trousers.**

**Fewer decisions, more ease. And that ease shows in the way you walk, with quiet confidence.**

## 5. The Everyday Tote

[ON SCREEN: white card: "5. The Everyday Tote"]
**Here's one you've probably seen without noticing.**

[ON SCREEN: photographs of Diana in London in the early 1990s carrying a soft tote with two curved bamboo handles]
**In the early nineties, Diana carried a soft Gucci tote with two curved bamboo handles all around London. Gucci had introduced it in 1991. And thirty years later, for what would have been her sixtieth birthday, Gucci renamed it the Diana.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: a woman at an open wardrobe shelf of many bags, unsure which to take]
**Many of us own a bag for every outfit, and still feel that nothing quite works.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman with one tan leather tote, first with jeans in the morning, then with a dress at

**Elegant women tend to rely on one good everyday bag. A classic shape, a warm neutral, no big logo, and big enough for real life. Because a calm, neutral bag goes with everything you already own, so your outfit always looks finished. It works with jeans in the morning and a dress in the evening. Look for a structured tote in tan, black or cream. You'll find one at any price.**

**When your bag never needs a second thought, you're free to think about everything else.**

## 6. The Bag She Didn't Save

[ON SCREEN: white card: "6. The Bag She Didn't Save"]
**And then, there's the gift.**

[ON SCREEN: press photographs, Paris, September 1995: Diana at the Grand Palais with Bernadette Chirac; the label

**In September 1995, Diana was in Paris for a Cézanne exhibition, and the wife of the French President, Bernadette Chirac, gave her a new Dior handbag. A small, structured bag with a top handle. Its nickname was Chouchou, which means favourite.**

[ON SCREEN: photographs of Diana carrying the black bag on her visits; then at the Met Gala, New York, December 1996]
**Diana didn't put it away for special occasions. Within two months, she was carrying it on her visits. In 1996, she took it to the Met Gala in New York. And that same year, Dior renamed it in her honour, the Lady Dior. It's still made today.**

[ON SCREEN: framed clip on caramel: a handbag going back into its dust bag; a silk scarf folded away in tissue]
**Many women save their most beautiful things for a special day. The good bag stays in its dust bag. The silk scarf stays in the drawer.**

[ON SCREEN: full screen: a woman carrying her best handbag on an ordinary weekday morning, a silk scarf at her neck,
buying flowers]
**Elegant women use what they love. Not carelessly, but often. Because when you use your beautiful things, you carry yourself differently. You feel more polished, and it shows. So start this week. Your nicest bag on a Tuesday. Your good scarf to the grocery store. Even a pretty handbag you already own, finally taken out of the wardrobe.**

**Because the special day you're saving them for is usually today.**

[ON SCREEN: a bottle of First by Van Cleef & Arpels on a dresser, soft morning light; the sentence beside it: "a bottle

[ON SCREEN: archive photograph of Diana laughing; the payoff written over it]
**Because the things you love, worn often, become the way people remember you.**
