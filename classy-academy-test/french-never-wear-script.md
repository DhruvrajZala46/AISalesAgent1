### 1. Heels you can't walk in
**Number one. Heels you can't walk in.**
[ON SCREEN: white number card "1. Heels you can't walk in", then a full-screen close-up of stilettos on cobblestones]

**We get it. Sky-high heels can feel glamorous, and they look beautiful sitting in the closet.**
[ON SCREEN: a pair of stilettos on a closet shelf, soft light]

**But Paris is a walking city. Cobblestone streets, metro stairs, long walks to the market. And nothing looks less elegant than struggling in your shoes.**
[ON SCREEN: row: cobblestone street, metro stairs, a woman walking briskly in flats]

**Inès de la Fressange, the French model and style icon who wrote a best-selling book on Parisian style, favours flats over heels.**
[ON SCREEN: script "Inès de la Fressange", then the book title "Parisian Chic" on the caramel backdrop]

**Elegance is a balance of comfort and structure. When your feet hurt, it shows in your face, your posture and your walk.**
[ON SCREEN: pair: a woman wincing on high heels | the same calm stride in flats]

**Instead of stilettos, elegant French women tend to reach for ballet flats, loafers, or a low kitten heel or block heel when they want a little height.**
[ON SCREEN: cut-outs float in one per item: ballet flats, loafers, kitten heel, block heel]

**A pair of navy ballet flats or camel loafers is a timeless classic. It goes with almost everything, and you may already own one.**
[ON SCREEN: macro of navy ballet flats, then camel loafers stepping out of a doorway]

**When you can walk with ease, you carry yourself with ease.**
[ON SCREEN: full-screen walk in loafers, script line "walk with ease"]

### 2. Gym clothes as an everyday outfit
**Number two. Gym clothes as an everyday outfit.**
[ON SCREEN: number card, then a woman in leggings and a hoodie with a coffee]

**Leggings and sweatpants are wonderful for the gym, or a slow Sunday at home.**
[ON SCREEN: pair: yoga class | cosy Sunday at home]

**But in France, most women don't wear them out and about, except for a quick errand on the way home from the gym. French style means making a small effort, even for errands.**
[ON SCREEN: centred clip, a woman doing errands at a market in a knit and trousers]

**It's not about dressing up. It's about looking like you chose your outfit, not just grabbed it.**
[ON SCREEN: compare: "Grabbed" (sweatpants + hoodie) | "Chosen" (jeans + soft knit + loafers)]

**Instead, try straight-leg jeans, a soft knit and loafers. Just as comfortable, and so much more polished.**
[ON SCREEN: recipe layout, two looks with "~ straight jeans ~ soft knit ~ loafers ~" written between them]

**And if you really love the comfort of leggings, choose a matte black pair, and wear them with a long oatmeal sweater and ankle boots.**
[ON SCREEN: cut-outs float in: matte black leggings, long oatmeal sweater, ankle boots; then the look walking]

**That one small change takes you from the gym to the café.**
[ON SCREEN: full-screen, a woman in that look sitting down at a café table, script "gym → café"]

### 3. A perfectly matching outfit, head to toe
**Number three. An outfit that matches perfectly, from head to toe.**
[ON SCREEN: number card, then a woman in a fully matching set with a matching bag and shoes]

**It can feel safe. The matching set, the bag that matches the shoes, everything chosen together as one look.**
[ON SCREEN: row building: matching set, matching bag, matching shoes]

**But the very first rule in Inès de la Fressange's style book is simple: mix styles, and never coordinate.**
[ON SCREEN: quote beside a clip: "mix styles, and *never* coordinate"]

**The secret is harmony, not matching. Pieces that belong together, without looking like a uniform.**
[ON SCREEN: script hint "harmony, not matching", over a slow pan of a mixed outfit]

**So elegant French women pair a classic blazer with jeans, or a beige trench coat over a simple navy sweater.**
[ON SCREEN: pair: blazer + jeans | beige trench over a navy sweater; cut-outs: blazer, jeans, trench coat]

**That small contrast is what makes an outfit look effortless instead of planned.**
[ON SCREEN: full-screen Paris walk in a trench over a sweater, script "effortless"]

### 4. Too much jewellery at once
**Number four. Too much jewellery at once.**
[ON SCREEN: number card, then a close-up of stacked necklaces, bracelets and rings]

**A beautiful necklace, a stack of bracelets, statement earrings, rings on every finger. Each one is lovely, but together, they compete.**
[ON SCREEN: labels pop on one over-accessorised look: necklace / bracelets / earrings / rings]

**Inès de la Fressange's second rule is just two words: reject bling.**
[ON SCREEN: white script "reject bling" on the caramel backdrop]

**And one French woman shared her own simple rule: never more than three pieces of jewellery at once, and a statement piece always on its own.**
[ON SCREEN: white script "3 pieces"; three jewellery cut-outs float in: small gold hoops, a simple watch, one ring]

**True elegance whispers. It doesn't shout. Choose small gold hoops, a simple watch and one ring, or one bold piece and nothing else.**
[ON SCREEN: pair: three small pieces | one bold earring alone]

**And when the outfit is simple, a white shirt and jeans, let one detail do the work, even just a red lip.**
[ON SCREEN: close-up of a woman in a white shirt applying red lipstick, then the full look with jeans]

### 5. Clothes that don't fit
**Number five. Clothes that don't fit.**
[ON SCREEN: number card, then trousers bunching over shoes and sleeves past the hands]

**This is the quiet secret behind French style. A simple piece that fits you perfectly looks more expensive than a costly piece that doesn't.**
[ON SCREEN: compare: "Doesn't fit" | "Fits perfectly" (the same simple blazer, tailored)]

**French women tend to look put together even in the simplest outfits, because their clothes are clean, pressed and properly tailored.**
[ON SCREEN: row: an iron gliding over a white shirt, clean loafers, a tailor pinning a hem]

**It's not about size. It's about fit.**
[ON SCREEN: row of three women of different sizes and ages, each in perfectly fitted trousers]

**A hem taken up, a waist taken in, sleeves shortened. Small changes, and the whole outfit looks made for you.**
[ON SCREEN: labels pop on one tailored look: hem / waist / sleeves]

**And you don't need to buy more. Before you shop, look at what's already in your closet. One small alteration can make a piece you forgot about feel new.**
[ON SCREEN: a woman pulling a navy blazer from her closet, trying it on at the mirror, script "already yours"]

### 6. Shiny, synthetic fabrics
**Number six. Shiny, synthetic fabrics.**
[ON SCREEN: number card, then a macro of a shiny polyester blouse]

**Polyester blouses, plastic-looking leather, knits that pill after a few wears.**
[ON SCREEN: row: shiny blouse, plastic-look jacket, a pilled knit close-up]

**No matter their budget, French women prefer natural fibres, cotton, linen, silk, wool, cashmere and leather, because they last, and they age beautifully.**
[ON SCREEN: macro clips one per fibre: cotton, linen, silk, wool, cashmere, leather; each labelled]

**We get it, that can sound expensive. But it doesn't have to be. Sometimes it means saving up a little for one good piece, and sometimes it simply means not buying what you don't need.**
[ON SCREEN: pair: a woman putting a shiny top back on the rail | the same woman holding one soft wool sweater]

**As Inès de la Fressange says, don't buy a lot, buy the good things.**
[ON SCREEN: quote card: "Don't buy a lot. *Buy the good things*." – Inès de la Fressange]

**A cream silk blouse, a camel wool coat, a navy cashmere sweater. A few timeless pieces will always look more elegant than a full closet.**
[ON SCREEN: cut-outs float in one per item: cream silk blouse, camel wool coat, navy cashmere sweater; script "fewer, better"]

**So before you buy, turn the label and read the fabric. It's a small habit, and it changes everything you bring home.**
[ON SCREEN: close-up of a hand turning a care label inside a sweater, "100% wool" visible]

### 7. The same style she wore twenty years ago
**Number seven. The same style she wore twenty years ago.**
[ON SCREEN: number card, then a vintage-feel photo dissolving into a modern street-style clip of the same age group]

**This one is for every woman who found what works, and kept it.**

[ON SCREEN: quote beside a clip of a woman in her 50s walking in Paris: "don't get *stuck* in a style"]

**That doesn't mean chasing every trend. It means keeping your classics, and letting your style grow with you. A new cut of jeans, a softer colour near your face, a modern pair of flats.**
[ON SCREEN: cut-outs float in one per item: straight jeans, a soft cream scarf, modern ballet flats]

**French women don't freeze their style in time, and that's one reason they seem to grow more elegant with age.**
[ON SCREEN: row of three women in their 40s, 60s and 70s, each beautifully dressed]

**Elegance has no age.**

  "saving up a little… not buying what you don't need", "never about money".
- Pieces they already own: "you may already own one", "already in your closet", the closing capsule.
- Women 40-70+ (29 comments): point 7, "at any age", women in their 60s and 70s on screen.
- Diversity (108 likes for natural hair): women of different ages, sizes, skin tones and backgrounds
  in the turn, points 5 and 7 and the stylist guide.
- Calm voice (255 comments): short sentences, pauses at every rule line.

**What viewers share:** short, numbered, checklist-able tips. One viewer's 5-rule summary got 210
likes. So every point ends in one clean, repeatable rule: "walk with ease", "gym to café",
"never coordinate", "3 pieces", "fit, not size", "buy the good things", "elegance has no age".

**Left out on purpose:**
- **Bright colours "in large swaths"** (PureWow): the channel's red line. Viewers pushed back with
  667- and 424-like replies. The script only adds named colours as positive swaps.
- **"Fake designer pieces"**: could shame budget viewers.
- **"Nude tights"** and **"push-up bra"**: risk the 40-70+ audience.
- **Logos**: already point 1 of the channel's #1 video.
- **Fur over 50** (Inès): divisive, not a tip the channel teaches.
- **Luxury brand names** (Chanel, Dior, YSL appear in the sources): the channel almost never names them.
