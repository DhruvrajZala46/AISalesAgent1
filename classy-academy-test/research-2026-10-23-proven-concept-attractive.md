## D. Why people click

**Read from titles and 50 thumbnails; this is interpretation.**

**The desire:**
- A near-universal wish to look and feel more attractive, combined with a low-cost promise: "without makeup", "without changing your looks", "zero dollars", "instantly", "simple".
- It is aspirational and positive, not fear-based. It is the mirror image of "look older".
- It is not tied to one age. The winners run from teen glow-ups to Monica Gupta's "Tips for Women" and The Feminine Universe.

**The title pattern:**
- [number] + things / ways / habits / tips + "(instantly) more attractive", or "look beautiful / better without makeup", or "glow up".
- Often a qualifier that lowers the cost: "without makeup", "zero dollars", "beyond looks".
- Questions are rare.

**Thumbnails** (30 winners, 10 recent hits and 10 failures inspected; sheets `th_winners.jpg`, `th_recent.jpg` in the session):
- About a third show a **before/after of the same woman**.
- About a third are **faceless collages of polished women** with one big keyword ("GLOW UP", "MORE ATTRACTIVE", "LOOK BEAUTIFUL WITHOUT MAKEUP"), the format closest to Classy Academy. Examples: AmbiJyo, KaniVerse, Dr Faye Bate, Whimsy Rosy, Wily.
- About a third are a **creator portrait** with text.
- **The failures use the same devices.** So the thumbnail style does not explain success; a broad, timeless promise does.

**Content:** not verified.
- Transcripts and comments of competitor videos could not be read this time. YouTube showed this machine "Sign in to confirm you're not a bot", and Google's CAPTCHA page answered the web reader. Neither was bypassed.
- The titles name the content: grooming, skin and hair, no-makeup beauty, posture and body language, small psychology habits (smile, eye contact), style.
