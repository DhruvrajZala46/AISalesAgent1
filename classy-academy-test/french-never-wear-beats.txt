Before you buy another pair of heels, stop. Which 7 things do elegant French women never wear, and why is number 1 in your closet?
Have you ever seen a woman in Paris wearing something so simple, a white shirt, jeans and flat shoes, and yet she looks more elegant than anyone around her?
There's no flashy outfit, no logos, nothing trying too hard. That kind of elegance isn't accidental. And the secret is often not what she wears. It's what she knows not to.
Of course, not every French woman dresses the same way. But the style the world calls French chic follows a few quiet, timeless rules, and you can borrow them anywhere, at any age, on any budget.
In today's video, we're revealing seven things elegant French women never wear, and what they wear instead. Some of these may surprise you, but once you see them, you'll never unsee them. Let's get started.
Number one. Heels you can't walk in.
We get it. Sky-high heels can feel glamorous, and they look beautiful sitting in the closet.
But Paris is a walking city. Cobblestone streets, metro stairs, long walks to the market. And nothing looks less elegant than struggling in your shoes.
Inès de la Fressange, the French model and style icon who wrote a best-selling book on Parisian style, favours flats over heels.
Elegance is a balance of comfort and structure. When your feet hurt, it shows in your face, your posture and your walk.
Instead of stilettos, elegant French women tend to reach for ballet flats, loafers, or a low kitten heel or block heel when they want a little height.
A pair of navy ballet flats or camel loafers is a timeless classic. It goes with almost everything, and you may already own one.
When you can walk with ease, you carry yourself with ease.
Number two. Gym clothes as an everyday outfit.
Leggings and sweatpants are wonderful for the gym, or a slow Sunday at home.
But in France, most women don't wear them out and about, except for a quick errand on the way home from the gym. French style means making a small effort, even for errands.
It's not about dressing up. It's about looking like you chose your outfit, not just grabbed it.
Instead, try straight-leg jeans, a soft knit and loafers. Just as comfortable, and so much more polished.
And if you really love the comfort of leggings, choose a matte black pair, and wear them with a long oatmeal sweater and ankle boots.
That one small change takes you from the gym to the café.
Number three. An outfit that matches perfectly, from head to toe.
It can feel safe. The matching set, the bag that matches the shoes, everything chosen together as one look.
But the very first rule in Inès de la Fressange's style book is simple: mix styles, and never coordinate.
The secret is harmony, not matching. Pieces that belong together, without looking like a uniform.
So elegant French women pair a classic blazer with jeans, or a beige trench coat over a simple navy sweater.
That small contrast is what makes an outfit look effortless instead of planned.
Number four. Too much jewellery at once.
A beautiful necklace, a stack of bracelets, statement earrings, rings on every finger. Each one is lovely, but together, they compete.
Inès de la Fressange's second rule is just two words: reject bling.
And one French woman shared her own simple rule: never more than three pieces of jewellery at once, and a statement piece always on its own.
True elegance whispers. It doesn't shout. Choose small gold hoops, a simple watch and one ring, or one bold piece and nothing else.
And when the outfit is simple, a white shirt and jeans, let one detail do the work, even just a red lip.
And here's a little secret. Elegant women know exactly which colours, silhouettes and pieces flatter them, so getting dressed feels easy.
If you'd like to discover yours, our personal stylist guide is linked in the description.
Number five. Clothes that don't fit.
This is the quiet secret behind French style. A simple piece that fits you perfectly looks more expensive than a costly piece that doesn't.
French women tend to look put together even in the simplest outfits, because their clothes are clean, pressed and properly tailored.
It's not about size. It's about fit.
A hem taken up, a waist taken in, sleeves shortened. Small changes, and the whole outfit looks made for you.
And you don't need to buy more. Before you shop, look at what's already in your closet. One small alteration can make a piece you forgot about feel new.
Number six. Shiny, synthetic fabrics.
Polyester blouses, plastic-looking leather, knits that pill after a few wears.
No matter their budget, French women prefer natural fibres, cotton, linen, silk, wool, cashmere and leather, because they last, and they age beautifully.
We get it, that can sound expensive. But it doesn't have to be. Sometimes it means saving up a little for one good piece, and sometimes it simply means not buying what you don't need.
As Inès de la Fressange says, don't buy a lot, buy the good things.
A cream silk blouse, a camel wool coat, a navy cashmere sweater. A few timeless pieces will always look more elegant than a full closet.
So before you buy, turn the label and read the fabric. It's a small habit, and it changes everything you bring home.
Number seven. The same style she wore twenty years ago.
This one is for every woman who found what works, and kept it.
Inès de la Fressange gives two pieces of advice to women over forty: never neglect yourself, and don't get stuck in a style you wore in your thirties, because it will age you instantly.
That doesn't mean chasing every trend. It means keeping your classics, and letting your style grow with you. A new cut of jeans, a softer colour near your face, a modern pair of flats.
French women don't freeze their style in time, and that's one reason they seem to grow more elegant with age.
Elegance has no age.
So, the seven things elegant French women never wear:
heels you can't walk in,
gym clothes as an outfit,
a perfectly matching look,
too much jewellery,
clothes that don't fit,
shiny synthetics,
and a style frozen in time.
And what they wear instead is a short list of timeless pieces you may already own: a blazer, a trench coat, a navy sweater, a little black dress and a perfect pair of jeans.
As Inès de la Fressange puts it, jeans are like salt. They go with everything.
Keep your wardrobe small, and clear out what you no longer wear.
French elegance was never about money or a big budget. It's about choosing well, and wearing it with ease.
And as Inès says, you can carry off anything with a smile.
