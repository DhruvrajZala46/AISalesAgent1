## 6. The three ideas, ranked

### 1. (the pick) "10 Easy, Elegant 2-Minute Hairstyles"

- **Title lint:** 36 characters, 100/100; fits a phone feed whole.
- **Evidence:** section 4. It is the only lane topic with many 10M videos from many independent channels, small channels
  included, and it is the channel's own second-best video.
- **Why now:** party season (late November to New Year), and the wave is live (Lainey Ostrom, Hanna Khimiak).
- **What is new against the 966k video:** the number and the time promise in the title, and every style shown start to
  finish.
- **The promise:** ten elegant hairstyles, each done in under two minutes, with no heat and no skill, for short, medium
  and long hair.
- **How to make it so it can travel:**
  - open on the three best finished styles in the first 10 seconds;
  - each style is one continuous clip, step by step, so it works with the sound off;
  - a small timer on screen proves "2 minutes"; step labels 1-2-3 in two or three words;
  - start with the bun and the French twist (the styles behind the 58.6M, 14.2M, 7.5M and 5.7M videos);
  - cover short, medium and long hair, and say which is which;
  - only pins, an elastic or a claw clip;
  - the channel's calm voice (the viewers' most-liked trait: 255 comments).
- **Thumbnail:** the finished bun from behind beside one step, on a light background, with "2 MINUTES" or no words.
- **Risk to settle first:** a hairstyle video is built from other creators' whole tutorials. A copyright claim or YouTube's
  reused-content rule would hit hardest exactly when the video takes off. Ask the creators' permission, or film the
  styles. The creator decides.

## 7. How a winning idea is found (the repeatable method)

1. **Rank by multiple, not by views.** A video's views over its own channel's median says the idea won, not the channel.
2. **Count channels, not videos.** One channel at 50x is that channel. The same topic and format winning on five or more
   channels is a shape.
3. **Check the ceiling for the goal.** For a 10M goal, count the topic's 10M videos and ask whether small channels have
   one. A topic whose best is 2M cannot give 10M.
4. **Check the channel's own audience.** The channel's multiple on that topic, all videos, not only the best.
5. **Check the timing.** The season, or a wave that is live in the last two months.
6. **Package first.** The title and thumbnail are written before the script; if they are not clearly better than the
   winners' ones, the idea is dropped.
7. **Deliver the promise in the first minute,** then keep the viewer: YouTube weighs clicks, watch time and satisfaction
   together.
8. **Write many, keep one.** Ten titles for every one made.

**What the 10M topics have that the niche topics lack (from this data):**

| Trait | Hairstyles | "Elegant ladies" topics |
|---|---|---|
| Who has the need | everyone with hair, every day | women who care about elegance |
| Works without the language | yes: watched with the sound off, worldwide | no: the value is in the words |
| A small win, fast | two minutes, no tools | a habit or a wardrobe change |
| A visible before and after | yes | sometimes |
| Searched again and again for years | yes | partly |

## 8. Honest limits

- No guarantee. Well under 1% of YouTube videos reach 1M; the 10M share is smaller still.
- The 10M hair videos are years old and their channels made hundreds of hair videos. One video rarely does it; a series
  can. If this one beats 4x the median, the next are short hair, over 50, claw clip, party styles.
- The channel's second hair video did 245k: a repeat without a new promise decays.
- Subscriber counts are today's. Publish dates are month-accurate at best.
- Search results are a sample; the topic counts depend on the searches run.
- The web sources on how YouTube recommends are third-party write-ups; YouTube's own post was not retrieved.
- The formula label of a title describes the title, not why the video worked.
