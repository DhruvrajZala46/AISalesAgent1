# The strongest proven long-form style idea (17 Nov 2026)

The user's ask: one long-form idea on style / outfits / fashion with the most proof behind it (title, hook, script
shape copied from videos that already reached multi-million views). Method: the `content-idea` skill plus a search of
all of YouTube by view count. **This file finds the idea. It is not the script.**

## How it was found

- **Niche, by multiple:** fresh lists from 52 niche channels today (2,978 long videos; 8 channels failed to load), each
  video scored as a multiple of its own channel's median (`collect.py`, `rank.py`, `shapes.py`).
- **All of YouTube, by raw views:** 41 public searches sorted by view count (yt-dlp flat listings, no login), 1,500+
  results; 157 women's-style long videos at 1M+ (`research-2026-11-17-study/search-top-1M.tsv`).
- **Our own channel (The Classy Elegance):** YouTube Data + Analytics API for our 23 long videos.
- **The winners' insides:** Gemini watched 5 winners and wrote out their hooks, rules and visuals
  (`research-2026-11-17-study/*.json`). The Mademoiselle Spends hook matches the one quoted in
  `research-2026-10-12-craft.md`; the other four are not cross-checked against captions (YouTube blocks captions here).

## The honest ceiling

- No long-form video that TEACHES women's style has passed 10M in these searches, except one title family (below).
- The biggest: 10.67M, 9.36M, 8.72M (Anna Bey, "10 Things Elegant Ladies NEVER Wear!", 2019), 8.06M, 7.32M. All are 3-8
  years old; views built up over years.
- Our channel: 387 subscribers; 23 long videos, **median 355 views**. One broke out: "7 Things Elegant French Women
  Never Wear" **25,206 views = 71x our median** (19,324 from YouTube's home/browse feed, 5,173 from suggested, +87
  subscribers, 3:40 average watch of 9:51).
- So nothing here supports "10M on the next video". The idea below is the one with the most independent proof.

## The one shared structure

A numbered list of **rules that sort outfits into right and wrong**, shown as the same woman or outfit side by side
with a cross and a tick. 6 of the 7 "rules" winners use that exact thumbnail; inside, Mademoiselle Spends shows 8 of
her 10 rules as a side-by-side comparison.

2. **French style, by raw views:** Alexa Chung Learns How To Dress The French Way 6.66M; Camille Rowe's French Style

3. **A live wave in our lane:** French videos in the last 60 uploads per channel: 9 at 5x+ from 6 channels (33.1x 303k

4. **Our own viewers:** our French video is our only long-form breakout (71x, above).

- **Why now:** the French wave is live this month, and our French video shows YouTube already offers our French
  content on the home feed.
- **Audience fit:** French = our best long video. The lens: rules as kind tips ("when I say rules, I don't mean you
  must", Audrey Coyne), passed down by mothers and grandmothers (the Mademoiselle Spends hook; a 6,800-like comment
  on it). No red lines in the title.
- **Caution:** we already published "8 Style Rules Every Woman Should Know" (451 views) and the French Never Wear
  video. Their points must not be repeated (list below).
- **The promise:** the small rules French women learn at home, so she can get dressed without second-guessing.

What the winners do inside (to copy):

- **Hook (Mademoiselle Spends, 1.87M, first 25 s):** "Everyone wonders how French women are always so well dressed...
  there are a lot of rules in French fashion that French women are taught from a very young age by their mothers and
  grandmothers. And I haven't seen any of those rules on the internet. So in this video, I'm going to share..."
  Shape: the question everyone has -> a hidden source (family) -> "not on the internet" -> the promise.
- **Softening (Audrey Coyne, 8.06M):** "when I say rules, I don't mean you must follow these things or you'll never be
  stylish... tips and tricks... helpful for hopefully the rest of your life."
- **Each rule:** one sentence she can repeat, with a number where possible ("up to 7 cm", "two colours", "two max"),
  one reason, one side-by-side right/wrong picture, one exception ("Exception: blue jeans", Justine Leconte).
- **Pace:** 10 rules in 5.5 minutes (Mademoiselle Spends), 10 in 5 (Justine), 14 in 4.5 (Bright Side): about 30 s a
  rule. Ours run about 60 s a rule.
- **Close:** ask for her family's rule / a part two (Audrey Coyne asked; Part 2 reached 1.29M).

Candidate rules (provisional: from 5 winners; the script stage studies 8-15 winners and keeps only rules with two
proofs, per `GOLD-STANDARD.md`):

| Candidate rule | Taught by |
|---|---|
| Neutrals first, one accent colour ("two colours, plus denim") | Justine #2 and #7; Audrey Coyne #5 |
| Tuck it in to show the waist | Mademoiselle Spends #2; Audrey Coyne #4 |
| Match your leathers (bag, belt, shoes), not your whole outfit | Mademoiselle Spends #6; Justine #6; Bright Side #10 |
| Pressed clothes, polished leather | Mademoiselle Spends #9; Bright Side #11 |
| Socks and tights follow the shoe | Justine #9; Bright Side #14 |
| Keep your own style; re-wear your best pieces | Justine #5; our channel's stance on trends |
| One finished detail, the rest natural (red lip or done hair) | Justine #8; Mademoiselle Spends (effortless) #4-5 |
| One structured piece over something soft | Mademoiselle Spends (effortless) #2 and #9 only: needs a second proof |
| Two statements at most | Mademoiselle Spends #10 (1,716 likes in comments); Justine #4: close to our "one star per outfit" |

Already taught by us, so not to repeat: reveal one not two, fit first, balance the shape, fabric that holds its shape,
underwear invisible, one star per outfit, shoes first, cared-for hands (Style Rules); unwalkable heels, gym clothes as
an outfit, head-to-toe matching, too much jewellery, poor fit, shiny synthetics, a 20-year-old style (French Never
Wear).

## Honest limits

- Search results are YouTube's top results per query, not a census; a bigger video under another wording can exist.
- Multiples use the latest 60 uploads per channel; publish dates are month-accurate; older videos had longer to grow.
- The formula label describes the title, not why a video worked. Titles and topics are proven here; the footage,
  voice and edit of our version are not.
- Like rates are likes / views today.
