## 4. POTENTIAL CONTENT DEPTH (point concepts only, not an outline)

There is enough clearly new ground for 7 points, with spare. Every rule must be checked against an etiquette authority at the script stage:
- Emily Post Institute (US);
- Debrett's (UK);
- a German Knigge source.

**Point concepts:**
- **The napkin's silent language:** when it goes on your lap; on your chair if you step away; beside the plate at the end.
- **Knife and fork as a signal:** "I'm resting" vs "I've finished". This is where the US differs from the UK and Germany: American "zig-zag" vs Continental, and different resting positions. It's the one point that speaks to each of the three markets.
- **Reading a set table:** bread plate on the left, glasses on the right, cutlery from the outside in.
- **Bread:** tear it, and butter each piece on the plate. Don't cut it or butter it whole.
- **Pace:** wait for the host or for everyone to be served, and match the table's pace.
- **Passing, not reaching:** salt and pepper travel together.
- **What never goes on the table:** your bag, your keys, your sunglasses. *The phone is excluded.*
- **The toast:** don't drink before it. A Germany-specific custom (eye contact when clinking) is still **unverified**; it needs a German source before use.
- **The holiday guest:** what to bring the host; offering help; the written thank-you afterwards. This also answers a request for "a video on elegant writing styles" (13 likes, [Sound More Elegant](https://youtu.be/RUDXBR8nyHw)).
- **Foods that trip people up:** soup, pasta, small bones or pits. Use only if a point needs a concrete example.
